# Groundwater observations

A water level in an open hole is an observation at a moment, not a water table.

## What to record

- Depth to water, and the time since the hole was advanced to that depth.
- Whether water was added during drilling.
- Whether the level was still rising when observed.
- Seepage: at what depth, and whether it was a defined horizon.
- "Not encountered" as an explicit statement, because absence of a note is not
  the same as absence of water.

## Why the caveat matters

In low-permeability clay a hole can take days to equilibrate, so a short-term
reading understates the standing level. In fractured rock the reading may reflect
one defect rather than a regional level. Neither is a design groundwater level
without a standpipe and repeat readings.
