# DCP testing

Dynamic cone penetrometer — 9 kg hammer, 510 mm drop, driving a 20 mm cone.
Blows per 100 mm increment. Quick, cheap, and shallow.

## What it is good for

Consistency and density profiling in the top couple of metres, checking that
founding material is as expected across a footprint, and identifying soft spots
and fill.

## What it is not

It is not a bearing capacity test. Correlations from blow counts to allowable
bearing pressure carry wide scatter, and they are correlations for the material
type assumed. Applying a sand correlation to clay produces a confident number
that is wrong.

## Reporting

Report the blow count profile. If an allowable bearing pressure is given,
state the correlation used, the assumed material and the depth range it applies
over, and flag refusal or possible obstruction rather than reading it as very
dense material.
