## Scope

Test-pit investigation and laboratory programme supporting a DA for residential
subdivision. CG assessed the soil and rock profile, salinity and sodicity,
preliminary AS2870 site classification, earthworks and farm-dam removal,
retaining wall and footing parameters, and pavement subgrade strength.

## Fieldwork

- Fieldwork carried out 29 August 2025 under CG direction, logged to AS1726 (2017)
- Walkover survey of the lot
- Ten test pits TP01 to TP10 excavated to termination/refusal depths of 1.5m to 2.5m using a 5 tonne tracked excavator with a 450mm bucket
- Dynamic Cone Penetrometer tests adjacent to the test pits
- Disturbed samples collected and sent to NATA accredited laboratories under Chain of Custody

## Subsurface profile as logged

- Topsoil: Silty CLAY, medium to high plasticity, dark grey, trace fine grained sand, gravel and organics, moisture >= plastic limit; base 0.2 - 0.5m
- Residual: CLAY / Silty CLAY, medium to high plasticity, red, orange, pale grey, some shale fragments, moisture >= plastic limit; base 1.0 - >1.5m
- Bedrock: SHALE, extremely weathered, fine to medium grained, dark grey, very low to low strength, Class V (TP01 and TP03 to TP10 only); base >1.5 - >2.5m

## Rock

Extremely weathered Bringelly Shale, very low to low strength, Class V,
encountered in nine of ten test pits (TP01, TP03 to TP10) at 1.0m to 1.8m below
existing surface. Higher strength shale may lie below test pit refusal level.
Foundation parameters given for Class V Bringelly Shale only.

## Groundwater

No free groundwater encountered during the investigation. Standing groundwater
and seepage may fluctuate with rainfall and temperature. No long-term
groundwater monitoring undertaken.

tell you.

## Recommendations

- Adopt Class P now because of abnormal moisture conditions; reassess differential movement after earthworks and before building pad construction, with slabs designed by engineering principles
- Decommission and desilt the two farm dams: divert upstream water permanently, dewater, desilt and dry the material, remove soft/compressible zones, test roll under geotechnical supervision, then backfill as engineered fill
- Re-use excavated residual soil and shale as engineered fill if clean, organic-free and under 75mm particle size, subject to environmental approval
- Compact engineered fill in 300mm loose layers to 95-99% SMDD within 2% of SOMC; extend each layer 1m beyond design geometry for edge compaction and trim back afterwards
- Backfill service trenches in 150mm loose layers with 50mm maximum particle size, compacted with trench roller, padfoot attachment or rammer
- Density test at one per layer per 2500 m2 or one per 500 m3 or 3 tests per lot, whichever gives most tests; trench backfill at one test per two layers per 40 linear metres; Level 1 supervision for fill supporting buildings, Level 2 for pavements
- Consider vibration controls where fill placement is within about 15m of nearby buildings, including a static rolling exclusion zone
- Cover exposed clay and shale faces with waterproof membrane; keep surcharge at least 2.5H from batter crests; retain excavations deeper than 1.5m
- Use 100 kPa allowable bearing pressure only for footings at least 0.8m deep in controlled fill or very stiff natural clay, not underlain by uncontrolled fill; avoid planters and gardens against the building
- Where piles are used, bored piers are the preferred option, socketed at least 0.3m into Class V Bringelly Shale with clean, rough (R2) sockets
- Design pavements on Grandeur Parade for CBR 3%; Road No. 1 is entirely in fill so its design CBR cannot be confirmed until fill is placed and tested; take further CBR samples after boxing out
- If services are excavated deeper than about 1.3m near TP10, design buried concrete/steel for AS 3600 Class A2 or higher exposure and undertake confirmatory salinity testing
- Undertake additional subsurface investigation and proof rolling where existing rural-residential dwellings may overlie undocumented fill

## Stated limitations

- Prepared for the exclusive use of Development Risk Management Pty Ltd for the described development
- Conclusions based on interpolation between test pits; conditions can vary between test locations
- Not to be used as part of a project specification without CG review and agreement
- Must be read in its entirety with the attached Information Sheets
- Assumes no buildings or structures near the central high-voltage transmission corridor
- Site classification recommendations assume AS2870-2011 performance expectations are acceptable and CSIRO BTF-18 maintenance is followed
- Design parameters may need reassessment because better shale may occur below test pit refusal depth

## Reviewer flags

The salinity (Table 6), aggressivity (Table 5) and ESP/CEC (Table 7) tables are
column-scrambled in the extraction; values were recovered by matching the header
order and are reported here in that interpretation - worth a visual check
against the PDF. Minor internal inconsistencies: Table 1 lists FMC quantity as 8
while Section 8.1 and Table 3 report nine samples; Section 10.8 cites 'Table 6'
for aggressivity results that appear in Table 5; the sentence in Section 10.6.1
about shrink-swell movements is left incomplete in the source. Revision history:
Rev 0 12.09.2025, Rev 1 16.09.2025, Rev 2 and Rev 3 both 18.09.2025. Author
Gopinath Naga, reviewer Raj Singh. Moderately saline soils are confined to TP10
at about 1.3m to 2.5m depth.
