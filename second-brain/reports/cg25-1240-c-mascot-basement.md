## Scope

Investigation for a mixed-use hotel with three basements next to an airport
tunnel asset. Three boreholes with SPTs, rock coring and standpipe installation,
laboratory testing, and advice on excavation, groundwater control, shoring
parameters, foundations, basement slabs, seismic subsoil class and aggressivity.

## Fieldwork

- BYDA search before drilling; specialist on-site services search
- Field investigation 25 to 27 February 2026 and 2 March 2026, logged to AS1726:2017
- Walkover survey of landform, surface conditions and adjacent structures
- Three boreholes BH01 to BH03 drilled with a track mounted rig to 30.32m, 12.5m and 30.5m respectively
- SPTs at regular intervals; NMLC rock coring after auger refusal at 21.4m (BH01) and 21.3m (BH03); core photographed
- Standpipes installed in all three boreholes to 12m depth, slotted screen 6m to 12m, sand/gravel filter to 5m, 1m bentonite seal 4m to 5m, site-derived backfill 0.5m to 4m, concrete seal in top 0.5m; wells developed with a submersible pump
- Groundwater observed during and shortly after drilling; long-term monitoring ongoing under a hydrogeologist
- Disturbed soil samples dispatched to NATA accredited laboratories under Chain of Custody

## Subsurface profile as logged

- Concrete slab; base 0.12m
- Unit 1 'Possibly' Fill: Silty SAND, fine grained, pale grey, low plasticity fines, moist, medium dense; base 0.3 - 0.4m
- Unit 2/3a Alluvium: SAND, fine grained, pale grey becoming pale orange, brown, trace low plasticity fines, moist to wet, loose to medium dense; base 6.2m
- Unit 3b Alluvium: SAND, fine grained, dark grey becoming grey, trace low plasticity fines, wet, medium dense to dense; base 10 - 16.2m
- Unit 3c Alluvium: Silty CLAY, high plasticity, dark grey, trace fine grained sand, moisture > plastic limit, very soft (BH01 and BH02 only); base >12.5 - 18.0m
- Unit 4 Residual: Silty CLAY, high plasticity, trace fine grained sand, grey, moisture > plastic limit, stiff to hard; base 20.5 - 21.4m
- Unit 5a Bedrock: SHALE, extremely weathered, fine grained, grey, very low strength with high plasticity clay bands, Class V (BH01 and BH03 only); base 24.0m
- Unit 5b Bedrock: SHALE, highly weathered, fine to medium grained, grey, low strength, Class IV (BH01 and BH03 only); base 26.7 - >30.32m
- Unit 5c Bedrock: SHALE, highly to moderately weathered, fine to medium grained, grey, low to medium strength, Class III (BH03 only); base >30.5m

## Rock

Shale of the Wianamatta sequence cored in BH01 and BH03 below about 21.3m to
21.4m, grading from extremely weathered Class V through highly weathered Class
IV to highly/moderately weathered Class III. Is50 values 0.075 to 1.7MPa (very
low to high strength), estimated UCS 1.125 to 25.5MPa. One laboratory UCS test
on BH01 core from 24.50-24.70m returned 4.3MPa.

## Groundwater

Encountered at about 3.0m to 3.2m below existing ground level during the field
investigation. Circulating water during diamond drilling in BH01 and BH03
prevented reliable measurement in those holes. Standpipes installed and longer-
term monitoring is in progress under a hydrogeologist. Permanent groundwater
lowering is generally not permitted, so the basement should be tanked; inflow
into a 12m deep excavation should be expected and temporary dewatering will
likely be required.

## Recommendations

- Undertake dilapidation surveys of underground services and nearby structures before construction
- Prepare a vibration monitoring plan with trigger levels for demolition, excavation and piling, using the tabulated PPV limits confirmed by the structural engineer
- Design the basement as a tanked structure; permanent groundwater lowering is generally not permitted
- Provide an effective perimeter groundwater cut-off - diaphragm walls, secant piled walls, cutter soil mix walls or equivalent embedded walls; steel sheet piles are not preferred and soldier piles with shotcrete/lagging will not cut off groundwater in these sands
- Do not use ground anchors because of adjacent infrastructure, services, road reserves and the nearby airport tunnel; support progressively with internal bracing, strutting, raking props or top-down construction
- Embed the retained wall below final excavation level for toe stability and cut-off, allowing for over-excavation and local deeper pits
- Implement groundwater and deformation monitoring with trigger levels and contingency measures
- Do not assume a conventional raft is suitable; very soft high plasticity silty clay below the excavation level makes piled foundations or a piled raft more likely appropriate, with CFA piles founded in verified shale
- Control CFA pile installation to avoid soil decompression and ground disturbance; apply AS2159 strength reduction factors and undertake settlement analyses
- Place a 200mm DGB20 working platform on the inspected subgrade before basement slab and waterproofing works; ensure tanking continuity at joints, columns, piles and penetrations
- Adopt Subsoil Class De for seismic design per AS1170.4-2024
- Have the shoring and piling designs reviewed against this report, and have a geotechnical engineer inspect founding levels before concrete placement
