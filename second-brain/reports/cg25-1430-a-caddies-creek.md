## Scope

Core Geotech investigated the Stage 2 reserve footprint to characterise ground
conditions for four soccer pitches, car parks and access roads, a Centre of
Excellence building, sports field lighting poles, and vehicle and pedestrian
bridges over Caddies Creek and an unnamed tributary. Work covered a walkover, 41
boreholes with in-situ testing, laboratory testing, and engineering advice on
earthworks, pavement design, foundations, groundwater and soil aggressivity.
Advice is preliminary and intended to support concept and early design
development.

## Fieldwork

- Site walkover survey and BYDA search plus contractor on-site services search
- 41 boreholes (BH01 to BH41) drilled with ute and track mounted rigs using 110 mm solid flight auger, terminated/refused at 2 m to 6.45 m
- 5 of those holes (BH32, BH33, BH39, BH40, BH41) extended by NMLC rock coring after auger refusal, to 8.12 m to 8.36 m
- DCP, pocket penetrometer and SPT testing in selected boreholes
- Groundwater observations during and shortly after drilling; holes backfilled by hand with spoil
- Disturbed sampling for NATA-accredited geotechnical and chemical laboratory testing
- Fieldwork undertaken on 28 October 2025, 2 to 6 November 2025, 13 November 2025, and 3, 10 and 11 December 2025

## Rock

Ashfield Shale (Wianamatta Group) was reached in most boreholes at variable
depth reflecting the floodplain setting. It is shallowest under the Centre of
Excellence building, where extremely weathered shale was met at about 1.6-1.7 m
in BH19 and BH23, and shallow in BH15 under the lighting area. Under the
pavements, extremely weathered Class V shale was logged from as shallow as about
1.1-2.0 m. At the bridge locations the cored profile runs from extremely
weathered Class V shale (base 4.5-6.0 m) through highly weathered Class IV shale
(base 5.3-6.85 m) into slightly weathered Class III/IV and Class III
siltstone/laminite of medium to high strength below about 6.0-8.2 m. Point load
Is(50) results on core ranged from 0.044 to 0.83 MPa diametral and 0.082 to 1.6
MPa axial, classified very low to high strength. No RLs are recorded on the
logs, so rockhead is expressed in depth only.

## Groundwater

Section 8.4 states groundwater was not encountered in BH12, BH18, BH20 to BH22,
BH24 to BH26, BH30, BH31, BH33, BH35 and BH36 to depths of 1.8 m to 5 m at the
time of investigation, and notes levels may not have stabilised over the short
observation window. Circulating water used for the cored holes (BH32, BH33,
BH39, BH40, BH41) prevented any water level measurement. Later sections report
groundwater as encountered: about 1.8 m in BH35 and about 2 m in BH36 (Carpark
3), about 5 m in BH30 and about 2 m in BH31 (Field 4), and about 3 m in BH22, 5
m in BH26 and 5 m in BH30 (lighting). No standpipes or longer-term monitoring
were installed. See notes_for_reviewer - Section 8.4 and Sections
10.2.4/10.3.1/10.5 contradict each other for BH22, BH26, BH30, BH31, BH35 and
BH36.

## Recommendations

- Strip topsoil across turf footprints and stockpile separately for reuse where the environmental consultant approves
- Treat all fill as uncontrolled unless placement and compaction records are produced
- Remove fill and unsuitable, disturbed or compressible material to expose competent natural subgrade, then inspect and proof roll before any filling
- Undercut and replace soft or pumping zones with engineered fill or imported granular material; use a temporary working platform where wet conditions persist
- Place all filling as engineered fill under Level 2 geotechnical control to AS 3798-2007 for fields and pavements, and Level 1 control for the Centre of Excellence building pad
- Set geotechnical hold points at completion of stripping, after soft spot treatment, before fill placement, and at acceptance of final platform level
- Stage earthworks to limit trafficking of exposed clay subgrades and re-proof roll after rain
- Expect localised very soft alluvium in Fields 2 and 3 near BH12 and allow extra undercut and replacement there
- For Field 4, plan deep fill removal (up to about 2.5 m) allowing for wet excavation and groundwater inflow, particularly near BH31, with sump pumping and staged excavation
- Adopt a preliminary design subgrade CBR of 3% for Carparks 1-3 and the access road, and confirm with further CBR testing after trimming and proof rolling
- Provide a capping layer beneath pavements and maintain positive drainage during construction and in the final works
- Use DGB20 base to TfNSW QA Specification 3051, compacted in layers not exceeding 200 mm loose
- Provide perimeter subsoil drains with invert at least 200 mm below pavement formation, connected to the site stormwater system
- Reassess AS 2870 differential movement after earthworks and immediately before building pad construction; design slabs using engineering principles allowing for reactivity
- For the Centre of Excellence building, either pad/strip footings at 150 kPa embedded at least 0.8 m, or shallow bored piers socketed at least 0.6 m into competent extremely weathered shale
- Pour high level footings promptly after excavation and cleaning; use 75 mm blinding concrete if the pour is delayed and prevent ponding in footing bases
- Use deep footings for all sports field light poles founded in very stiff residual clay or socketed into shale, except potentially BH15 where a shallow pad on shale may suit
- Ignore shaft resistance in the upper 1.8 m of reactive clay for piled footings and use 50% of compression shaft adhesion as an uplift screening value
- Use deep foundations for the vehicle and pedestrian bridges, with rock-socketed bored piers taken 1 to 2 m into competent Class III rock and founded below the design scour level
- Allow for temporary casing or drilling fluids through soft alluvium and below groundwater, and consider downdrag where new fill is placed
- Inspect all footing excavations, pile bores and rock sockets before reinforcement and concreting to confirm founding material, socket depth, cleanliness and groundwater inflow
- Use tremie placement where groundwater inflow prevents a dry founding surface
- Have CG review civil, structural, architectural and landscape drawings and the contractor's earthworks methodology before construction
- Re-engage CG if conditions differ materially from those described or if the design, layout or site levels change

## Stated limitations

- Prepared exclusively for Environmental Group Australia Pty Ltd, for the described development and purpose only, under CG's Terms of Engagement and proposal QU25-0109 Rev 0 dated 10 January 2025
- Conclusions rely on interpolation between boreholes; conditions can vary between test locations in ways the investigation cannot define
- Depths and layer thicknesses in Tables 2 to 6 are indicative only and may not represent the whole site
- No detailed contour survey was available at the time of reporting; site levels are based on walkover observations and aerial imagery
- Test locations were measured from the supplied plan to an accuracy of about +/- 5 m
- Groundwater levels may not have stabilised over the short observation period; no longer-term monitoring was undertaken and levels can fluctuate with rainfall, temperature and other factors
- Circulating water in the cored holes prevented groundwater measurement
- Foundation and pavement parameters are preliminary, to be confirmed by the structural and pavement designers and by construction-stage inspection
- AS 2870 provisions do not strictly apply to the Centre of Excellence building because of its size; the classification given is preliminary
- The report should not be used as part of a project specification without CG review and agreement, and must be kept whole and read with the attached Information Sheets
- Information supplied by the client and their agents was relied on and not independently verified

## Reviewer flags

Text extraction of the narrative and of Tables 1 to 16 is clean and legible.
Three things to check. First, a direct internal contradiction on groundwater:
Section 8.4 lists BH22, BH26, BH30, BH31, BH35 and BH36 among the holes where
groundwater was NOT encountered, but Sections 10.2.4, 10.3.1 and 10.5 report
groundwater encountered in exactly those holes at about 3 m (BH22), 5 m (BH26),
5 m (BH30), 2 m (BH31), 1.8 m (BH35) and 2 m (BH36). I have recorded both
statements rather than picking one. Second, the borehole logs in Appendix C have
a blank R.L. SURFACE field for all 41 holes, so no reduced levels exist anywhere
in this report and rockhead can only be quoted as depth below ground. The Water
column in the log graphics also extracted blank for the holes where water is
reported in the text, so the log-side evidence could not be cross-checked.
Third, Table 10 (salinity/exposure classification) has rotated column headers
that extracted as a vertical jumble above the data; the data rows themselves are
readable and in the order EC (uS/cm), multiplication factor, ECe, salinity
rating, pH, exposure classification, but a reviewer should confirm against the
source PDF before relying on individual rows. Table 11 (aggressivity) has the
same rotated-header issue. Also note Table 13 lists 18 PLSI rows while Table 1
schedules 20 PLSI tests. Minor typos in the source: 'laminate' is used for
'laminite' in Section 10.6, and Section 10.4.1 reads 'are likely produce'.
