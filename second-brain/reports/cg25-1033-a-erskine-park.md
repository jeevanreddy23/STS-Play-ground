## Scope

Core Geotech was engaged under proposal QU25-0381 Rev 0 (1 August 2025) to carry
out a desktop review, drill and log a borehole programme, schedule laboratory
testing, and build a ground model for the wastewater treatment plant upgrade.
Deliverables asked for were subsurface descriptions, suitable foundation options
with design parameters, pavement design parameters, and an assessment of soil
aggressivity. A waste classification of excavated material was commissioned
separately from Development Risk Management Pty Ltd.

## Fieldwork

- Site walkover survey to assess landform and surface conditions
- Underground services search by a specialist contractor prior to drilling
- 5 boreholes (BH01 to BH05) drilled with a track-mounted rig using 120 mm solid flight auger; termination depths 3.0 m to 8.5 m
- BH01 to BH04 each terminated at 3.0 m; BH05 terminated at 8.5 m
- Fieldwork on 25 September 2025 (BH01-BH04, drilled by Geotrace Australia Pty Ltd) and 3 October 2025 (BH05, drilled by Pace Drilling Services Pty Ltd)
- SPTs at regular intervals in all boreholes (BH01 N=8/32/29; BH02 N=9 and 6,12,14/80 mm double bounce; BH03 N=8/30; BH04 N=14/41; BH05 N=11/27/20/40 and 5,14/60 mm double bounce at 7.0 m)
- Disturbed sampling with Chain of Custody to NATA-accredited laboratories; boreholes backfilled with quick-set concrete
- Laboratory schedule: 1 CBR (AS 1289.6.1.1), 2 aggressivity soil suites (APHA), 11 field moisture contents (AS 1289.2.1.1), by Eurofins Environment Testing Australia and Benchmark Geotechnical
- Logging and descriptions in general accordance with AS 1726 (2017)

## Subsurface profile as logged

- FILL - sandy GRAVEL / silty sandy CLAY / silty CLAY, medium to high plasticity, fine to medium grained sand and gravel, grey and brown, moisture condition at or below plastic limit; base of layer 0.3 m to 4.0 m. Thin (about 0.3-0.6 m) across BH01-BH04 on the eastern side; about 4 m thick at BH05 in the proposed tank footprint. Treated as uncontrolled fill (no compaction records).
- RESIDUAL CLAY - medium to high plasticity, brown, pale grey, orange and yellow, trace ironstone gravel and shale fragments, moisture condition at or below plastic limit; base of layer stated as >3.0 m to 7.2 m. Logged stiff near surface grading very stiff to hard with depth.
- BEDROCK - SILTSTONE, extremely weathered, fine grained, pale grey to brown, trace high plasticity fines, very low to low strength; encountered in BH05 only, from about 7.0 m to the 8.5 m hole termination (Table 2 gives base of layer as >8.5 m).

## Rock

Extremely weathered siltstone reached only in BH05, at approximately 7.0 m
depth, and proved to the 8.5 m termination. Described as fine grained, pale grey
to brown, very low to low strength, with trace high plasticity clay; SPT in the
unit recorded 5, 14 for 60 mm with double bounce. BH01-BH04 were terminated at
3.0 m in residual clay without reaching rock. No RLs are given (surface RL and
datum fields on all logs are blank). Table 6 design parameters label the same
unit 'Bringelly Shale, Class V'.

## Groundwater

Not encountered. All five borehole logs (25 September and 3 October 2025) are
annotated 'GROUNDWATER NOT ENCOUNTERED'. The report cautions that standing water
and seepage can fluctuate with rainfall, temperature and other factors, and
states no long-term groundwater monitoring was carried out.

## Recommendations

- Treat all fill on site as uncontrolled under AS 3798-2007 because no placement or compaction records exist.
- Do not rely on shallow footings for the tank - roughly 4 m of uncontrolled fill at BH05 plus adjacent sensitive assets (leachate plant, retaining walls) make settlement performance unacceptable.
- Use piles to carry the tank loads down to competent ground; bored piles, CFA and screw piles are all listed as viable options.
- Socket piles in the BH05 area at least 2 m into the low to very low strength siltstone, going deeper where ultimate or serviceability checks demand it.
- Design piles to AS 2159, using shaft friction, end bearing and modulus values for Sydney Basin siltstone from Pells et al. (2019) as tabulated, and allow for potential downdrag through the fill.
- Check serviceability for both individual and group piles, including rotations, allowing for group interaction and stress bulb overlap.
- Case through the uncontrolled fill, keep the socket stable, roughen socket walls, and tremie concrete or grout under fluid to avoid segregation; socket base and walls must be clean and sound before pouring.
- Have a geotechnical consultant inspect and approve bored pile bearing and socket depths before concreting; allow for temporary steel casing if water is met in the clay during drilling.
- For the access road, base preliminary design on the prepared formation rather than in-situ conditions; proof roll after boxing, rework to SOMC +/- 2%, compact to at least 100% Standard, maintain drainage and carry out density and moisture QA.
- Obtain at least two confirmatory soaked CBR tests on the prepared formation at the design surcharge to verify the characteristic design value.
- Where weak or highly plastic material is exposed, undercut and replace with select material or stabilise in situ (granular capping, lime or lime-cement), with extent confirmed by the geotechnical consultant after boxing.
- Adopt concrete and steel appropriate to a 'mild' AS 2159-2009 exposure classification for both concrete and steel piles.
- Refer tank foundation design to AS 3798, AS 2159, AS 3600 and the relevant tank code rather than AS 2870.
- Have CG review the architectural, civil and structural drawings to confirm the report's recommendations have been carried into the design.
- Clear all footings of debris and softened material, have them designed by a structural engineer, and have them inspected and approved before concrete is poured.
- Seek further advice if ground conditions differ materially from those described, or if the project scope changes significantly.
- Have an experienced person inspect the stability of any excavation deeper than 1.0 m.
- (From the DRM waste report) Dispose of excess soil to a licensed landfill per the assigned classifications, keep VENM residual clay separate from fill so the classification is not voided, stop work and consult the environmental consultant if contamination indicators appear, and retain disposal records for the NSW EPA.

## Stated limitations

- Scope of services was set by the proposal and may have been constrained by time, budget, access and site constraints.
- Report is for the exclusive use of H & H Consulting Engineers and only for the development and purpose described; it may not hold enough information for any other purpose or site.
- Findings are accurate at the date of issue for the conditions then present; subsurface conditions can change over time.
- Conclusions rest on interpolation between borehole locations, and conditions between test points can vary in ways no investigation can fully reveal.
- Depths and layer thicknesses in the summary table apply at the investigation points and may not represent the whole site.
- CG relied on information supplied by the client and their agents and did not verify its accuracy or completeness.
- Groundwater levels on logs are point-in-time records; actual levels may differ over longer periods and can shift seasonally or with construction.
- No long-term groundwater monitoring was undertaken.
- Soil and rock descriptions are based on visual and tactile assessment to AS 1726-2017, except where field or laboratory testing was done.
- The report is advice and opinion, not construction instructions, and should not be used in a specification without CG's review and agreement.
- The report must be kept whole and read with the attached information sheets and explanatory notes; individual pages or sections should not be separated.
- CG accepts no responsibility for third-party interpretations of the report or test data that the report does not itself support.
- Architectural and civil drawings were not available at the time of reporting; the development description came from discussions with the client.
- AS 2870-2011 does not cover the proposed tank, so no site classification applies to it.

## Reviewer flags

Text extraction was clean for the narrative and mostly readable for the tables,
but several internal inconsistencies in the source document are worth flagging.
(1) Section 1 describes the work as being for a 'proposed residential
development', which contradicts the industrial wastewater treatment plant
project - likely template carry-over. (2) Section 8.1 states nine (9) FMC
samples were tested, but Table 3 lists 11 results and Table 1 schedules 11. (3)
Address conflict: the CG report gives 85 Quarry Road (and 85-87 Quarry Road)
Erskine Park, while the appended DRM waste classification report (Ref. DRM
P25.1033-R01, 27 October 2025, though internally dated 20 October 2025 on one
figure) gives 4 Quarry Road, Erskine Park, Lot 4 DP1094504 - reviewer should
confirm which is correct. (4) Unit naming conflict: Table 2 and the BH05 log
call the bedrock SILTSTONE, but the Table 6 design parameter row is labelled
'Bringelly Shale, Class V'; presumably the same material, but the report never
states this explicitly. (5) Section 10.2 is headed 'Preliminary AS2870-2011 Site
Classification' yet gives no site class at all - it only says the tank is
outside AS 2870's scope. No classification (M, H1 etc.) is stated anywhere for
the site. (6) All borehole logs have blank R.L. SURFACE and DATUM fields, so no
reduced levels are available anywhere in the report; all depths are metres below
ground. (7) Table 2 depth ranges are awkwardly written ('>3.0 - 7.2' for
residual, '>8.5' for bedrock) and do not obviously reconcile with the BH05 log,
which shows siltstone from about 7.0 m to termination at 8.5 m; the bedrock base
was never proved. (8) Table 5 (aggressivity) had vertically rotated column
headers that interleaved in extraction; I mapped values by order and cross-
checked that conductivity and resistivity are reciprocal (13 uS/cm vs 800 Ohm.m;
92 uS/cm vs 110 Ohm.m), which confirms the mapping - but note BH05 chloride (92
ppm) and BH05 conductivity (92 uS/cm) share the same figure, which could
conceivably be a transcription error in the original and is worth checking
against the Eurofins certificate. (9) Table 6 gives no allowable bearing
pressure for residual clay (shown as '-'). (10) The DRM report footnote records
that 8 boreholes were originally proposed but 3 could not be drilled because of
access restrictions - the CG report does not mention this reduction in coverage.
(11) Minor typos in the source: 'trace irstone gravel' (ironstone), 'shale
fragmnets', 'extrremely weathered', 'non-putrecible'. (12) Only one CBR test
supports the pavement advice, and the report itself calls the value preliminary.
(13) The exact tank footprint location relative to BH05 is not dimensioned in
the extracted text; the site plan (Drawing CG25/1033-1) is an image and could
not be read.
