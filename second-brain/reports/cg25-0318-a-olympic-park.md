## Scope

Core Geotech investigated a small parcel on Australia Avenue where a pump well
is planned, requiring roughly 3 m of excavation. Work covered a desktop review,
site walkover, two machine boreholes with SPT, laboratory testing, and
development of a ground model with excavation, retention and foundation
parameters. A subconsultant (DRM) prepared a preliminary waste classification
for the spoil.

## Fieldwork

- Field investigation on 15 April 2025 following a BYDA search and on-site services search by a specialist contractor
- 2 machine boreholes (BH01, BH02) by Stratacore Drilling Pty Ltd using a Massenza M12 track-mounted rig with 120 mm solid flight auger; text states depths of 7.6 to 8 m, both logs record termination at 8 m
- SPT at regular intervals in both boreholes; 5 SPTs logged in BH01 and 5 in BH02
- Disturbed/SPT tube samples collected and sent to NATA-accredited laboratories (ALS, Benchmark Geotechnical) under Chain of Custody
- Boreholes backfilled by hand with excavated spoil
- 8 field moisture content tests
- 2 aggressivity suite tests (BH01 2.5–2.95 m, BH02 4.0–4.45 m)
- Site walkover survey; test locations measured from site features to +/- 2 m

## Subsurface profile as logged

- Unit 1 Topsoil – Silty Sandy CLAY, low to medium plasticity, dark grey, fine to medium grained sand, some grass rootlets, moisture > plastic limit; base 0.2 m
- Unit 2 Fill – Gravelly Sandy CLAY/CLAY, low to high plasticity, grey/dark grey/brown, fine to medium grained sand, brick pieces, fine to medium gravel, moisture > plastic limit; base 2.6–2.8 m (assessed as uncontrolled fill)
- Unit 3a Alluvial – CLAY, high plasticity, dark grey, moisture > plastic limit, wet, very soft; base 4.0 m
- Unit 3b Alluvial – CLAY, high plasticity, grey, moisture >= plastic limit, stiff to very stiff; base 5.5–6.0 m
- Unit 4 Residual – CLAY, medium to high plasticity, brown/grey, fine gravel with ironstone and trace fine grained sand, moisture >= plastic limit; base 7.0 to >8.0 m
- Unit 5 Bedrock – SHALE, extremely weathered, fine grained, dark grey/brown, very low to low strength, Class V; encountered in BH02 only
- Strong petroleum odour noted in the BH01 SPT sample from 2.5–2.95 m; slight petroleum odour in the BH02 sample from 2.5–2.95 m

## Rock

Extremely weathered SHALE, Class V, fine grained, dark grey/brown, very low to
low strength, encountered in BH02 only. Table 1 gives the base of the bedrock
unit as >8.0 m; the BH02 log shows shale from roughly 7 m to termination at 8 m
and the waste-classification summary describes weathered shale from 7 m to 8 m.
Final SPT in BH02 recorded 8 blows then +10 double bounce. No rockhead RL is
given as the logs carry no surface RL or datum. BH01 did not reach rock
(residual clay tending to extremely weathered shale at the base of the hole).

## Groundwater

Groundwater was encountered during the investigation on 15 April 2025 at 2.8 m
in BH01 and 4.0 m in BH02, within low permeability alluvial clay. Levels may not
have stabilised over the short observation period and are expected to fluctuate
with rainfall and temperature; no longer-term monitoring was undertaken and no
RLs/AHD levels are reported.

## Recommendations

- Commission dilapidation surveys of nearby structures, adjoining public infrastructure (footpath, concrete wall) and above/underground services before excavation starts, and have owners confirm the records
- Excavation through the gravelly sandy clay fill and soft alluvial clay can be achieved with conventional earthmoving plant; refer to the current Safe Work Australia Excavation Work Code of Practice
- Keep unsupported excavations outside the zone of influence of adjacent structures (30 degree line from adjacent foundation level)
- Battering to the required depth is judged impractical given uncontrolled fill, services and sign-structure foundations; use a temporary support system such as shoring boxes with steel struts or vertishores with boards, advanced in step with excavation
- Design shoring using the stated unit weights and earth pressure coefficients; increase coefficients or treat inclined backfill as a surcharge where the backfill surface is not horizontal
- Remove shoring in reverse order of installation, with no worker outside the protection of the support system and no removal until the trench is ready for backfill
- Have a competent person repeatedly check excavation faces and shoring for fretting, slipping, slumping or swelling, since soil condition can change quickly
- Found the structure below the very soft alluvial clay (roughly 3 to 4 m depth), or excavate that layer and replace with compacted well-draining granular coarse fill, then design for 100 kPa
- Where higher capacity is needed, use piles founded in very stiff alluvial or residual clay (250 kPa end bearing, 20 kPa shaft adhesion below 1.0 m from excavation level)
- Manage groundwater inflow by conventional sump and pump or gravity drainage; monitor seepage during excavation and test groundwater quality before discharging to stormwater
- Allow for uplift/buoyancy on the pump well from fluctuating groundwater in detailed design; a one-way or non-return valve may be appropriate subject to confirmation
- Select concrete and steel appropriate to the assessed aggressivity (non-aggressive concrete, moderate steel, soil type B)
- Have CG review architectural, civil and structural drawings to confirm the report recommendations are carried into the design
- Clear all footings of debris and softened material and have them inspected and approved by a structural or geotechnical consultant before pouring concrete
- Have temporary and permanent support designs approved by an experienced consultant, and inspect any excavation deeper than 1.0 m
- Seek further advice if conditions differ significantly from those described
- Waste (per DRM): assume the fill contains friable asbestos and use Class 1 licensed asbestos removal contractors with air monitoring during disturbance, stockpiling and load out; re-classify stockpiled soil before offsite disposal including for acid sulphate soils; assess alluvial clays for acid sulphate soils and contamination before any VENM classification; keep truck registers and disposal receipts and use lawfully licensed facilities

## Reviewer flags

Body text and recommendations extracted cleanly; several internal
inconsistencies and extraction issues should be checked against the source PDF.
(1) Section 5 states boreholes were drilled 'to a depth ranging from 7.6 to 8m'
but both logs record termination at 8 m. (2) Table 1 lists the base of the
bedrock unit as >8.0 m for BH02, while the BH02 log shows extremely weathered
shale from roughly 7 m and the DRM summary states alluvium to 7 m then weathered
shale to 8 m; DRM also describes everything below the fill in both holes as
'alluvium' whereas the CG logs identify residual clay from about 5.5 m. (3) The
BH02 4.0-4.45 m sample is reported with FMC 24.1% in Table 2 but 18.8% in the
aggressivity table (Table 3). (4) Neither borehole log records a surface RL or
datum, so no AHD levels exist anywhere in the report - all depths are below
existing surface. (5) Table 3 (aggressivity) has heavily collapsed column
headers in the extracted text; the values were assigned following the printed
header order (EC uS/cm, resistivity ohm.cm, chloride ppm, sulphate ppm) and
should be verified against the Appendix E certificates. (6) Section 7.2 relies
on the Sydney 1:100,000 Sheet 9130 (1983) but reference item 8 cites the Penrith
1:100,000 Sheet 9030 (1991). (7) DRM records the hydrocarbon odour in BH02 at
2.5 m, whereas the CG logs record a strong petroleum odour in BH01 2.5-2.95 m
and only a slight odour in BH02 at the same depth. (8) DRM notes no visual ACM
during drilling, yet laboratory analysis found friable asbestos in BH1 and
bonded asbestos in BH2 - not contradictory, but worth flagging for the
excavation methodology. (9) The 100/250/20 kPa foundation values are stated
without any supporting calculation or derivation from the SPT data. (10) No RL,
invert level or plan dimensions for the pump well are given; architectural
drawings were not available at the time of reporting.
