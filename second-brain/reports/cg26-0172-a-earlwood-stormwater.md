## Scope

Core Geotech characterised the ground along the nominated rockface corridor for
a stormwater drainage upgrade and assessed slope instability hazards and risk
qualitatively to AGS (2007). Two concept options were considered: an open
channel of about 400 mm by 500 mm internal dimensions, or a nominally DN600
pipeline partly fixed above ground to the rockface with brackets and rock
anchors, possibly with intermediate drop structures. The assessment supports
concept planning and approvals only and excludes detailed design of
stabilisation, anchors, structural supports or temporary works; the client
advised that other parts of the broader drainage upgrade did not need
investigation.

## Fieldwork

- Site walkover survey and BYDA search plus contractor on-site services search
- 2 boreholes (BH01 and BH02) drilled on 3 February 2026 by Tightsight Sampling Pty Ltd, hand auger in soil and NMLC coring in rock with portable rock coring equipment, hole size 125 mm
- BH01 terminated at 4.13 m (surface RL 29.49 m); BH02 terminated at 3.77 m (surface RL 30.55 m)
- DCP tests carried out adjacent to the boreholes (DCP = 0 at BH01 surface, DCP = 7 at BH02 surface, with double bounce recorded at refusal)
- Rock core photographed and groundwater observations recorded during and shortly after drilling; boreholes backfilled by hand with spoil
- Disturbed soil and rock sampling for NATA-accredited laboratory testing (8 PLSI tests, 1 aggressivity suite)

## Subsurface profile as logged

- Unit 1 - Fill: silty sandy CLAY, low plasticity, grey, fine to medium grained sand, some grass rootlets, moisture < plastic limit; base 0.3-1.1 m
- Unit 2 - 'Probable' Residual (BH01 only): sandy CLAY, medium plasticity, brown and pale grey, fine to medium grained sand, moisture < plastic limit; base 1.5 m
- Unit 3a - Bedrock (BH01 only): SANDSTONE, extremely weathered, fine to medium grained, red, brown, pale grey, very low strength; base 1.69 m
- Unit 3c - Bedrock: sandstone, highly weathered, fine to medium grained, brown, very low strength, highly fractured to disaggregated, locally friable with soil-like infill, interpreted as a weathered seam or defect zone; base 2.85 m
- Unit 3b - Bedrock: SANDSTONE, slightly weathered, fine to medium grained, red and pale grey becoming grey, low to medium strength, Class IV/III; base >3.77 m (BH02) to >4.13 m (BH01)

## Rock

Hawkesbury Sandstone was reached in both holes. Extremely weathered very low
strength sandstone was logged in BH01 from about 1.5 m to 1.69 m; in BH02 the
fill passes into cored rock from about 0.3 m. Section 10.3 states competent
Class IV/III sandstone occurs at relatively shallow depth, typically below about
0.3 m to 1.69 m below existing ground level at the borehole locations. A
distinctly weaker highly weathered horizon (Unit 3c) was encountered in BH02 at
approximately 2.43 m to 2.85 m and may be localised. Point load Is(50) values
for the Hawkesbury Sandstone ranged from 0.0064 to 0.77 MPa, corresponding to
very low to medium strength, with estimated UCS of 0.096 to 11.55 MPa.

## Groundwater

Not encountered at the time of investigation. Both borehole logs record
GROUNDWATER NOT ENCOUNTERED. Circulating water used for the diamond drilling
prevented any groundwater level measurement. The report notes standing
groundwater and seepage can fluctuate with rainfall, temperature and other
factors.

## Recommendations

- Treat the fill as uncontrolled under AS 2870-2011 because no placement, material or compaction records exist
- Carry out dilapidation surveys of underground services and nearby structures before construction, and have owners confirm the records
- Avoid conventional shallow footings for the stormwater infrastructure and support structures because of steep terrain, rock outcrop and variable near-surface materials
- Found supports, thrust blocks, headwalls, pits and pier supports in competent Class IV/III sandstone at depth
- Design socketed elements to extend past any highly weathered or low strength sandstone zone, such as the Unit 3c horizon logged in BH02
- Verify founding conditions at every rock anchor and bracket location; anchors must sit in competent sandstone, not in highly weathered bands, with proof testing where required
- Confirm final founding level and socket length during construction by inspecting excavations, reviewing drilling spoil and drill response, and short confirmatory coring where needed
- Establish delineated exclusion zones along the Homer Street footpath where required
- Have a suitably experienced geotechnical consultant carry out staged inspection of the rockface at and above proposed fixing locations, with scaling or removal of loose material where identified
- Do not assume the presence, condition or support function of legacy rock bolts and shotcrete without inspection
- Control stormwater discharge and temporary diversions and protect against scour at drop structures and outlets
- Undertake targeted supplementary investigation (test pits or short cores) at critical support locations where weaker weathered sandstone bands are suspected
- Review final civil and structural drawings and the contractor's methodology, including access arrangements and footpath protection
- Implement geotechnical inspection hold points at each support location before drilling and during socketing, and adjust socket lengths as needed
- Complete a post-installation inspection with periodic follow-up checks after major rainfall events
- Select concrete and steel appropriate to the assessed aggressivity
- Re-review the assessment once the final alignment, fixing locations and construction methodology are confirmed, or if the works materially change drainage, slope geometry or crest/toe loading

## Stated limitations

- Prepared exclusively for H & H Consulting Engineers Pty Ltd for the described development and purpose, under CG's Terms of Engagement and proposal QU25-0483 Rev 0 dated 28 October 2025
- The slope stability assessment is qualitative, indicative and based on a walkover plus limited boreholes and coring; it is not a detailed design of stabilisation measures, anchor systems, structural supports or temporary works
- Ratings reflect conditions at the time of inspection and may change once the final alignment and fixing locations are set, vegetation is cleared or drainage patterns are altered
- Depths and layer thicknesses in Table 2 are based on the two investigation locations and may not represent the whole site
- Test locations were measured from the supplied plan to an accuracy of about +/- 3 m
- Circulating water precluded groundwater measurement during diamond drilling; standing water and seepage can fluctuate seasonally
- Is(50) testing is not readily performed on extremely low to very low strength rock, so strength classification for weaker sequences relied on visual and tactile assessment
- Geotechnical design parameters are for preliminary sizing only; detailed foundation sizing and structural design remain with the project structural designer
- Vegetation, soil mantle and leaf litter obscure rock exposures and may conceal loosened blocks, open joints and debris
- Conclusions rely on interpolation between discrete test locations; variations between them cannot be explicitly defined
- Must not be used as part of a construction specification without CG's written agreement, and must be kept and reproduced whole with the attached Information Sheets

## Reviewer flags

Extraction is clean throughout; the narrative, Table 2 (strata), Table 4 (PLSI),
Table 5 (risk matrix) and Table 6 (foundation parameters) all read reliably. Two
caveats. Table 3 (aggressivity) has rotated column headers that extracted as a
vertical jumble; the single data row reads 2.5 / 6.2 / 25 / 40,000 / 10 / 10 and
I have mapped these to moisture content, pH, conductivity, resistivity, chloride
and sulphate in header order, which is consistent, but confirm against the
source PDF. Second, there is a small internal inconsistency in depth reporting
for the weak Unit 3c horizon: Table 2 gives its base at 2.85 m without an upper
bound or a borehole attribution, while Section 10.3 places it in BH02 at
approximately 2.43 m to 2.85 m; I have carried both. Note also that unlike the
companion report, RLs are present here (BH01 surface RL 29.49 m, BH02 surface RL
30.55 m, datum stated only as 'm'), so rockhead can be expressed as RL if
needed. No AS 2870 site classification is given because the works are drainage
infrastructure, not residential slabs and footings; AS 2870 is cited only for
the uncontrolled-fill definition. Also note Section 7.2 describes Hawkesbury
Sandstone as a 'Wianamatta Group' subgroup, which is a geological error in the
source (Hawkesbury Sandstone underlies the Wianamatta Group).
