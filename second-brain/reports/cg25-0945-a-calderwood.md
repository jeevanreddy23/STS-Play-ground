## Scope

Core Geotech investigated a 42 ha rural property proposed for staged residential
subdivision, covering both sides of Macquarie Rivulet. The work involved a
desktop review of geological and soil landscape mapping, a site walkover, 26
test pits with adjacent DCP testing, and a laboratory programme, leading to a
ground model and advice on earthworks, batters, retention, footings, pavements,
salinity, aggressivity and slope stability.

## Fieldwork

- Fieldwork carried out on 6 and 7 August 2025 (per report body) after a BYDA search and on-site services location search, under full-time direction of a CG Geotechnical Engineer
- 26 test pits (TP01 to TP26) excavated to depths of 1 m to 3 m using a 5-tonne track-mounted excavator with a 450 mm wide bucket; pits backfilled with spoil and compacted by bucket and tracks
- Individual termination depths: TP01 1.0 m, TP02 2.1 m, TP03 3.0 m, TP04 1.7 m, TP05 to TP18 1.0 m each (TP17 1.0 m), TP19 3.0 m, TP20 2.0 m, TP21 to TP26 1.0 m each
- Dynamic Cone Penetrometer (DCP) tests adjacent to the test pits
- Groundwater observations recorded during excavation and shortly after completion
- Disturbed samples collected and sent to NATA-accredited laboratories (ALS, Benchmark Geotechnical) under Chain of Custody
- Laboratory schedule: 4 CBR (AS 1289.6.1.1), 4 PSD (AS 1289.3.6.1), 6 aggressivity soil set (APHA), 7 Atterberg limits and linear shrinkage (AS 1289 3.1.2, 3.2.1, 3.3.1, 3.4.1), 9 ESP/CEC (APHA), 35 EC/pH (APHA), 36 field moisture content (AS 1289.2.1.1)

## Subsurface profile as logged

- Topsoil - Silty CLAY / Silty Sandy CLAY / Silty Gravelly CLAY, medium plasticity, dark grey, trace fine grained sand, fine to medium gravel, moisture >= plastic limit; base 0.2 to 0.4 m (about 0.1 to 0.3 m thick per Section 10.1)
- 'Probable' Alluvium - Silty CLAY / Silty Gravelly CLAY, medium plasticity, red, brown, orange, grey, with fine grained sand, moisture >= plastic limit, firm to very stiff (locally soft); base >1.0 m; encountered in TP05 to TP07, TP11 to TP14, TP21 to TP24 and TP26 (low-lying ground near Macquarie Rivulet)
- 'Probable' Residual - Silty CLAY / Gravelly CLAY, medium to high plasticity, dark brown, grey, with shale boulders, moisture >= plastic limit, stiff to very stiff (becoming hard with depth); base 0.4 to >3.0 m; encountered in TP01 to TP03, TP08 to TP10, TP15 to TP20 and TP25 (northern, western and eastern elevated ground)
- Bedrock - SANDSTONE and interbedded CONGLOMERATE and Sandstone, extremely to highly weathered, fine to medium grained, brown, with cobbles, low to medium strength; base >1.0 to >2.1 m; recorded in TP02, TP04, TP17, TP20 and TP25
- No uncontrolled fill was observed in any test pit
- Test pit surface RLs range from 19.5 m to 36 m (datum shown as 'm' on the logs)

## Rock

Extremely to highly weathered SANDSTONE and interbedded CONGLOMERATE and
Sandstone, fine to medium grained, brown, with cobbles, low to medium strength.
Table 2 gives the bedrock unit as >1.0 m to >2.1 m depth in TP02, TP04, TP17,
TP20 and TP25; Section 10.1 states weathered sandstone or conglomerate was
encountered at depths between about 1.7 m and 2.1 m. The TP04 log shows
sandstone from 1.0 m depth at RL 23.5 (surface RL 24.5), with excavation refusal
and termination at 1.7 m. Depth-to-rock statements are not internally consistent
(see reviewer notes).

## Groundwater

Groundwater was encountered during the investigation in TP04, TP07, TP09, TP19,
TP20, TP24 and TP26 at depths of approximately 0.7 m to 0.9 m below existing
ground surface, with minor seepage and localised inflow consistent with perched
groundwater or shallow saturated horizons. It was not observed in the remaining
test pits, though moisture conditions were generally at or above the plastic
limit. Levels may not have stabilised over the short observation period, are
expected to fluctuate seasonally and to rise near the surface after prolonged
rainfall or flooding of Macquarie Rivulet, and no longer-term monitoring was
carried out. Surface ponding was observed in low-lying paddocks and along access
tracks on both sides of the rivulet.

## Recommendations

- Adopt Class P for the site as investigated because of abnormal moisture influences; reassess reactivity and differential movement after bulk earthworks and immediately before building pad construction, since fill depth will vary
- Design slabs and footings using sound geotechnical and structural principles accounting for both reactivity and structural loads, and provide the homeowner with CSIRO BTF-18 maintenance guidance
- Excavate residual soils and very low strength shale with conventional plant, with light ripping or hammering in the upper weathered rock; medium strength bedrock will need moderate to heavy ripping, large hydraulic hammers and rock saws or milling heads for footings and trenches
- Require bulk excavation tenderers to make their own assessment of plant, as excavation rates in rock vary with jointing, strength, machinery and operator
- Run a vibration trial at the start of rock excavation and monitor vibration during works near roads and infrastructure
- Proof roll the exposed subgrade under geotechnical observation, undercut any heaving or pumping areas to a firm base and replace with engineered fill; water and re-roll shrinkage-cracked subgrade
- Reuse excavated residual soils and weathered rock as engineered fill where clean, organic-free and no larger than 75 mm, subject to environmental consultant approval for residential land use
- Place engineered fill in 300 mm loose layers to 95% Standard MDD within +/- 2% Standard OMC under Level 1 supervision to AS 3798-2007, with regular density testing documented in a final Geotechnical Compliance Report
- Remove soft, saturated, compressible, organic or unknown-origin material, particularly in the alluvial ground near Macquarie Rivulet, and treat soft subgrade by undercutting, lime/cement stabilisation (dosage by testing to RMS QA Q3051 or equivalent), geogrid or geotextile reinforcement, or surcharge preloading and staged filling
- Overbuild fill batters at least 1 m beyond design geometry and trim back after filling to achieve edge compaction
- Backfill service trenches with engineered fill in 150 mm loose layers using trench rollers, padfoot attachments or rammers, with maximum 50 mm particle size, especially below stormwater pipes
- Use the stated maximum temporary batter slopes for cuts up to 2 m above groundwater, cover exposed clay and bedrock faces with a waterproof membrane, keep surcharge 2.5H clear, avoid near-vertical excavation in heavy rain, and inspect after rainfall over 30 mm in 24 hours or at any sign of instability
- Retain excavations adjacent to structures, boundaries or services with an in-situ wall system where batters cannot be achieved, designed to AS 4678-2002 using the tabulated parameters and allowing for compaction-induced pressures (AS 4678 Figure J5) and hydrostatic pressure unless permanent drainage is installed
- Adopt 100 kPa for high level footings at least 0.8 m deep in Level 1 engineered fill or very stiff natural clay, provided shrink-swell movements can be accommodated; avoid planters, gardens and grassed areas immediately adjacent to buildings on high level footings and extend pavements around the perimeter to reduce differential movement
- Excavate, clean, inspect and pour footings with minimum delay, or protect the base with at least 75 mm of blinding concrete, and prevent ponding in footing excavations
- Adopt a design subgrade CBR of 4% (or 40 MPa short-term modulus) for internal roads and confirm traffic loading before finalising pavement design; inspect the subgrade after preliminary boxing to identify stabilisation requirements
- Blend, treat, or lime/gypsum stabilise soils with ESP over 15% before reuse, and provide surface drainage control, vegetative cover and erosion protection (turfing, geofabric in concentrated flow paths) during construction
- Maintain surface drainage, minimise uncontrolled fill and protect exposed soils with turf or erosion matting to manage shallow slumping and rutting in the soft near-surface zones
- Manage ongoing erosion and shallow bank instability along Macquarie Rivulet through surface drainage, vegetation maintenance and setback of engineered fill and structures from the top of bank
- Install temporary surface drainage (interceptor drains, sump pumping) where seepage or ponding occurs and incorporate permanent subsoil drainage in detailed design; provide subsoil drains around pavement perimeters with inverts at least 200 mm below subgrade
- Install a network of roughly 6 to 8 standpipe piezometers and monitor groundwater seasonally to confirm design levels for pavements, building platforms and retaining wall drainage
- Carry out a detailed geotechnical investigation at final design levels before detailed design of retaining walls and road pavements
- Inspect any excavation deeper than 1.5 m, review structural drawings, and re-engage the geotechnical engineer if conditions or the design change significantly

## Reviewer flags

Report body extracted cleanly and the parameter tables are legible; the test pit
logs are not. Points to verify against the source PDF: (1) Fieldwork date
conflict - the body states 6 and 7 August 2025, but all 26 test pit log headers
read 'DATE STARTED 6/7/25 COMPLETED 6/7/25', which reads as 6 July 2025 in
Australian format. The ISO date given here is the report issue date (6 October
2025), which is unambiguous. (2) Depth to rock is inconsistent: Table 2 lists
bedrock in TP02, TP04, TP17, TP20 and TP25 and Section 10.1 puts it at about 1.7
m to 2.1 m, yet the TP17 and TP25 logs terminate at 1.0 m and the TP04 log shows
sandstone from 1.0 m (RL 23.5) with termination at 1.7 m. Rockhead depths should
be re-read from the original logs. (3) Section 8.1 gives a plasticity index
range of 5.5% to 62%, but the minimum PI in Table 3 is 11% - 5.5% is the minimum
linear shrinkage, so the PI lower bound appears to be a transcription slip. (4)
Table 1 lists 7 Atterberg/linear shrinkage tests while Table 3 reports only 6
sets of LL/PI/LS results. (5) Section 8.6 and Section 9.1 refer to 35 EC/pH
samples, but the extracted Table 8 contains only 16 unique rows plus duplicate
TP01 (0.3-0.5) and TP02 (0.8-1.0) rows repeated at the foot of the table - the
full table may not have carried through the extraction. (6) The test pit logs
are column-collapsed in the extracted text: DCP entries appear as isolated 'DCP
= 1' / 'DCP = 2' markers with loose blow-count digits floating beside the strata
descriptions, so per-depth DCP profiles and the water-level symbols cannot be
reconstructed reliably. Only the summary statement of 1 to 2 blows per 100 mm in
the upper 0.8 m and the 19 'NONE ENCOUNTERED' water annotations can be relied
on. (7) Section 7.2 cites the Wollongong 1:250,000 Sheet SI 56-9 Second Edition
1966, while reference item 8 cites the Wollongong - Port Hacking 1:100,000 Sheet
9029-9129 (Edition 1) 1985. (8) Section 10.2 refers to 'Australian Geogrids LR8'
where the appendix list and AGS publication name is Australian Geoguide LR8. (9)
Section 8.3 concludes the tested soils are 'silty clays of low plasticity' and
suitable for engineered fill, which sits awkwardly against the LL up to 84%, PI
up to 62% and the H1-H2 reactivity assessment elsewhere in the report. (10) No
allowable bearing pressure is given for footings founded directly on the
weathered sandstone or conglomerate, and no pile capacities are provided despite
deep foundations being in the stated scope. (11) Table 1 lists 36 FMC tests;
Section 8.1 accounts for 34 soil plus 2 sandstone samples, but Table 3 lists 39
rows - the count should be checked. (12) A design CBR of 4% is adopted although
all four measured CBR values were 8% to 20%; this is conservative but the report
does not state the basis for the reduction.
