# Set up the plate load test loading train under a reaction counterweight

> **Source:** `050-Setup Proceedure for PLT Draft 4 Feb 2024.pdf` (Section D Steps 1-4 and Section E, pp. 2-4), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** The engineer can position a reaction weight, prepare the bearing surface, and assemble the plate, load cell, hydraulic cylinder and ball joint ready for loading.

## Inputs

- A stable reaction counterweight (e.g. loaded backhoe or truck)
- Loading plate / load cell module, hydraulic cylinder, extensions, magnetic ball joint
- Hydraulic pump and hoses
- Trowel or spatula, brush, hand level
- Fine to medium grained bedding sand

## Procedure

1. Park the counterweight over the test location, leaving clearance greater than 600 mm to fit the loading device beneath the jacking point (p. 2).
2. Drop a little sand or loose soil from the jacking point to mark where the plate centre must sit (p. 2).
3. Prepare a test area large enough for the plate, trim it to a smooth level plane with a trowel or spatula checked against a hand level, and brush away loose material.
4. Seat the loading plate / load cell module by pressing it down with a slight twisting motion; on rough surfaces first spread a thin layer of fine to medium bedding sand to improve contact.
5. Place the hydraulic cylinder on the plate module and fix the magnetic ball joint to a stable point on the counterweight.
6. Pump the hydraulics only until the piston just touches the upper ball joint without applying load, keeping the ball joint bolt from jamming in the piston; fit extensions to the piston if the gap requires them.
7. Set up the pump: confirm the hydraulic coupling is fully closed with its cap nut, crack open the bleed/fill cover part of a turn, use the discharge valve (counterclockwise to lower, clockwise to stop) to retract the piston as needed, then return the bleed/fill cover to its prescribed position.

## Decision rules

- Use bedding sand only when the prepared surface is rough (p. 3).
- Use piston extensions when the cylinder cannot otherwise reach the ball joint (p. 3).

## Checks

- The sand-drop mark confirms the plate is centred under the jacking point.
- The piston contacts the ball joint with no load registered and no jamming of the ball joint bolt.

## Common errors

- Applying load while bringing the piston up to the ball joint.
- Jamming the ball joint bolt in the piston.
- Leaving loose material or an uneven surface under the plate.

## Caveats

- No minimum counterweight mass is stated in this document (the companion Anix loading-stage sheet gives a reaction-load rule).
- Some pump-setup details are placeholders awaiting training photos, so field verification against the equipment manual is advisable.
