# Manage over-wet earth fill using NR44 placement strategies

> **Source:** `nr44 Earthworks Specification.pdf` (Guide commentary to Clause 7.2 (moisture) and Clause 7.4.1 (NR44 Ed 2 Rev 0)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Over-optimum-moisture material is placed or treated without compromising embankment stability or pavement support, minimising drying downtime.

## Inputs

- Moisture content results vs the project A5 range
- Material type (permeability, expansiveness)
- Embankment geometry and available placement zones (deep fill core, medians, flat batters)
- Season/climate outlook and available drying areas

## Procedure

1. Recognise early that drying over-wet clays is slow in winter/wet seasons and that large drying areas rarely exist at project start; if wet heavy clays in cuttings are known pre-tender, push for specified (and paid) stabilisation instead of relying on drying.
2. Where over-optimum cutting material must be used, consider placement deep in high fills with geotechnical branch concurrence - compaction is achievable but the specified moisture may not be, and compaction-induced pore pressures will cause heave under plant.
3. Pace the work: wait (typically overnight) for pore pressures to dissipate before the next layer; avoid stacking layers in quick succession on wet clays.
4. Check for problematic heave by proof rolling with a fully loaded item of plant and watching for surface movement - minor movement at depth is normally tolerable for the finished pavement.
5. Use zoned construction (wet material in the fill core where future moisture change is small) rather than full-width placement of over-wet expansive clay; alternatively place over-wet material in wide medians or very flat batters clear of the carriageway deflection zone.
6. Stay alert to apparently stable layers destabilising after rain or when wet material is placed on them.

## Decision rules

- Full-width over-wet expansive clay placement risks embankment instability - zone it or relocate it.
- If heave under loaded plant is severe or persists, stop and allow further dissipation or re-treat; do not bury the problem under the next layer.
- The specification still governs conformity: deviations (using material outside the moisture range) need Principal/geotechnical concurrence, not just site judgement.

## Checks

- Pore-pressure/heave observations logged layer by layer
- Placement zones for wet material clear of pavement deflection influence
- Contract cost allocation understood - drying, lime treatment and rehandling sit with the contractor unless specified otherwise

## Common errors

- Programming earthworks assuming quick drying in cool/wet months
- Placing successive wet layers before pore pressures dissipate, then failing proof rolling
- Treating minor deep-seated movement under proof rolling as automatic rejection

## Caveats

- NR44 is guidance on intent, not a contract document; R44 and the contract govern acceptance.
- Based on R44 Ed 4 Rev 1 - cross-check clause numbers against the project's R44 edition.

## Standards cited

RMS R44, RMS 3211 (lime), RMS T198
