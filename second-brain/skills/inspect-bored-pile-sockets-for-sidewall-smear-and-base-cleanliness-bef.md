# Inspect bored pile sockets for sidewall smear and base cleanliness before certifying design assumptions

> **Source:** `Bored Piles Socketed Into Shale And Sandstone by pells.pdf` (Walker & Pells, Australian Geomechanics Dec 1998, pp 31-33; Sections 3 (Inspection) and 6 (Pile construction and inspection)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A socket confirmed (or rejected) as meeting the cleanliness and roughness conditions that modern high-capacity side-shear and end-bearing design values assume.

## Inputs

- Socket diameter, depth and drilling method (dry, under water, under mud, hand excavation)
- Design assumptions on sidewall roughness class and base cleanliness
- Access method (descent by cage/ladder for >=600 mm diameter, downhole TV camera for smaller)
- Nearby cored borehole logs or proving holes in the socket base

## Procedure

1. Confirm rock quality at socket level matches the class assumed in design, using nearby cored boreholes or cored/percussion proving holes in the base.
2. Inspect sidewalls for crushed or smeared rock or soil; smear from auger drilling in moist weathered rock can reach roughly 20 mm thick and infills the grooves that generate side shear.
3. Inspect the base for debris (softened, broken or crushed rock), which alters load-deflection behaviour and increases working-load settlement.
4. If smear or debris is found, require cleaning (drilling or stirring under water, special sidewall-cleaning tools, hand or purpose-built bucket base cleaning) and re-inspect.
5. Record and report the inspection items required by the specification (the paper's Draft Specification Clause A6.2) and certify only that inspections were done and construction matches design assumptions.

## Decision rules

- Piles that cannot be inspected (method precludes descent or camera access) must not be designed with the high side-shear and end-bearing parameters of the referenced methods.
- Piles formed under drilling mud should not normally use parameters applicable to piles formed dry or under water.
- Piles under 600 mm diameter cannot be descended, so use a downhole TV camera.
- If the required cleanliness standard is uneconomic for the project, revert to conservative code-level capacities instead of invoking high design values.

## Checks

- Sidewall substantially free of crushed/smeared material; base debris within design limits.
- Rock class at founding level matches design assumption.
- NSW confined-space requirements (harness, breathing apparatus, crane licence for lowering personnel) satisfied for manned descent.

## Common errors

- Assuming flight-auger drilling in moist weathered shale or sandstone leaves clean walls; it usually does not.
- Relying on drag-pick buckets or augers for final base cleaning; they usually leave too much debris.
- Having the inspecting engineer engaged by the contractor rather than the principal, creating a conflict of interest.
- Treating statutory confined-space rules as a reason to skip inspection; they restrict but do not preclude descent.

## Caveats

- Guidance is based on Sydney-area geology; adapt with care elsewhere.
- This is a design reference, not a certification: all values and acceptance criteria must be verified against the source paper and reviewed by a qualified geotechnical engineer before use.
