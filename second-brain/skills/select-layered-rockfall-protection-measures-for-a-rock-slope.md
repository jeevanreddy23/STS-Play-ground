# Select layered rockfall protection measures for a rock slope

> **Source:** [YouTube — How to Protect Slopes from Rock Falls — Effective and Inexpensive Methods](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZD7aTftDZn4&t=0s) (00:00-09:33). A YouTube lecture — learning

**Outcome.** A practical, cost-conscious combination of scaling, benches, fence or barrier, ditch and drapery mesh that contains future rockfalls on a loose-rock slope

## Inputs

- Site walkover observations: loose rocks at the crest, fallen rocks on benches and at the toe
- Presence and condition of existing benches
- Value and proximity of infrastructure at the toe (building, highway) that needs protection
- Typical falling-rock sizes, from inspection or simulation

## Procedure

1. Start with a site investigation / rockfall hazard assessment to map loose rocks at the crest, on benches, and at the toe
2. Scale the slope first: remove all loose rocks — presented as one of the most effective and cheapest measures, and standard practice before anything else
3. Assess existing benches as rock catchment; a bench that already holds fallen rocks is demonstrably working, with only some rocks bouncing over
4. Where rocks can still reach the toe, choose between a fence/barrier and a ditch depending on the importance of the infrastructure being protected and the budget; simulate fence performance in rockfall software if needed
5. Design the ditch width and depth from slope inclination and height (a separate design method), and use a gravel bed in the ditch to absorb kinetic energy so rocks cannot bounce out
6. Hang drapery mesh over source areas so falling rocks stay constrained against the face instead of bouncing outward; multiple mesh panels can cover separate source zones
7. Leave the bottom edge of drapery mesh open so contained rocks can fall through and accumulate on the bench or in the ditch for later cleanout

## Decision rules

- Scaling comes first on essentially any slope with rockfall potential
- Spend on high-capacity fencing only when large rock sizes or high-value infrastructure justify it; modest rock sizes may need only an inexpensive fence
- Never fix drapery mesh closed at the bottom: trapped rocks accumulate behind it, have nowhere to go, and progressively damage the mesh

## Checks

- After design, confirm each hazard path is addressed: crest sources (scaled/meshed), mid-slope trajectories (bench/mesh), toe run-out (fence, barrier or ditch)
- Plan periodic cleanout of benches and ditches as part of the scheme

## Common errors

- Installing barriers or mesh without scaling, leaving the loose-rock source in place
- Closing the drapery mesh at the toe, causing rock accumulation and mesh damage
- Omitting the gravel bed so rocks bounce out of the ditch

## Caveats

- YouTube lecture — learning material, not an approved design procedure; verify against project specification and current standards
- Ditch sizing and fence capacity design are only referenced, not derived, in this lecture — they need the companion methods or project-specific design
