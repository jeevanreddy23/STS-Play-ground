# Scope the extent and depth of a site investigation using the Look planning tables

> **Source:** `Handbook of Geotechnical Investigation and Design Tables_Bert Look 2014.pdf` (Chapter 1 Site investigation, Sections 1.6-1.13 and 1.18-1.19 (Tables 1.6 to 1.13, 1.18, 1.19), pp 5-17), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A defensible investigation plan - number, spacing and depth of boreholes, test pits and in-situ tests - matched to the geotechnical category and project phase, with the reasoning traceable to published guidance.

## Inputs

- Project phase (desktop, planning/IAS, preliminary design, detailed design)
- Development type (building, bridge, road, embankment, culvert, car park, canal, tower)
- Anticipated foundation type and loaded width B, or pile diameter
- Site area (hectares) and map scale of the current phase
- Known or suspected competent strata depth and groundwater conditions
- Capital cost estimate and consequence-of-failure setting

## Procedure

1. Assign a geotechnical category first. Enter Look Table 1.8 with nature and size of construction, surroundings, ground and groundwater conditions, seismicity and capital cost; read across to GC1, GC2 or GC3. The category sets the level of investigation, the expertise required, and the indicative SI budget as a percentage of capital cost.
2. Cross-check the category against the development risk ranking. Enter Table 1.13 with the development type and read the risk letters for loading, environment, water, ground, economic and design life; the overall band maps back to the GC bands, so a mismatch between Table 1.8 and Table 1.13 is a prompt to re-examine the assumptions rather than to average them.
3. Set test intensity from the phase. Enter Table 1.6 with the phase and its typical map scale to read boreholes per hectare and an approximate spacing. Look's own rule is that testing intensity should track the map scale of the current phase - do not run detailed-design intensity on a feasibility footprint, or the reverse.
4. Set spacing and depth from the development type. Enter Table 1.10a (structures) and 1.10b (linear works, canals, culverts, car parks) with the development to read a spacing and a depth expressed in multiples of footing width B, pile diameter, or a fixed penetration below the founding level or invert.
5. Overlay the load criterion: for square footings investigate below the depth where the pressure bulb has decayed to a small fraction of the applied load (a multiple of B), and a larger multiple for strip footings. Where load and width are not yet known - the usual case at this stage - substitute the ground criterion and drill to a proven competent stratum, probing below any compressible layer.
6. For piles driven to refusal on rock, use Table 1.11 to set the minimum drilling depth beyond refusal by material type (sandstone versus shale each have their own inferred-N trigger plus a fixed further penetration). Note the table was calibrated on Queensland bridge piles of a particular size class and that steel piles drive further than the table assumes.
7. For groundwater, size the observation-well network from Table 1.7 by area under study, and set well depth from the lowest expected groundwater level across the hydrological year, not the level on the day of drilling.
8. Phase the work. Use Table 1.3 and Table 1.4 to allocate effort across desktop study, definition of needs, preliminary investigation, detailed investigation and construction monitoring, and plan the preliminary investigation as a defined fraction of the planned detailed investigation so it can steer it.
9. Close out the plan with the Table 1.9 planning checklist - informative items, site-specific safety plan, and SI management items - before any ground truthing.
10. Estimate duration and cost from Table 1.18 productivity rates (land, floating barge, jack-up barge; cyclonic versus non-cyclonic) and sense-check the total against Table 1.19 percentage-of-capital-cost ranges by work type.

## Decision rules

- Geotechnical category drives everything downstream: level of investigation, then extent, then choice of plant. Work in that order.
- Where the two-stage requirement is triggered (GC3), a single-shot investigation is not compliant with the intent of the table - split it.
- Depth is governed by whichever is deeper: the load criterion (multiples of B) or the ground criterion (proven competent stratum below any compressible layer).
- Services searches are mandatory before ground truthing; treat any service within a few metres of a test location as requiring physical location or isolation.
- A preliminary or exploratory hole used directly in final design without further work is flagged in the Preface as a recurring cause of both over-conservative and under-designed outcomes - do not let phase 1 data become the design basis by default.

## Checks

- Boreholes per hectare achieved versus Table 1.6 target for the phase
- Depth achieved versus both the B-multiple and the competent-strata criterion
- Groundwater observation points versus Table 1.7 for the study area
- Investigation budget as a percentage of capital works versus Table 1.8 and 1.19 - Look nominates a floor below which information is insufficient
- Planning checklist (Table 1.9) signed off before mobilisation

## Common errors

- Setting borehole spacing from cost rather than from phase and development type
- Terminating holes at refusal without the additional penetration required by Table 1.11 for driven piles
- Measuring water level once at the end of drilling and treating it as the design groundwater level
- Applying structure spacings (Table 1.10a) to linear works, which have their own rules in 1.10b
- Assuming Queensland/Australian cost and productivity figures transfer unchanged to other markets or to restricted-access sites

## Caveats

- Reference text, not a design standard - verify against the source pages and Australian practice before use.
- Cost, productivity and category cost thresholds are 2013 Australian figures and need escalation and market adjustment.
- Table 1.11 depth rules were derived for Queensland bridge piles of a specific size range; confirm applicability before adopting elsewhere.
