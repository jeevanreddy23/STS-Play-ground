# Operate, maintain and repair a working platform through its service life

> **Source:** `Working Platforms for tracked plant.pdf` (Chapter 5 (Operation, maintenance and repair); Sections 1.3, 1.6), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A platform that keeps performing to its design over the works duration, with controlled usage, monitored condition, prompt reinstatement of excavations, and clear responsibility for upkeep.

## Inputs

- Design statement: permitted plant, designated working areas, acceptable minimum thickness, design life
- Contractual allocation of maintenance/repair responsibility (principal contractor holds overall site safety responsibility under CDM)
- Monitoring arrangements: regular visual assessment of platform, plant behaviour and drainage

## Procedure

1. Keep the platform under day-to-day control of competent site staff; ensure contractual arrangements cover inspection, control and maintenance for the whole working life.
2. Restrict use to the plant and areas the platform was designed for; manage conflicts where the platform doubles as a haul road (concrete trucks, service cranes may need strengthened routes).
3. Monitor regularly: visual assessment of performance, drainage, contamination (mud, bentonite, spoil) and rutting; simple thickness checks with profile boards; give special attention to ramps and heavily trafficked routes.
4. Maintain a reasonably level, well-drained surface; prevent water flowing across the platform; use suitably graded stable material similar to the installation spec for maintenance.
5. Permit any activity that breaches the platform (e.g. excavation through it) only under competent supervision, and reinstate to the original specification.
6. Backfill all empty bores with self-compacting granular material; take particular care backfilling around guide walls.
7. Repair damage promptly, reinstating to the required specification; increase local thickness above minimum where performance problems demand.

## Decision rules

- Most rig instability incidents trace to maintenance/repair failures rather than design or installation defects — treat deterioration signals as safety-critical, not cosmetic.
- Rig bouncing when travelling is not covered by design calculations; it must be prevented operationally, with specialist advice if it persists.
- If the plant list, loading or usage changes from what was designed for, refer back to the platform designer before allowing it.

## Checks

- Regular documented inspections; deteriorating areas detected before they become hazards.
- All excavations through the platform logged and reinstated to spec; empty pile bores backfilled.
- Platform edges, exclusion zones and unfinished areas clearly marked and physically controlled.

## Common errors

- Assuming the original design protects against a platform later trafficked by heavier plant or used as an unplanned haul road.
- Leaving bores or service trenches through the platform unreinstated or poorly backfilled — the classic localised failure that overturns rigs.
- Letting surface water pond or flow across the platform, softening the subgrade.
- Unclear responsibility between subcontractors so nobody owns maintenance.

## Caveats

- OCR noise present — verify details against the original BR 470.
- Responsibility framework is UK CDM-based; translate to the applicable local safety legislation.

## Standards cited

BRE BR 470 (2004), CDM Regulations (UK), CIRIA C703 (crane stability on site)
