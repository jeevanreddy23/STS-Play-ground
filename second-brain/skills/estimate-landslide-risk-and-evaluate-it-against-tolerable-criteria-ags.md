# Estimate landslide risk and evaluate it against tolerable criteria (AGS 2007)

> **Source:** `42_1_4_amend.pdf` (AGS Practice Note 2007, Sections 6-8 (Eqns 1-2 in 7.1; Table 1 in 8.2), Appendices C and F), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Quantified risk to life and quantified or qualitative risk to property for every landslide hazard, summed and compared against regulator tolerable-risk criteria to decide accept/treat.

## Inputs

- Hazard analysis outputs: P(H), travel distance and P(S:H) for each landslide
- Elements at risk (property portions, people, services, roads, vehicles) listed per hazard
- Occupancy/usage data for temporal spatial probability P(T:S)
- Vulnerability estimates (Appendix F examples for persons); property values and indicative damage costs
- Regulator's tolerable risk criteria (or AGS Table 1 suggested values where none set)

## Procedure

1. List elements at risk for each landslide hazard, including environmental/social where relevant (Section 6.1).
2. Assess temporal spatial probability P(T:S) as the proportion of time the person/element occupies the affected area, allowing for warning and evacuation potential (Section 6.2).
3. Evaluate property consequence: vulnerability V(Prop:S) from slide volume, position, displacement and rate; estimate indicative total damage cost (reinstatement, stabilisation, fees, consequential costs) and market value — documented broad-brush estimates suffice (Section 6.3).
4. Evaluate consequence to persons V(D:T) considering slide volume/type/depth/velocity, burial potential and whether persons are in the open, vehicles or buildings; even small slides and single boulders can kill (Section 6.4).
5. Compute property risk R(Prop) = P(H) x P(S:H) x P(T:S) x V(Prop:S) x E, and individual loss-of-life risk R(LoL) = P(H) x P(S:H) x P(T:S) x V(D:T) (Section 7.1, Eqns 1-2).
6. Sum risk across ALL landslide hazards for the property and for the person most at risk; state clearly whether results are 'as existing' or residual after mitigation.
7. For property, optionally express results with the preferred qualitative likelihood/consequence/risk matrix of Appendix C (which supersedes AGS 2000 Appendix G).
8. Where many lives could be lost in one event (schools, hospitals, hotels), assess societal risk via frequency-number (f-N) pairs and total annual risk sum(f x N) (Section 7.4(b)).
9. Evaluate against tolerable criteria: AGS-suggested individual loss-of-life tolerable risks are given in Table 1 (order 1e-4/annum for existing slopes/development, 1e-5/annum for new slopes/development and existing landslides — verify against source); acceptable risk is typically one order of magnitude lower (Section 8.2).

## Decision rules

- Risk to life is always assessed quantitatively for the person most at risk; property risk may be qualitative/semi-quantitative for screening or data-poor cases (Sections 3.3.4, 7.2).
- Existing landslides attract the stricter 'new' criterion even if remediation is not otherwise required (Table 1 note 5).
- If risk exceeds tolerable, mitigation is mandatory before acceptance; ALARP applies within the tolerable band.
- Non-standard qualitative schemes are acceptable to regulators only if clearly defined, mapped to the preferred Appendix C terminology, and demonstrably better suited.

## Checks

- All hazards summed — not just the dominant one — for comparison with criteria.
- P(T:S) between 0 and 1, with building occupancy taken as ~1.0 for houses per Section 7.1 unless justified.
- Sensitivity of the result to uncertain inputs displayed; conservative outcome adopted where unjustified uncertainty remains.

## Common errors

- Back-converting qualitative terms into probabilities (explicitly warned against).
- Comparing single-hazard risk to the criterion instead of summed risk for the person most at risk.
- Not stating whether the reported risk is pre- or post-mitigation (residual).
- Ignoring societal risk for high-occupancy buildings.

## Caveats

- Tolerable-risk values quoted are pointers to Table 1 — confirm against the source and against the specific regulator's adopted criteria, which govern.
- Vulnerability values are indicative examples only (Appendix F); justify site-specific choices.

## Standards cited

AGS 2000 (superseded companion), AGS 2007a/2007d/2007e, Local regulator LRM policies (e.g. NSW council landslide policies)
