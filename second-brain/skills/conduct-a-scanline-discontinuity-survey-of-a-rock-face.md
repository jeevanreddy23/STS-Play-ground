# Conduct a scanline discontinuity survey of a rock face

> **Source:** [YouTube — How to Perform Fracture Discontinuity Survey of Rock Mass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pe1tcfjycQY&t=26s) (00:26-04:33). A YouTube lecture — learning
> material, not an approved design procedure. Equations and numbers were
> heard through auto-captions; read them off the video before use, and
> verify anything design-relevant against the standard and the project
> specification. Engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A field record, per discontinuity along a scanline, of type, orientation, persistence, aperture/infill, roughness, and spacing/count — the inputs needed by rock mass classification systems

## Inputs

- Accessible rock wall or exposure
- Tape/rule for the scanline and for measuring persistence and aperture
- Compass-clinometer for dip and dip direction
- Survey sheet or spreadsheet with type options (fault, joint, cleavage, shear, foliation, bedding)

## Procedure

1. Establish a scanline (an imaginary measuring line) across the face and work systematically along it, e.g. left to right
2. For each discontinuity intersected, record its type (bedding plane, joint set, shear plane, fault, cleavage, foliation)
3. Measure and record dip and dip direction (compass-clinometer technique covered in a companion video)
4. Measure persistence — the trace length of the discontinuity (about 1 m for the demonstrated example)
5. Assess aperture: measure the opening of open joints (about 3 mm in the demo); record tightly closed joints as closed; record any infill material (e.g. clay or sand) when present
6. Assess surface roughness (measurement method covered in a companion video)
7. Measure the spacing between successive discontinuities along the scanline
8. Count the total number of discontinuities intersected by the line (nine in the demo) and use the count to estimate average spacing

## Decision rules

- Spacing and discontinuity count are the priority measurements: they feed classification systems and indicate rock mass strength
- Record infill material type whenever a joint is not clean — soil infill (clay, sand) changes behaviour

## Checks

- Every discontinuity crossing the scanline has an entry — none skipped
- Count along the line and measured spacings are mutually consistent

## Common errors

- Recording only orientations and neglecting persistence, aperture, and infill
- Measuring spacing between arbitrary joint pairs rather than successive intersections along the scanline

## Caveats

- YouTube lecture — learning material, not an approved design procedure; verify against project specification and current standards
- The demonstration uses a rock wall simulation; field surveys add access and safety considerations not covered here
