# Determine maximum dry density and optimum moisture content by Proctor compaction

> **Source:** `Principles of Geotechnical Engineering 8th Edition.pdf` (Das 8th ed., Ch. 6, §§6.2–6.3 (compaction principles, standard Proctor), 6.4 (factors), 6.5 (modified Proctor), 6.13 (evaluating fill materials)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A compaction curve with maximum dry density and optimum moisture content at a stated compactive effort, benchmarked against the zero-air-void line, for use as the reference in earthworks specifications.

## Inputs

- Representative bulk sample, air-dried and broken down, screened to the maximum particle size the chosen method allows
- Compaction mould of known volume with collar and base plate
- Rammer of the specified mass and drop for standard or modified effort
- Balance, oven, moisture tins and mixing equipment
- Specific gravity of solids for the zero-air-void line

## Procedure

1. Choose standard or modified effort to match the specification, and choose the mould/method variant that suits the maximum particle size of the material.
2. Mix the soil to a moisture content well dry of the expected optimum and compact it into the mould in the specified number of equal layers, applying the specified number of rammer blows per layer, distributed evenly.
3. Trim level with the top of the mould after removing the collar, weigh, and take a moisture sample from the full height of the compacted specimen.
4. Compute bulk density from the mass and mould volume, then dry density from the measured moisture content.
5. Repeat at successively higher moisture contents, at least two points dry of and two points wet of the peak, so the curve is defined on both limbs.
6. Plot dry density against moisture content, draw a smooth curve, and read off maximum dry density and optimum moisture content at the peak.
7. Plot the zero-air-void (saturation) curve on the same axes from the specific gravity of solids, and confirm the compaction curve lies to its left and approaches it on the wet limb.

## Decision rules

- Increasing compactive effort raises maximum dry density and lowers optimum moisture content — always state which effort applies to a quoted maximum dry density.
- If the material contains oversize particles, either use the larger-mould method variant or apply an oversize correction, and record which was done.
- Do not re-use compacted material for successive points in soils that break down under compaction; use fresh material for each point.
- For free-draining granular soils the compaction curve is often flat or one-and-a-half peaked — use the density index (relative density) route instead.

## Checks

- Check that no plotted point lies on or to the right of the zero-air-void line — that is physically impossible and indicates an error in specific gravity, moisture content or mould volume.
- Check the mould volume calibration and rammer drop height.
- Check the peak is bracketed by at least two points each side; an unbracketed peak is not a valid optimum.

## Common errors

- Quoting a maximum dry density without stating standard or modified effort.
- Sampling moisture from only the top layer, which is typically wetter than the average.
- Ignoring oversize material, so the laboratory maximum does not represent the material actually being placed.

## Caveats

- Textbook learning material, not a design standard — verify method currency and all values against the source and Australian practice before use
- Mould volumes, rammer masses, drop heights, layer counts and blow counts are specified numbers — take them from the governing standard, not from this description.
- Australian practice uses AS 1289.5.1.1 (standard) and AS 1289.5.2.1 (modified); apparatus differs from ASTM.
