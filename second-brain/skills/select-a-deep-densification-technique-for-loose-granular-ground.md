# Select a deep densification technique for loose granular ground

> **Source:** `Principles of Geotechnical Engineering 8th Edition.pdf` (Das 8th ed., Ch. 6, §6.14 (vibroflotation, dynamic compaction, blasting) with the fill evaluation table in §6.13), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A justified choice among vibroflotation, dynamic compaction and related deep compaction methods, with spacing/energy assumptions and a verification plan.

## Inputs

- Thickness, grading and fines content of the loose deposit, and depth to groundwater
- Target density index or bearing/settlement performance requirement
- Site constraints: adjacent structures, vibration limits, access, availability of backfill
- Baseline in-situ testing (SPT, CPT or density index) against which improvement will be measured

## Procedure

1. Screen the deposit's grading against the published suitability zones: clean sands and gravelly sands respond well to vibroflotation, while excessive fine sand and silt content sharply reduces effectiveness.
2. For vibroflotation, plan the four-stage sequence — jet the vibrating unit down under water, let it sink to depth, add granular backfill from the surface as the hole is fed, then raise the unit in short lifts holding vibration at each lift.
3. Set probe spacing on a grid appropriate to the objective: square or rectangular patterns beneath isolated shallow foundations, equilateral triangular patterns for treating large areas; the effective radius of compaction increases with the power rating of the unit.
4. For dynamic compaction, select tamper mass, drop height and grid spacing from the required depth of influence, and plan multiple passes with an ironing pass at the end.
5. Estimate and check ground vibration at neighbouring structures before committing to dynamic compaction.
6. Verify improvement with post-treatment in-situ testing on the same basis as the baseline, testing at the least-favourable point in the grid (typically the centroid between probes) and allowing for ageing effects before testing.

## Decision rules

- Discard vibroflotation where the fines content places the soil outside the effective grading zone; consider stone columns for reinforcement (rather than densification) instead.
- Dynamic compaction depth of influence scales with the square root of the energy per drop — use it for a first pass on tamper mass and drop height, then confirm with a trial.
- Always allow for volume loss at the surface: vibroflotation requires imported granular backfill to make up the volume as the ground densifies.
- Run a field trial with verification testing before committing to a production grid.

## Checks

- Check the verification testing was done at the worst location in the pattern, not adjacent to a probe.
- Check groundwater conditions — dynamic compaction near or below the water table in silty soils can generate pore pressures that undo the improvement.
- Check vibration monitoring results against the limits agreed for adjacent structures and services.

## Common errors

- Assuming a uniform improvement across the treated block instead of testing between probes.
- Applying vibroflotation to a silty sand outside the suitable grading zone and then being unable to reach the target density.
- Testing immediately after treatment, before pore pressures dissipate and ageing gains develop.

## Caveats

- Textbook learning material, not a design standard — verify method currency and all values against the source and Australian practice before use
- Probe power ratings, effective radii, suitability grading zones and dynamic compaction energy relationships are given in the source's tables and charts — read the numbers there.
- Ground vibration limits for adjacent structures are set by local guidance and standards, not by the textbook.
