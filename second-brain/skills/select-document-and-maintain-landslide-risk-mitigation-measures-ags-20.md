# Select, document and maintain landslide risk mitigation measures (AGS 2007)

> **Source:** `42_1_4_amend.pdf` (AGS Practice Note 2007, Sections 9-10, Appendices D and G), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A mitigation plan that brings landslide risk to tolerable levels, with design life, maintenance regime, responsibilities and reporting that satisfy AGS 2007 minimum standards and survive regulator scrutiny.

## Inputs

- Completed risk estimation with hazards exceeding tolerable criteria identified
- Development proposal and constraints; regulator approval conditions
- Applicable design-life expectations (e.g. AS 2870, AS 4678, AS 3600 ranges — Section 9.3.1)

## Procedure

1. Identify feasible options per hazard and quantify the residual risk of each: accept (only if tolerable), avoid (relocate/redesign/abandon), reduce frequency (re-profile, surface and subsurface drainage, retaining/anchored structures), reduce consequence (defensive works such as catch fences, relocation), manage (monitoring and warning systems with workable response time), transfer (insurance/other authority), or postpone pending better data (Section 9.1.1).
2. Prefer engineered, robust options that reduce uncertainty; apply ALARP within the tolerable band and adopt cheap risk reductions even below tolerable where practicable (Section 9.1.2).
3. Detail the adopted measures in a mitigation plan: stakeholder responsibilities, costs, programme, inspection regime, performance measures, and an emergency plan with defined trigger levels and actions where monitoring is relied on (Section 9.1.3).
4. Specify site-specific development conditions split across detailed design, construction (sequencing, temporary works) and maintenance stages, sufficient to allow later certification (Section 9.2).
5. State design life on the drawings; where a component's effective performance life (e.g. timber crib walls, subsoil drains, stressed anchors) is shorter, prescribe an inspection/repair/replacement regime to bridge the gap (Section 9.3).
6. Establish the maintenance plan and make it durable — register mitigation/maintenance obligations on the land title so future owners inherit them (Sections 9.1.3, 9.4.3).
7. Report to the Section 10 standard: data sources, methods and limitations, scaled plan and sections, factual logs, past-performance evidence, hazard-by-hazard likelihoods, and transparent logic from data to conclusion; use the Appendix D forms where the regulator requires them.
8. Monitor and review risk during option assessment, implementation and ongoing operation; reassess when new data emerges (Section 9.1.4).

## Decision rules

- Risk cannot be removed, only reduced — if no option reaches tolerable levels, development should not proceed or must be radically conditioned.
- Monitoring/warning counts as consequence reduction only when the warning-to-action time is realistically sufficient.
- Postponement means the risk is being temporarily accepted — permissible only where further investigation will genuinely improve the assessment.
- Owner (and successors) hold responsibility for maintaining mitigation measures; the practitioner confirms approval conditions are fulfilled.

## Checks

- Residual risk after mitigation recalculated and shown to meet the criteria — not asserted.
- Design life stated on drawings; shortfall components covered by an explicit maintenance regime.
- Mitigation plan reviewed for workability; land-title annotation or equivalent enforcement mechanism in place.

## Common errors

- Recommending measures without recomputing residual risk.
- Emergency/monitoring plans with warning times too short for evacuation or response.
- Maintenance obligations that die with the first owner because nothing was registered on title.
- Treating LRM as a documentation exercise rather than designed, constructed and maintained works.

## Caveats

- Design-life figures quoted are pointers to Section 9.3.1 and the underlying codes — verify current code editions.
- Regulator-specific policies may impose stricter conditions than the Practice Note.

## Standards cited

AS 4678 (earth retaining structures), AS 2870 (residential slabs and footings), AS 3600 (concrete), AGS 2007e GeoGuides (maintenance and record keeping)
