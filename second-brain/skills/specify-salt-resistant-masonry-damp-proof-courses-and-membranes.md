# Specify salt-resistant masonry, damp-proof courses and membranes

> **Source:** `building-in-saline-environment-080145.pdf` (pp. 9-15 (Bricks Resistant to Salt and Water; Preventing Water and Salt Moving into the Building; Damp Proof Membranes; Building Codes)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Masonry and moisture-barrier specifications (brick class, mortar, DPC height and continuity, under-slab membrane) that meet BCA/AS requirements for saline sites and avoid the common DPC failure modes.

## Inputs

- Site salinity risk assessment (existing or potential)
- Building type and floor system (slab-on-ground vs suspended)
- Local council building policy requirements
- BCA and relevant Australian Standards

## Procedure

1. Specify exposure-class bricks where salt attack is possible: BCA lists them as suitable for severe local conditions including below the DPC in salt-affected ground; classification follows AS 3700 via the AS 4456.1 salt-attack test (bricks surviving 40 wet/dry salt cycles with less than 0.4 g mass loss, or demonstrated saline-environment history). Within a brick style, exposure-grade often costs no more than general purpose.
2. Consider low initial salt content products using the AS 4456.6 efflorescence-potential test as an extra precaution (not a BCA requirement).
3. Use the BCA-specified mortar mix for exposure-class brickwork and do not use raked mortar joints with exposure-class bricks.
4. Detail the DPC as a continuous barrier: nominal 150 mm above finished ground, reducible to 100 mm on sandy well-drained ground or 50 mm where adjoining paving/concrete slopes away under a verandah.
5. Guard against DPC failure modes: chemical breakdown, cracking/puncture at installation, breakage by differential settlement, incorrect installation, and bridging (render or pointing carried over the DPC, raised garden beds, paving, mortar droppings in the cavity, later renovations such as replacing a timber floor with sand and slab without a separating barrier).
6. For slab-on-ground in NSW/SA, specify a high-impact-resistant 0.2 mm polyethylene damp-proof membrane continuous under the slab and wrapped around footings to ground level (NSW BCA state variation since May 2004); some councils also require/recommend ~50 mm of sand under the membrane against puncture and capillary rise.
7. For salt-prone areas consider pier-and-beam construction: less material in ground contact, ventilation and evaporation occur in the soil, damage is inspectable, less cut and fill (minimum 400 mm subfloor clearance where termite inspection applies).

## Decision rules

- If salinity status is unknown, exposure-grade bricks and a damp-proof (not vapour) membrane are cheap insurance relative to repair cost — Australia-wide, most urban sites have some water source capable of mobilising salts.
- Existing NSW council policies in saline areas typically add: 50 mm sand under slab, 32 MPa concrete, seven days curing, plus the high-impact membrane — check the local policy since councils may exceed but not relax the BCA.
- Distinguish damp-proof membranes (high impact resistance) from vapour barriers (low impact resistance): both look identical laid face-down, so specify and verify the product, not the colour.

## Checks

- DPC continuity verified around the full perimeter and above finished ground/paving at the specified height.
- Membrane laps, penetration sealing and extension to ground level inspected before the pour (it is hidden early in construction).
- Landscaping plans checked for future DPC bridging (garden beds, paths against walls).

## Common errors

- Render, pointing or paving bridging the DPC after handover, defeating an otherwise sound barrier.
- Substituting a low-impact vapour barrier where a damp-proof membrane is required.
- Using general-purpose bricks below the DPC on a saline site when exposure-grade was available at the same price.
- Assuming extra subfloor vents can fix salt damage driven by high groundwater (barriers and salt-resistant materials are usually needed instead).

## Caveats

- Classification and aggressivity calls must be verified against the source document, current BCA/NCC editions and the project spec — this booklet is 2008 and code clause numbers have since changed.
- Membrane requirements are state-specific (NSW/SA variation described); confirm the current jurisdictional position.
- Membrane detailing requirements merged from INDUSTRY_GUIDE_T56_Residential_Slabs_and_Footings_in_Saline_Environments.pdf Section 6.6 (20 mm sand layer minimum, 200 mm laps, sealed penetrations, extend to ground level) — note the sand layer figure differs between sources (20 mm in T56 vs 50 mm in NSW council policies).

## Standards cited

Building Code of Australia Vol 2 (damp/weatherproofing, NSW state variation on damp-proof membranes), AS 3700 Masonry Structures; AS 4456.1 (salt attack resistance test); AS 4456.6 (efflorescence potential), AS 2870 Residential Slabs and Footings (membrane, DPC detailing clauses)
