# Finalise logs and manage sample custody

> **Source:** `guideline-geotechnical.pdf` (Sections 5-6), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Reviewed, finalised borehole logs and a defensible sample retention/chain-of-custody position through construction claims windows.

## Inputs

- Draft field logs and adjacent-hole comparisons
- Outstanding laboratory results schedule
- Core photography setup
- Retention policy timelines

## Procedure

1. After drafting, sanity-check layer boundaries against the evolving geological model by laying out adjacent holes and drafting sections.
2. Route PRELIMINARY logs through senior engineering-geologist review; issue FINAL logs only after corrections and all lab results are in; only finalised logs go into the reviewed geotechnical report.
3. Deliver rock core to the core store as a report deliverable; photograph all core in high resolution and colour-correct light as the permanent record.
4. Apply retention rules: untested soil samples held for a minimum period after report completion; treat undisturbed samples as time-limited for testing; retain core until construction completion and claims risk has passed, unless early disposal is formally approved.

## Decision rules

- Untested soil samples retained at least 90 days after the geotechnical report (guideline section 6.1).
- Undisturbed tube samples degrade and are generally not useful for testing after about 3-6 months - schedule testing accordingly (guideline section 6.1).
- Core disposal before construction completion requires director-level approval and a low claims-risk determination; photography is mandatory in all cases (guideline section 6.2).

## Checks

- No FINAL log issued with outstanding laboratory results.
- Sample ownership/custody understood (samples become the department's property until disposal or repossession).

## Common errors

- Issuing preliminary logs into design reports.
- Letting U-tubes sit past their testable life.
- Disposing of core mid-project without approval or photographs.

## Caveats

- TMR policy - other clients' retention rules differ; contract requirements override.
- Verify currency of the guideline edition.

## Standards cited

AS 1726:2017
