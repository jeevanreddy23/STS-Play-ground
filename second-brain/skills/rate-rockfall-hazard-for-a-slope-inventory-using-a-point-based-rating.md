# Rate rockfall hazard for a slope inventory using a point-based rating system

> **Source:** [YouTube — How to Perform Rockfall Hazard Assessment — Step by Step](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1i5D-RdYsR0&t=0s) (00:00-14:55). A YouTube lecture — learning
> material, not an approved design procedure. Equations and numbers were
> heard through auto-captions; read them off the video before use, and
> verify anything design-relevant against the standard and the project
> specification. Engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A summed hazard rating for each inspected slope that ranks it for remediation priority and sets the re-inspection or mitigation response

## Inputs

- Inventory of slopes with rockfall potential from regular site surveys
- Slope height
- Ditch/catchment condition and effectiveness
- Roadway width
- Geologic character: discontinuity orientation, persistence, joint surface friction, presence of clay infill (or, if unavailable, degree of erosion)
- Typical block size or potential rockfall volume
- Climate: precipitation and freezing periods
- Rockfall history at the site
- Traffic data and speed limit for the exposure ratings (see companion skill)

## Procedure

1. Inspect every slope in the inventory area that could produce rockfall in the near future
2. Score each factor from the rating table, where points escalate steeply between categories (low points = benign, high points = adverse)
3. For slope height beyond the table maximum, extrapolate the height-points trend but cap at the maximum allowed points
4. Judge ditch effectiveness from field evidence: loose rock retained in the catchment with a clear road indicates an effective ditch (low points); limited or no catchment scores high points
5. Score geologic character from discontinuity orientation and joint friction; if that information cannot be obtained, score the erosion category instead
6. Estimate average block size by walking the ditch and slope; if block sizes vary widely, estimate the potential rockfall volume instead
7. Score climate higher where precipitation is high and where freeze periods drive water-ice cycling in joints
8. Score history from observed frequency of past falls (few falls = low points, many or constant falls = high points)
9. Add the traffic exposure ratings (average vehicle risk and percent decision sight distance)
10. Sum all factor points to obtain the slope rating

## Decision rules

- Total below about 300 points: low priority, re-inspect on the normal cycle
- Total of about 500 points or more: act immediately with remedial works
- Totals between the thresholds: apply preventive measures if budget allows, otherwise increase monitoring/inspection frequency
- When a description sits between two category scores, use engineering judgment and document the reasoning

## Checks

- Confirm every factor received a score and that extrapolated scores respect the table caps
- Cross-check subjective scores (geology, history, ditch) against the written site description
- Verify the same rating system and category boundaries are used consistently across the whole inventory so rankings are comparable

## Common errors

- Treating the rating as objective; several categories (geologic character, history, ditch effectiveness) are judgment calls and should be justified
- Ignoring absence of clay-infill information: infill presence is the key difference between adjacent geology categories
- Failing to re-inspect slopes near the action threshold more frequently

## Caveats

- YouTube lecture — learning material, not an approved design procedure; verify against project specification and current standards
- Exact point values (3/9/27/81 style scales) and thresholds are read from the lecturer's table — read the table from the video before use
- Lecturer stresses the scoring is partly subjective and improves with field experience
