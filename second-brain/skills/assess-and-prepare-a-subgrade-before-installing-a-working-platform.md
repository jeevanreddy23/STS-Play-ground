# Assess and prepare a subgrade before installing a working platform

> **Source:** `Working Platforms for tracked plant.pdf` (Chapter 2 (Sections 2.1-2.3, Boxes 1-3); Section 1.3), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A characterised, inspected and prepared subgrade with soft spots, hard spots, voids and drainage issues resolved, so the platform design assumptions are valid on the ground actually built on.

## Inputs

- Site investigation data targeting the upper ~2 m of ground (most SI targets deeper strata and may not cover this)
- Historical records: previous site use, demolition records, service plans, local knowledge
- Design statement of assumed characteristic subgrade strength and acceptable variability
- Current drainage and groundwater conditions at time of construction

## Procedure

1. Carry out a thorough whole-site appraisal early, including walkover inspection; specifically hunt for weak/variable areas and drainage problems (Section 2.1).
2. Augment the main-works site investigation where near-surface strength has not been adequately characterised for the platform; use trial pits with careful reinstatement.
3. Identify localised weak zones (soft spots, poorly backfilled excavations, old trenches/tunnels) and hard spots (old foundations, basements) — both are hazardous; examine the whole platform footprint.
4. Excavate localised weak zones, old foundations and abandoned services; backfill with suitable, adequately compacted material. Consider zoning the site by bearing resistance or setting exclusion distances from pits/trenches (Section 2.3).
5. Treat natural or man-made voids (cavities, shafts, wells, culverts, basements) by backfilling or grouting, or physically isolate and mark them.
6. Consider ground treatment (e.g. stabilisation) where the subgrade is very soft or loose; note sulfate-heave risk with lime stabilisation.
7. Protect the exposed subgrade from deterioration — drain it, minimise exposure to wet/frost weather, and minimise the time between exposure and platform placement; remove and replace any deteriorated material.
8. Have a competent person inspect and record the subgrade immediately before placing platform material, to verify design assumptions; also review any late changes to platform level from cut/fill against the design.

## Decision rules

- If actual subgrade strength or variability differs from the design's stated characteristic values, refer back to the platform designer before proceeding.
- Where a surface crust or vegetated layer contributes strength and is retained, inform the permanent-works designer so later removal can be arranged.
- Where voids cannot practicably be filled, mark and barrier them off and maintain the exclusion for the works duration.

## Checks

- Subgrade inspection record signed off by a competent person immediately prior to platform placement.
- Weak zones and hard spots identified relative to the design's stated strength assumptions (Box 3).
- Drainage installed/verified before and during platform life; risk assessments lodged in the health and safety file including what underlies the platform.

## Common errors

- Relying on a deep-focused site investigation that never characterised the top couple of metres.
- Trial pits or other excavations reinstated poorly, creating the very soft spots the process is meant to remove.
- Ignoring hard spots because attention is on soft ground — differential support also destabilises rigs.
- Leaving the stripped subgrade exposed to weather for long periods before platform placement.

## Caveats

- OCR noise in the source scan — verify wording and any thresholds against the original BR 470 before relying on them.
- UK-centric legislative references; map responsibilities to local WHS legislation (e.g. Australian jurisdictions) before use.

## Standards cited
