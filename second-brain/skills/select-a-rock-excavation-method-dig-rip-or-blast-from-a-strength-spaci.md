# Select a rock excavation method (dig, rip or blast) from a strength-spacing chart

> **Source:** [YouTube — How to Choose Rock Excavation Methods — Digging, Ripping or Blasting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9W1vB1dhc5w&t=0s) (00:00-04:34). A YouTube lecture — learning

**Outcome.** The most economical open-excavation method for a rock mass, chosen from point load strength and discontinuity spacing

## Inputs

- Unconfined compressive strength (or point load index directly)
- Discontinuity frequency from field mapping (joints per metre)
- Excavation assessment chart with digging / ripping / blasting zones plotted on point load index vs discontinuity spacing axes

## Procedure

1. Map discontinuities on site and count joints per metre; compute average spacing as the reciprocal (example: 8 joints per metre gives 0.125 m)
2. Convert UCS to point load index using the general correlation UCS = 24 times point load index unless a rock-specific correlation exists (example: 60 MPa / 24 = 2.5 MPa)
3. Plot the point (point load index, spacing) on the chart
4. Read the zone the point falls in: digging (cheapest), ripping (intermediate cost and plant), or blasting (hardest, most technical and costly); zone labels also guide equipment choice (example fell in easy ripping)

## Decision rules

- Prefer a site- or rock-specific UCS to point-load correlation over the general factor of 24 when one has been established
- Method cost and complexity escalate from digging to ripping to blasting — the chart zone drives plant selection and project cost planning

## Checks

- Verify axis scales (both often logarithmic) before plotting
- Confirm the spacing used is an average from mapping, not a single measurement

## Common errors

- Plotting UCS directly on a point load index axis without converting
- Inverting the spacing (using joints per metre instead of metres per joint)

## Caveats

- YouTube lecture — learning material, not an approved design procedure; verify against project specification and current standards
- The chart and its zone boundaries are shown on screen — read from the video
