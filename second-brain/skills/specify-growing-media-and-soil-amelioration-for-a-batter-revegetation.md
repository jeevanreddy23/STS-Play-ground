# Specify growing media and soil amelioration for a batter revegetation

> **Source:** `guideline-for-batter-surface-stabilisation-using-vegetation.pdf` (Section 3.3 growing media and Flowchart 3.3 (pp. 12-13), Section 3.4 soil and growing media testing (p. 14), Section 3.5 soil amelioration including Table 3.5.1a ameliorants, 3.5.2 in-situ, 3.5.3 during stripping, 3.5.4 in stockpile, 3.5.5 post-establishment (pp. 14-16), Section 3.13 preparation (pp. 19-20), Table 3.15 quality assurance issues (pp. 22-23). **Outcome.** A growing medium on the batter face that will actually germinate and hold vegetation, backed by soil test results, an amelioration prescription from a soil scientist, and a stripping and placement method that does not destroy the material before it gets there.

## Inputs

- Soil test results and a written laboratory report on the site-won or imported material, covering both topsoil and subsoil
- Sampling regime designed by an independent soil scientist, with sample depth extending to the intended rooting depth
- Identification of the growing media available: site-won topsoil, subsoil, compost, imported topsoil or manufactured products
- Batter gradient (drives what plant can incorporate ameliorants and how deep topsoil can be placed)
- Known constraints on the source material: weed seed bank, deleterious material, dispersive or sodic behaviour
- Target vegetation, cover crop and permanent species mix

## Procedure

1. Sample and test before designing anything. Collect enough samples to cover the different soil types on or proposed for the slope, and to the depth roots will actually reach — sampling only topsoil misses the subsoil the roots will occupy.
2. Obtain a written report from an appropriate soil laboratory setting out the material's limitations and its suitability for revegetation.
3. Have an independent soil scientist prescribe the ameliorants and rates. There is no single formula; the prescription can vary between stages of the same project.
4. Plan amelioration at the earliest feasible stage. Applying ameliorants before stripping, so they are incorporated by the stripping operation itself, is the most effective point; stockpile treatment is next; in-situ treatment on the finished batter is the hardest and least reliable.
5. Where stripping topsoil: write a stripping plan giving depths and limitations, supervise the stripping depth in the field, avoid stripping in excessively wet or dry conditions, keep horizons contaminated with weed seed or deleterious material out of the reusable stockpile, avoid compacting stockpiles, and revegetate the stockpile promptly to keep the soil biota alive.
6. For in-situ amelioration, match the method to the gradient: on slopes no steeper than about 3h:1v, broadcast spreaders plus ripper tynes or scarifiers on tractor, dozer or grader can incorporate cost-effectively along the contour; on steeper slopes, incorporation is limited to excavator bucket teeth dragged across the face.
7. Carry out all ripping, scarifying and roughening along the contour. Vertical rutting concentrates flow and increases erosion; a batter scarified vertically will rill.
8. Place the growing medium at the thickness the gradient allows and do not compact it after placement.
9. Roughen or scarify compacted and smooth surfaces before applying any technique that has to key into the face, so the product and the seed stay on the slope.
10. If no site topsoil is available or usable, import a growing medium or a manufactured product, and establish its quality and compliance before it comes to site.

## Decision rules

- Soils cannot be managed or ameliorated without test data. Revegetation programs have failed on road projects specifically for want of testing and amelioration.
- Ameliorants must be physically mixed into the soil to work. Ameliorants surface-applied and then hydromulched over are liable to wash off.
- Do not compact the subsoil or the growing medium after preparation or placement. Compacting the subsoil breaks the bond between growing medium and slope; compacting the growing medium prevents germination and root penetration.
- Conventional earthmoving plant can only work safely along the contour on slopes no steeper than about 3h:1v; a dozer can work perpendicular to contours between roughly 3h:1v and 1h:1v only in ideal soil conditions, and ripping in that direction is inappropriate regardless.
- Exercise care deep ripping sodic, dispersive or magnesic soils. Water ponding in rip lines can start tunnel erosion and cascade into gully failure down the slope.
- All growing media, site-won or imported, must comply with the applicable soil, mulch and soil conditioner standards.
- Expect repeat applications of ameliorants over time, and budget for them; reapplying over a partly vegetated batter is difficult and is routinely left out of budgets.

## Checks

- Confirm the required tests were done, at the right sample sizes, from the stated origin locations, and that the reports were received.
- Confirm the ameliorants actually used match the ones recommended, and that they were incorporated rather than surface-dumped.
- Observe growing medium placement for compaction, material consistency, depth, uniform thickness and coverage against the specification.
- Confirm scarification and ripping ran along the contour, and that no vertical rutting was left on the face.
- Confirm the timing gap between soil preparation and application of the stabilisation treatment matches what the technique requires.

## Common errors

- Sampling topsoil only and never characterising the subsoil the roots will grow into.
- Stripping topsoil in wet conditions, or stockpiling it deep and compacted so it goes anaerobic and the biota die.
- Mixing weed-seeded horizons into the reusable stockpile and re-infesting the batter.
- Applying ameliorants to the finished batter face at a gradient where nothing can incorporate them.
- Scarifying or ripping up and down the slope, creating preferential flow paths.
- Track-rolling or otherwise compacting the growing medium after placement.
- Deep ripping dispersive soil and triggering tunnel erosion in the rip lines.

## Caveats

- Ameliorant selection and rates are a soil scientist's call against the site's own test data; the guideline gives ameliorant types against target deficiencies, not project rates.
- Surface stabilisation only — this does not address the geotechnical stability of the batter.
- Guideline is NSW road-project specific; check current Transport for NSW specifications for material compliance requirements.
