# Describe soil colour with Munsell notation when logging

> **Source:** `Munsell Color Chart.pdf` (Hue pages 1-6 of the PDF: 10R, 2.5YR, 5YR, 7.5YR, 10YR, 2.5Y (2009 revision / 2013 production). Pages 7-8 are blank. Chip values and names are read from the chart itself and are not reproduced here.), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A defensible, repeatable soil colour recorded on a borehole or test pit log as hue, value and chroma with the matching book name, so that colour can be compared between holes, between loggers and between jobs.

## Inputs

- Munsell soil colour book covering the hue pages relevant to the material (this copy holds 10R, 2.5YR, 5YR, 7.5YR, 10YR and 2.5Y)
- A freshly broken or freshly cut face of the sample, not a smeared or trowel-polished one
- Moisture state of the specimen at the moment of matching (natural, wetted, or air-dried)
- Daylight or a known daylight-equivalent light source
- Sample identifier and depth so the colour attaches to a defined interval

## Procedure

1. Break the sample to expose a fresh face rather than reading a weathered, smeared or handled surface.
2. Record and hold the moisture state constant across the hole. State on the log whether colours were matched moist or dry; a soil read moist typically reads a step or more darker in value than the same soil dry.
3. Work in diffuse daylight with the sun behind the shoulder. Avoid direct low sun, tinted shade cloth, sodium or warm LED site lighting, and coloured clothing near the sample.
4. Hold the sample behind the aperture holes on the chart page so the sample and the chips are seen together against the same background.
5. Select the hue page first (the page label, e.g. 10YR), then move down the page for value and across for chroma until the chip nearest the sample is found.
6. Record the notation as hue value/chroma (for example 10YR 5/3) and copy the book's own name for that chip alongside it.
7. Where the sample falls between two chips, record the closer chip and note that it is intermediate rather than inventing a value between chips.
8. Log mottling separately: give the matrix colour first, then each mottle colour with its abundance, size and contrast, rather than averaging them into one colour.
9. Note any colour that changes on exposure (for example greying reduced material that oxidises within minutes) and record the colour at a stated time after exposure.

## Decision rules

- Colour is recorded as an observation, not an interpretation. Do not substitute an interpretive term (for example 'oxidised', 'weathered') for the notation.
- One moisture convention per project. Mixing moist and dry readings within a set of logs destroys comparability.
- If the material is mottled, banded or has a coating differing from the matrix, describe each component; a single colour is only appropriate for a genuinely uniform material.
- If the correct hue page is missing from the book in use, record which pages were available rather than forcing a match onto a neighbouring hue.
- Very dark and very low-chroma materials (organic soils, reduced clays) commonly need the low-value rows; do not default to 'black' without matching.

## Checks

- Confirm the notation quoted on the log actually exists on the page used (hue, value and chroma are all page-specific).
- Confirm the moisture convention is stated once in the log legend or methodology.
- Cross-check colours from adjacent holes at the same stratum; an unexplained hue jump usually means lighting, moisture state or a smeared face rather than a real change.
- Confirm the colour book is clean and not faded or oil-stained; worn chips read low in chroma.

## Common errors

- Matching under artificial site light or in strong late-afternoon sun, which shifts hue.
- Reading a trowel-smeared or handled surface instead of a fresh break.
- Recording moist and dry colours in the same set of logs with no note of which is which.
- Averaging a mottled soil into one colour and losing the drainage evidence the mottling carries.
- Writing the chip name only ('brown') without the notation, which is not reproducible.
- Quoting a notation that is not a real chip on the page (interpolating between chips).

## Caveats

- This is a method pointer only. The chip notations and their names live in the chart; read the colour from the book, do not recall it.
- This local copy carries six hue pages. A full soil colour book includes further hues (5Y, gley pages and others) that are not present here, so strongly reduced or greenish-grey materials may not be matchable from this copy.
- Colour matching by eye varies between observers. Where colour drives a decision (for example acid sulfate screening or a contamination boundary), corroborate with a test rather than colour alone.
