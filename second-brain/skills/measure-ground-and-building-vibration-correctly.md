# Measure ground and building vibration correctly

> **Source:** `vibrationguide0643.pdf` (Sections 4.2-4.5), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Vibration measurements whose transducer mounting, dynamic range and location make them valid for assessment against criteria.

## Inputs

- Triaxial accelerometers or geophones with conditioning and recording system
- Access constraints (indoor vs outdoor measurement)
- Surface types at measurement points (rock/concrete, firm soil, soft ground, floors)

## Procedure

1. Measure where the vibration affects people (inside buildings) where practical; otherwise measure externally and convert with a justified, ideally test-derived, transfer function; meeting criteria in external ground is an acceptable screen for meeting them indoors, subject to resonance caution.
2. Mount transducers per the accelerometer-mounting standard: rigid, light mountings; three uniaxial transducers on a rigidly fixed metal cube in preference to brackets; bolts/epoxy on rock, asphalt or concrete.
3. On soils, use a stiff star-stake driven through loose surface material (projecting only a few mm), burial to about three times the unit dimension in soft ground, or a well-bedded surface plate; keep clear of structures and large trees to avoid feedback.
4. Verify instrumentation performance and dynamic range, and correct or reject data with inadequate signal-to-noise margin over background.
5. Avoid measuring on soft floor coverings; if unavoidable, run comparative mass/coupling checks.

## Decision rules

- Transducer plus monitoring unit mass must not exceed 10% of the building element's mass (vibrationguide0643 Section 4.4).
- Never use double-sided foam tape for mounting (Section 4.4).
- Signal-to-noise ratio generally at least 5 dB; between 5 and 10 dB the measured value must be corrected downward and the method reported; dynamic range 40 dB adequate, 50 dB preferred (Section 4.3).
- In multi-storey buildings, external-source vibration tends to reduce with height, but floor/wall resonance can amplify indoor values - assess case-by-case (Sections 4.2, 2.2).

## Checks

- Transducer orientation per manufacturer, aligned to source direction where relevant.
- Instrumentation meets the referenced BS performance requirements.
- Background vibration defined and quantified.

## Common errors

- Foam-tape or magnet mounting on rough surfaces giving high-frequency artefacts.
- Heavy geophones loading light building elements.
- Measuring on carpet and reporting distorted spectra.

## Caveats

- Guideline is from 2006; instrument standards have since been revised - verify current editions.
- Consent conditions may prescribe different measurement locations or descriptors and override.

## Standards cited

AS 2775-2004, BS 6841-1987, BS 7482 Parts 1 and 3
