# Determine field dry density by sand replacement, rubber balloon or nuclear gauge

> **Source:** `Principles of Geotechnical Engineering 8th Edition.pdf` (Das 8th ed., Ch. 6, §6.11 (sand cone, rubber balloon, nuclear methods) with §6.10 for the acceptance framework), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** An in-situ dry density and moisture content for a compacted layer, and the relative compaction achieved, with the method's limitations recorded.

## Inputs

- Calibrated sand cone apparatus with uniform dry sand of known bulk density and known cone-fill mass, or a calibrated rubber balloon volumeter, or a calibrated nuclear density/moisture gauge
- Balance, moisture tins and oven (or a rapid moisture method with its own calibration)
- Reference laboratory maximum dry density for the material

## Procedure

1. Sand replacement: level a small area, seat the base plate, excavate a hole of the standard depth, and retain every particle removed; weigh the moist soil and take its moisture content.
2. Invert the sand-filled jar and cone over the hole, open the valve, and let the sand run until flow stops; weigh the apparatus again to get the sand used.
3. Subtract the calibrated mass of sand that fills the cone alone, and divide the remainder by the calibrated dry density of the sand to obtain the hole volume.
4. Compute dry mass of the excavated soil from its moist mass and moisture content, and divide by the hole volume to obtain field dry density.
5. Rubber balloon: follow the same excavation and moisture steps, but read the hole volume directly from the calibrated water-filled volumeter.
6. Nuclear gauge: seat the gauge on a prepared surface (direct transmission with the source rod driven to the required depth for layer testing), take the count, and read wet density and water content; subtract to obtain dry density.
7. Express the result as relative compaction against the laboratory maximum dry density at the specified effort.

## Decision rules

- Use direct transmission, not backscatter, when testing a defined compacted layer — backscatter is surface-weighted.
- Correlate the nuclear gauge against sand replacement (or another primary method) on the actual material at the start of the works, and re-check periodically; nuclear moisture readings in particular are biased by hydrogen in organics, lime or cement.
- Reject a sand-cone test where the hole wall collapses or where coarse particles prevent a clean excavation; the method is unsuited to very coarse or very soft material.
- Where oversize particles are present, apply the specified oversize correction to either the field or the laboratory value — not both.

## Checks

- Check the sand calibration (bulk density and cone mass) is current and was done with the same sand batch.
- Check the test depth corresponds to the layer being accepted, not a shallower zone.
- Check the moisture content used for the dry density came from the excavated material, not a nearby grab.

## Common errors

- Losing fines during excavation, which biases dry density low.
- Using a nuclear gauge moisture reading uncorrected on a lime- or cement-stabilised material.
- Testing before the layer has been fully rolled, or testing only in easily compacted locations.

## Caveats

- Textbook learning material, not a design standard — verify method currency and all values against the source and Australian practice before use
- Australian practice uses AS 1289.5.3.1 (sand replacement) and AS 1289.5.8.1 (nuclear); test method selection is often mandated by the earthworks specification based on oversize content.
