# Run the slake durability test through two cycles and record the masses for the durability index

> **Source:** [YouTube — Slake Durability Test: Procedure, Measurements, Calculations, Classification](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x86l8_gHlhQ&t=0s) (00:00-11:46). A YouTube lecture — learning
> material, not an approved design procedure. Equations and numbers were
> heard through auto-captions; read them off the video before use, and
> verify anything design-relevant against the standard and the project
> specification. Engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A complete set of drum-and-sample mass records over two wet-dry abrasion cycles, ready for computing the second-cycle slake durability index used for durability classification

## Inputs

- About 450-500 g of rock in roughly ten lump pieces of 40-60 g each, not too angular
- Slake durability drum, motorised trough, tap water, oven, scale

## Procedure

1. Check each lump's mass falls in the 40-60 g band (43 g and 57 g pieces both acceptable in the demo)
2. Weigh the empty drum (842.2 g in the demo)
3. Load the rock and weigh drum plus rock (1285.5 g), then fit and secure the lid
4. Mount the drum in the trough and fill with tap water to about 20 mm below the drum axis
5. Rotate at about 20 revolutions per minute for 10 minutes; fines migrate into the water and it turns muddy
6. Remove the drum, take off the lid, and oven-dry drum and retained rock for about 6 hours
7. Weigh the dry drum plus retained rock — this closes cycle one (1244.6 g in the demo)
8. Repeat the whole wet-rotate-dry-weigh sequence for a second cycle, topping up water first (final dry mass 1237.1 g in the demo)
9. Use the second-cycle retained dry mass data to compute the second-cycle slake durability index and classify the rock's durability

## Decision rules

- The classification is based on the second-cycle index, so the test is not complete after one cycle
- Follow the Franklin and Chandra testing procedure (named by the lecturer) for the setup details

## Checks

- Water level set at the pre-marked height about 20 mm below the drum axis before each cycle
- Drum connections secure before rotation
- All four masses recorded: empty drum, drum+rock initial, drum+rock after cycle 1 drying, drum+rock after cycle 2 drying

## Common errors

- Stopping after the first cycle and classifying from the first-cycle loss
- Using highly angular fragments, which abrade differently from the intended lump shape
- Skipping or shortening the roughly 6-hour drying stage so the retained mass includes moisture

## Caveats

- YouTube lecture — learning material, not an approved design procedure; verify against project specification and current standards
- The index formula itself is not spoken aloud — equation shown on screen — read from the video; it is derived from the retained dry mass ratios of the recorded weighings
- The durability classification bands for the second-cycle index are also shown on screen rather than narrated
