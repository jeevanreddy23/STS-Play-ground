# Present and interpret urban salinity investigation results

> **Source:** `booklet3siteinvestigationsforurbansalinity (2).pdf` (Phase 3, pp. 12-14; Appendix 1 pp. 19-21 (units and conversions)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Results reported with unambiguous units, spatial context (maps, transects, depth profiles) and a process interpretation (recharge vs discharge, through-flow, corrosion risk) that supports management decisions.

## Inputs

- Field and laboratory results with test methods and units
- Site map base (topography, drainage, vegetation)
- Soil profile descriptions per landform unit

## Procedure

1. Tabulate all results with units and any conversion factors explicitly stated (EC vs ECe, dS/m vs mS/m etc.).
2. Prepare a site map showing soil/landform units, drainage lines, all observation and sample locations, legend, scale and north; consider a second map of constraint/management zones.
3. Plot profile transects across the site to build a three-dimensional picture of soil and salt distribution.
4. Graph EC against depth for each profile and read the shape: EC decreasing with depth suggests a discharge site; steady low EC suggests recharge; a high-EC zone at depth can mark a seasonal watertable or low-permeability layer (typical salt-profile shapes after Qld DNR 1997).
5. Cross-check results against field observations (visible scalds should test salty) and against permeability/sodicity/dispersibility interpretations to explain where water and salt concentrate.
6. Convert units where needed using the booklet's Appendix 1: dS/m = mS/cm = mmho/cm; dS/m x 100 = mS/m; dS/m x 1000 = uS/cm (EC units); dS/m x 640 ~ ppm/mg/L total dissolved salts (average factor; varies by salt species from ~400 for MgCl2 to ~970 for NaHCO3); EC1:5 (dS/m) x 0.34 ~ % total soluble salts.

## Decision rules

- If salinity is reported as EC1:5 without texture conversion, results understate ECe by about x14 (sandy loam) to x6 (heavy clay) — always state which quantity is reported.
- dS/m expressed in mS/m is 100x larger numerically; misread scale factors are the classic reporting failure.
- Where the EC-depth profile conflicts with the landform interpretation (e.g. recharge-shaped profile on a mapped discharge site), revisit sampling or the conceptual model before selecting management.

## Checks

- Every table states units and conversion factors used.
- Field-observed salinity outbreaks reconcile with lab data; discrepancies trigger a labelling/reporting audit.
- Interpretation distinguishes ECw (water measurements — snapshot only) from soil ECe.

## Common errors

- Mixing dS/m, mS/m and uS/cm in one report without flags.
- Using the 640 ppm-per-dS/m average without noting it varies with salt species.
- Inferring soil salinity from creek/dam ECw readings.
- Reporting only tables without maps/transects, so the spatial pattern that drives management is lost.

## Caveats

- Classification and aggressivity calls must be verified against the source document and the project spec.
- Conversion factors (texture multipliers, the 640 factor) are averages with acknowledged spread — cite them with the source page rather than treating them as exact.
- Salt-profile shape interpretation is indicative, not diagnostic; corroborate with piezometer data.

## Standards cited

Rayment & Higginson (1992) Australian Laboratory Handbook of Soil and Water Chemical Methods (dS/m convention), Richards (1954) USDA Handbook 60 (ECe salinity classes)
