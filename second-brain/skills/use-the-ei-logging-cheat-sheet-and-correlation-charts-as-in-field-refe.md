# Use the EI logging cheat sheet and correlation charts as in-field reference aids

> **Source:** [Google Drive — Field Sheets (STS blank templates + reference)/EI Geotech Soil Logging Cheat Sheet - rev1 Oct 19.pdf, Approx. Correlations Chart.pdf, PSP correlation.pdf](https://drive.google.com/file/d/13WeSy9SmwQe52-198FqjHYF2kWf4-CMI/view) (Printed field reference aids kept with the blank BH log templates: logging vocabulary/criteria sheet, index-test correlation tables, and a Perth Sand Penetrometer density chart). Reconstructed from STS's own
> field records on Google Drive — completed forms, labelled photos and
> scans, catalogued in [[Field records catalogue]]. It shows what the
> records evidence, not necessarily the current approved procedure —
> confirm against the current STS form and procedure before treating it
> as the standard method.

**Outcome.** Faster, standard-compliant field logging and on-the-spot sanity checks by reaching for the right printed aid instead of memory.

## Inputs

- EI Geotech Soil Logging Cheat Sheet (rev1 Oct 19) - soil and rock-core logging guide sheets
- Approximate Correlations Chart - consistency/density vs SPT N, DCP blows, cone resistance, unit weight and modulus relationships
- PSP correlation chart - Perth Sand Penetrometer blows per 150 mm vs density/vertical effective stress (Stockton beach sand basis)

## Procedure

1. Carry the three aids with the blank log sheets; they are companions to the OP GEO 007/008 logs, not standalone deliverables.
2. Reach for the soil logging cheat sheet when writing descriptions: it gives the description order, granular-vs-cohesive field tests, particle size limits, plasticity/ribbon criteria, consistency and density terms with field guides, moisture condition definitions, soil structure/cementation terms, fill identification cues, weathering and rock-defect abbreviations, defect description order with worked examples, and colour abbreviations.
3. Reach for the approximate correlations chart when converting between field indices - e.g. relating DCP blows or pocket penetrometer readings to consistency terms, SPT N or an indicative modulus - and treat every value as approximate.
4. Reach for the PSP correlation when interpreting Perth Sand Penetrometer counts in clean sands, noting it derives from a specific sand and overburden-stress dependence.
5. When a correlation drives a reported number (e.g. an inferred density index), note on the log that it is correlation-based and which aid was used.

## Decision rules

- The cheat sheet governs wording on the log; if memory and the sheet disagree, the sheet wins.
- Correlations are depth- and moisture-dependent guides (stated on the aids) - never present a correlated value as a measured one.
- PSP correlations apply to sands like the calibration material; outside that, log the raw blows and defer interpretation.

## Checks

- Descriptions on completed logs follow the cheat-sheet order and vocabulary.
- Any correlated parameter in notes or reports is traceable to the specific chart used.

## Common errors

- Quoting correlation-derived strengths without the 'approximate/guide only' caveat the charts themselves carry.
- Using the PSP chart for silty or clayey materials where the correlation basis does not hold.

## Caveats

- Reconstructed from STS field records — confirm against the current STS procedure before treating as the standard method
- This skill is deliberately a pointer: the aids' actual correlation values and criteria must be read from the source documents, not from this summary.
