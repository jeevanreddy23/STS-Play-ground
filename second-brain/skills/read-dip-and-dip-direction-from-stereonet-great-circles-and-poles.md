# Read dip and dip direction from stereonet great circles and poles

> **Source:** [YouTube — Stereonet Quiz — Reading Stereonet Data, Great Circles, Poles, Dips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=naR9iWZjdW0&t=0s) (00:01-11:05). A YouTube lecture — learning
> material, not an approved design procedure. Equations and numbers were
> heard through auto-captions; read them off the video before use, and
> verify anything design-relevant against the standard and the project
> specification. Engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Correct interpretation of discontinuity dip magnitude and dip direction from either great-circle or pole representations on a stereonet

## Inputs

- A stereonet plot showing discontinuities as great circles, poles, or both
- The compass frame of the net: north at top is 0 degrees, east 90, south 180, west 270

## Procedure

1. For dip magnitude with great circles: dip is 90 degrees at the centre of the net and 0 at the perimeter, so the great circle passing closest to the centre represents the steepest plane, and the one furthest from the centre the shallowest
2. For dip magnitude with poles: the rule inverts because a pole plots 90 degrees away from its great circle — the pole furthest from the centre represents the steepest plane, and a pole near the centre a near-horizontal plane
3. For dip direction with a great circle: construct the line perpendicular to the chord joining the great circle's ends, passing through the circle's crest, and extend it to the perimeter; the azimuth where it meets the perimeter is the dip direction
4. For dip direction with a pole: draw a line from the pole through the centre of the net and extend to the far perimeter; the azimuth at the crossing point is the dip direction

## Decision rules

- Great circle near centre = steep; pole near centre = shallow — always state which representation you are reading before applying the closeness rule
- Two poles at the same azimuth but different radii represent planes with the same dip direction but different dips

## Checks

- Convert between representations as a self-check: the pole and its great circle must give the same dip and dip direction
- Confirm the answer against the perimeter azimuth graduations (0/90/180/270)

## Common errors

- Applying the great-circle closeness rule to poles, concluding a pole near the centre is a steep plane
- Reading dip direction from the pole's own azimuth instead of projecting the line through the centre to the opposite side of the net

## Caveats

- YouTube lecture — learning material, not an approved design procedure; verify against project specification and current standards
- Some quoted azimuth numbers in the auto-captions are garbled (e.g. a value read as both 230 and 330 degrees in the same exercise) — read exact values from the video frames
