# Plan and interpret a seismic geophysical survey for site investigation

> **Source:** [YouTube — Seismic Geophysics Methods in Geotechnical Investigations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c0B-XU8NESY&t=0s) (00:01-07:15). A YouTube lecture — learning
> material, not an approved design procedure. Equations and numbers were
> heard through auto-captions; read them off the video before use, and
> verify anything design-relevant against the standard and the project
> specification. Engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A cost-effective picture of subsurface layer boundaries over a large area from seismic wave velocities, correctly caveated and tied to borehole control

## Inputs

- Geophone array (spacing typically about 1 to 1.5 m) and a recording device
- An energy source to initiate elastic waves: a large hammer, or a small blast for large rock areas
- At least one borehole log in the survey area for ground-truthing
- An experienced analyst for the velocity data

## Procedure

1. Lay out geophones at roughly 1-1.5 m spacing across the area of interest
2. Initiate elastic waves with the hammer or small blast and record arrival times at each geophone
3. Analyse the recordings to produce a velocity-versus-depth profile; this is the hardest step because of wave noise, and should be done by someone with long experience
4. Interpolate between velocity values to pick boundaries between strata, understanding these picks are assumptions, not observations
5. Assign geology to each velocity layer only by matching against borehole logs from the same area — velocity alone (e.g. 1650 m/s) says nothing about what the material actually is
6. Optionally estimate rock strength from published velocity-strength correlations and the low/medium/high strength rock classification

## Decision rules

- Choose seismic methods when the area is large and budget matters: cheaper than boreholes, SPT or lab programmes, and covers area rather than points
- Never interpret geology from velocities without borehole confirmation; the combination of seismic coverage plus borehole control is presented as the best approach
- Expect the top metre or two to be soil, and treat near-surface velocities with extra caution

## Checks

- Confirm layer boundary picks against borehole logs before reporting
- Sanity-check velocity-derived strength classes against samples taken from the borehole

## Common errors

- Treating interpolated boundaries as measured contacts
- Assigning rock type from velocity alone with no borehole sample
- Underestimating the data-processing effort — noisy records need experienced interpretation

## Caveats

- YouTube lecture — learning material, not an approved design procedure; verify against project specification and current standards
- The transcript's description of the velocity-strength correlation direction is garbled (it reads as low velocity giving high strength at one point and the opposite shortly after) — read the correlation chart from the video before using it
- No penetration or sampling means the method cannot stand alone for design
