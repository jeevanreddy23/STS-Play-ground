# Manage salinity in subdivision and single-lot development design

> **Source:** `INDUSTRY_GUIDE_T56_Residential_Slabs_and_Footings_in_Saline_Environments.pdf` (Section 5 (Site Management, pp. 7-9)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Development-scale measures (drainage, earthworks, landscaping, water management) that keep groundwater and salt away from structures and avoid creating new salinity.

## Inputs

- Site investigation results (existing/potential salinity, recharge-discharge model, watertable depth)
- Development layout options (levels, cut/fill, retaining walls, drainage corridors)
- Landscape/irrigation plans and water sources (grey water, stormwater reuse)

## Procedure

1. For subdivisions: track salt loading when groundwater is intercepted (cut and fill, retaining walls, service trenches divert salt to receiving waters); consider deliberate interception to control watertable height, leach salts, or divert to evaporation basins sized for typical rain events.
2. Set block levels with positive falls to the street so runoff leaves and ponding/waterlogging (recharge) is prevented; use street stormwater or subsoil drains, deepened if needed, to hold the watertable down in flat, low-lying areas.
3. Prefer 'slabscaping' (terraced level blocks formed during subdivision) on sloping ground: it enables slab-on-ground without individual cut/fill and gives estate-wide drainage control.
4. When filling low-lying areas with non-saline material, install a drained drainage layer beneath the fill to stop groundwater rising through it; treat capping layers over saline soil cautiously — they can suppress evaporation, let groundwater rise to the fill underside, and capillary action in clayey capping can lift salt back toward the surface.
5. For single lots: design cut/fill knowing it may expose saline material (upgrade concrete durability accordingly); keep strip footings' greater embedment depth in mind; put membranes behind concrete retaining walls plus proper drainage; place membranes under external paving on saline soils; grade ground so water cannot pond; plant deep-rooted and salt-tolerant vegetation to limit recharge.
6. At the whole-site scale, minimise infiltration and disturbance: water-sensitive design that infiltrates stormwater, unsealed detention ponds, over-irrigation, septic/rubble pits and removal of deep-rooted vegetation all push the watertable up; replace soils in original order after excavation and design utility trenches so they do not become saline groundwater conduits.

## Decision rules

- If an existing salinity problem is identified, design every element for it and aim the subdivision design at improving conditions over time (tree planting, drainage that lowers groundwater and leaches salt).
- If only potential hazard exists, either demonstrate no future problem or build basic durability measures into concrete elements now — guarantees over a 50-year design life are hard to sustain.
- Estate filling over saline areas may be false economy if capillary rise can still deliver salts; putting the money into building design/materials can be more cost-effective.
- Match management to the process zone: recharge areas need infiltration control; discharge/shallow-watertable areas need drainage, salt-proof materials and possibly pier-and-beam construction.

## Checks

- Drainage layers beneath fill are actually drained (not just present).
- Capping-layer proposals assessed for long-term capillary bypass, not just short-term covering.
- Sediment and erosion control plans account for saline and sodic soils.
- Monitoring (piezometers) persists through construction into occupation.

## Common errors

- Detention basins and water features left unlined in recharge areas, feeding the watertable.
- Trench backfill creating preferential saline flow paths to structures.
- Assuming short-term impact assessments hold over the 50-year design life of housing.
- Exposing sodic/saline subsoil in cut without erosion or durability response.

## Caveats

- Classification and aggressivity calls must be verified against the source document and the project spec.
- Management-option lists merged from booklet3siteinvestigationsforurbansalinity (2).pdf Phase 4 (pp. 15-17), which is the secondary source (recharge/discharge/shallow-watertable option sets, pier-and-beam advantages).
- Evaporation basin and drainage designs are concept-level here — hydraulic design requires separate analysis.
