# Screen Hawkesbury Sandstone durability with sodium sulphate soundness before exposed or marine use

> **Source:** `substanceAndMassPropertiesForTheDesignOfEngineeringStructuresInTheHawkesburySandstone.pdf` (Sections 2.5 (durability, Tables 7 and grading; pp 8-9) and 2.6 (creep; pp 9-10)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A durability grading of sandstone for breakwater, cladding or dimension-stone applications based on sodium sulphate soundness loss, cross-checked against wet/dry strength ratio and expansive clay content.

## Inputs

- Sodium sulphate soundness test results (e.g. 15 cycles in 14% solution per AS/NZS 4456.10)
- Wet and dry UCS for the same stone (wet/dry ratio)
- Methylene blue adsorption / expansive clay mineral content where available
- Intended exposure (marine, aggressive, domestic, restoration)

## Procedure

1. Run the sodium sulphate soundness test as the primary index of physico-chemical breakdown resistance; the Maritime Services Board breakwater study found it the most useful discriminator.
2. Grade dimension stone per Spry (2000): A grade under 1% loss (restoration, thin cladding, aggressive environments), B grade 1-4% (high-grade construction), C grade 5-9% (domestic), D grade over 10% (less aggressive, less critical uses).
3. Cross-check with the wet/dry strength ratio, which correlates with sulphate loss, and with methylene blue adsorption as an index of expansive clay content.
4. Note that the ISRM slake durability test proved insufficiently severe to discriminate between these sandstones; do not rely on it alone.
5. For sustained high load ratios, consider creep and static-fatigue behaviour: strength decreases with log time-to-failure, and model footings loaded at 85-90% of ultimate showed time-dependent failures.

## Decision rules

- Low saturated strength combined with high sulphate loss flags unsatisfactory field performance (the exposed Botany Bay case: ~6 MPa saturated UCS with very high loss failed in service).
- Porosity strongly influences dimension-stone performance; measure it under standard conditions (ASTM C97 approach).

## Checks

- Test method and cycle count recorded with every soundness result.
- Grading matched to actual exposure aggressiveness.

## Common errors

- Substituting slake durability for sulphate soundness in these sandstones.
- Grading on dry strength while the service condition is saturated.
- Ignoring long-term creep at high sustained stress ratios.

## Caveats

- Grading thresholds come from a dimension-stone context; adapt with judgement for other applications.
- Design reference only: gradings and thresholds must be verified against the source and reviewed by a qualified engineer before use.

## Standards cited

AS/NZS 4456.10 sodium sulphate soundness, ASTM C97 (porosity/absorption of dimension stone), ISRM slake durability method (noted as insufficiently severe here)
