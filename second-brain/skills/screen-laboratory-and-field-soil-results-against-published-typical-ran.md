# Screen laboratory and field soil results against published typical ranges before design use

> **Source:** `Correlations of Soil Properties.pdf` (Carter & Bentley (1991), whole-book pointer; typical-value tables within each property chapter), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Early detection of implausible test results or data gaps by comparing site values with the typical property ranges per soil class compiled in Carter & Bentley.

## Inputs

- Site laboratory/field results (densities, permeabilities, strengths, compressibility indices, CBR, swelling indices)
- Soil classifications for the tested samples
- Carter & Bentley typical-value tables for the corresponding soil classes

## Procedure

1. Classify each tested sample and identify the corresponding typical range in the book's property chapters (density, permeability/grading, consolidation, shear strength, CBR, shrink/swell).
2. Flag any site result falling well outside the published range for its class and investigate (test error, misclassification, or genuinely unusual material).
3. Use the ranges to fill gaps only for preliminary design or small projects, marking such values as estimates.
4. Where frost susceptibility or swelling potential cannot be measured directly, use the book's indirect correlations as screening indicators only.

## Decision rules

- An out-of-range result is a trigger for investigation, not automatic rejection.
- Never let range-derived estimates govern final design of significant structures without confirmatory testing.

## Checks

- Classification of the sample confirmed before the range comparison.
- Screening outcomes documented in the ground model.

## Common errors

- Comparing results against ranges for the wrong classification system (USCS vs AASHTO tables differ).
- Treating typical ranges as statistical bounds rather than indicative spreads.

## Caveats

- OCR of this excerpt is largely unreadable; chapter identification is from headings only and all content must be taken from the printed book.
- Design reference only: verification against the source and qualified review are required before use.

## Standards cited

USCS and AASHTO classification systems (covered in the book's Chapters 1-2)
