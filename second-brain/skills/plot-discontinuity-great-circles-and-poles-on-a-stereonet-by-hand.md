# Plot discontinuity great circles and poles on a stereonet by hand

> **Source:** [YouTube — How To Draw Stereonet: Great Circles, Dip and Dip Direction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tg9PUGweZkY&t=31s) (00:31-08:36). A YouTube lecture — learning
> material, not an approved design procedure. Equations and numbers were
> heard through auto-captions; read them off the video before use, and
> verify anything design-relevant against the standard and the project
> specification. Engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Hand-drawn great circles (and poles) on tracing paper representing measured dip / dip-direction pairs from a field discontinuity survey

## Inputs

- Dip and dip-direction measurements for each joint set (three-digit number is dip direction, two-digit is dip; e.g. 78/255 and 35/040)
- Printed equal-angle stereonet
- Tracing paper (baking paper works) and a pencil

## Procedure

1. Lay tracing paper over the stereonet and trace the outer circumference in pencil
2. Mark north (0), east (90), south (180) and west (270) on the tracing so the original orientation can always be recovered after rotations
3. For each joint: find the dip-direction value on the perimeter graduations and mark that point on the tracing
4. Rotate the tracing paper until the marked dip-direction point lies on the east-west line — the only straight line on the net, which is what allows dip to be counted and a great circle traced
5. Count the dip angle inward from the perimeter along the east-west line: the perimeter is 0 degrees, the centre is 90 degrees, and each grid line on the net represents 2 degrees
6. At the counted dip position, trace the great circle of the net visible through the tracing paper (interpolate between adjacent printed circles for odd dip values)
7. Rotate the tracing paper back to its original position; the traced arc is the great circle for that discontinuity
8. To plot the pole instead: with the dip-direction point still aligned on the east-west line, count the dip angle from the centre of the net toward the opposite side (90 degrees from the plane) and mark a single point; label it (e.g. P2) and rotate back

## Decision rules

- Three digits in an orientation pair always denote dip direction; the remaining value is dip
- Use a pole (one point) instead of a great circle when a compact representation of many joints is needed

## Checks

- Verify the tracing returned to the original orientation using the marked cardinal points before reading results
- Verify the dip count started at 0 on the perimeter, not at the centre (for great circles); the pole count runs from the centre

## Common errors

- Counting dip from the centre outward when drawing a great circle (that count is only for poles)
- Forgetting to mark the original north, so the tracing cannot be restored after rotation
- Tracing the great circle without first aligning the dip-direction mark with the east-west line

## Caveats

- YouTube lecture — learning material, not an approved design procedure; verify against project specification and current standards
- Software now does this faster; the manual method is for understanding the underlying construction
- Auto-captions garble some counted numbers (e.g. '170' for what should be 270 on the west mark); read graduations from the video
