# Control concrete placement, compaction and curing for durability in saline ground

> **Source:** `INDUSTRY_GUIDE_T56_Residential_Slabs_and_Footings_in_Saline_Environments.pdf` (Sections 6.2.5, 6.4-6.6 (pp. 11-14)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Site concreting practice (water control, compaction method, curing duration, crack control, membrane installation) that delivers the low permeability the durability design assumes.

## Inputs

- Specified concrete grade and covers from the durability design
- Concrete supplied to AS 1379 from a quality-assured supplier
- Immersion vibrator (for beams/footings), curing method (sheeting, curing compound, or water)
- Damp-proofing membrane and sand bedding materials

## Procedure

1. Prohibit uncontrolled water addition on site: added water raises the water-cement ratio, cutting strength and dramatically increasing permeability and shrinkage-cracking tendency.
2. Remove water and debris from pier/footing bases before pouring to avoid locally elevated water-cement ratio and washed-out paste.
3. Compact deliberately in two stages (slump-to-fill, then expulsion of entrapped air): continue vibrating until air bubbles stop surfacing; use immersion vibrators for strip footings and slab beams, but rely on placing/screeding (or a vibrating screed) for 100 mm slabs where an immersion head would damage the membrane. Fresh concrete holds 5-20% entrapped air; ~10% residual air can halve strength.
4. Cure all exposed faces including edges: recommended minimums are 3 days for interior slab surfaces, 7 days for exterior surfaces, and 7 days for slab edges in saline or non-aggressive ground; maintain moisture by formwork retention, plastic sheeting, curing compound or continuous wetting.
5. Control cracking: avoid excess water, compact fully, cure properly, and consider heavier fabric (e.g. SL92 in place of SL72) plus trimmer bars at re-entrant corners and doubled fabric at narrow slab sections to distribute shrinkage cracks.
6. Install the damp-proofing membrane on a minimum 20 mm sand layer, lap joints 200 mm, seal or sleeve penetrations, and extend the membrane to ground level; treat top-surface protection of strip/pad footings as impractical and design the concrete accordingly.

## Decision rules

- Permeability, not strength per se, governs durability — strength is only an indicator; therefore prioritise water-cement ratio, compaction and curing over chasing grade alone.
- In saline ground a weak, poorly cured surface skin defeats the specified cover; if curing was compromised, treat the effective cover as reduced when assessing acceptability.
- Longer water curing keeps reducing paste permeability (immediate drying reaches only ~40% of long-cured strength; 3 days ~60%+, 28 days ~95%) — extend curing where exposure is severe.

## Checks

- Batch dockets confirm no site water addition beyond specification.
- Vibration continued until air release ceased; no honeycombing at stripped faces.
- Curing regime documented per surface, including edges.
- Membrane integrity (laps, penetrations, punctures) inspected immediately before pouring.

## Common errors

- Adding water for workability on hot days, unknowingly raising permeability.
- Stopping vibration when the surface levels (stage 1) instead of when entrapped air is expelled (stage 2).
- Curing the top surface but neglecting edges — often the faces with greatest soil exposure.
- Using an immersion vibrator in a thin slab and puncturing the membrane.

## Caveats

- Classification and aggressivity calls must be verified against the source document and the project specification; curing durations here are recommendations that may be exceeded by spec.
- Percentages quoted for strength vs curing/compaction are from the guide's illustrative figures — cite the figure, do not treat as universal constants.
- Companion requirements in building-in-saline-environment-080145.pdf (NSW council policies: 32 MPa, seven days curing, 50 mm sand) may exceed these minima locally.
