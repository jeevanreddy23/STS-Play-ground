# Describe discontinuities in rock core using standard symbol sets

> **Source:** [YouTube — How to Read and Understand Borehole Logs Part 2: Rock Core, Weathering, Strength, Discontinuity, RQD](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NMNzMLSO91w&t=579s) (09:39-14:25). A YouTube lecture — learning
> material, not an approved design procedure. Equations and numbers were
> heard through auto-captions; read them off the video before use, and
> verify anything design-relevant against the standard and the project
> specification. Engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A complete per-discontinuity description (type, inclination, roughness, aperture, infill/coating) matching the symbol strings found on borehole logs

## Inputs

- Rock core or exposure with visible discontinuities
- The logging scheme's symbol list

## Procedure

1. Record the discontinuity type: bedding, cleavage, joint(s) (J/JS), shear zone, etc.
2. Record depth of the discontinuity and its inclination to horizontal (e.g. bedding at 5 degrees, joint at 30 degrees)
3. Classify roughness in two steps: first the waviness class (stepped, undulating, planar), then the surface texture within it (rough/irregular RO, smooth, or slickensided)
4. Classify aperture: closed/tight is the most favourable for engineering; otherwise open, or filled — and if filled, record the filling thickness (e.g. filled, 8 mm)
5. Describe the surface condition of open/clean joints: clean, stained (often brownish iron discoloration), veneer if the coating is under about 1 mm, coating if thicker than about 1 mm

## Decision rules

- Stain vs veneer vs coating is decided by the thickness of the discoloration/mineral layer (stain = discoloration only, veneer < 1 mm, coating > 1 mm)
- Roughness is always assessed as waviness class first, texture second

## Checks

- Each logged discontinuity carries depth, type, inclination, roughness, aperture, and surface/infill condition — no field left blank
- Filling thickness recorded whenever aperture is 'filled'

## Common errors

- Recording roughness texture without the waviness class (or vice versa)
- Calling a filled joint 'open' without noting the infill material and thickness

## Caveats

- YouTube lecture — learning material, not an approved design procedure; verify against project specification and current standards
- Symbol abbreviations differ between organisations; decode against the specific log's legend
