# Use the Western Sydney salinity potential map in a desk study

> **Source:** `salinity-potential-western-sydney-map-2002.pdf` (Single-sheet map. Legend block, lower right: mapping category descriptions for known salinity, high, moderate, low and very low salinity potential, each with its associated soil landscapes column and landform-geology column. Disclaimers block, bottom of sheet. DIPNR; map dated March 2003, mapping condition August 2002; scale 1:100 000.), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A stated salinity potential category for a site in western Sydney, with the soil landscape and geology that drive it named, the map's limitations recorded, and a decision on whether site-specific salinity investigation is warranted.

## Inputs

- Site location and boundary, and the local government area
- The 2002 Salinity Potential in Western Sydney map (DIPNR, map dated March 2003)
- The companion document 'Guidelines to Accompany Map of Salinity Potential for Western Sydney 2002'
- Site position in the landscape: crest, mid-slope, lower slope, break of slope, foot-slope, floodplain or drainage line
- Soil landscape unit and geology from the relevant soil landscape and geological mapping
- Site observations for salinity indicators if a walkover is possible

## Procedure

1. Locate the site on the map and read its mapping category. The map carries five categories: known salinity, high salinity potential, moderate salinity potential, low salinity potential, and very low salinity potential.
2. Read the category description, not just the colour. Each category on the map is defined by three things together — the mapping category description, the associated soil landscapes, and the landform-geology setting.
3. Cross-check the site's soil landscape unit against the landscapes the map names for that category, since the category is a generalisation over the landscape unit.
4. Cross-check the landform and geology: the map ties known and high salinity to breaks of slope, lower slopes and drainage lines on Wianamatta Group shales, to the geological boundary between Tertiary gravels and underlying Wianamatta shales, and to Quaternary alluvium underlying drainage systems and wetland margins.
5. Note the topographic modifier within a unit. Within one soil landscape, drainage systems and convergent slopes carry the highest risk, and elevated well-drained positions the lowest — so position in the landscape can move the site's real risk away from the mapped category.
6. Read the known salinity category as evidence-based: it marks areas of known saline soil, or where air photo interpretation and field observation confirmed more than one of scalding, salt efflorescence, vegetation dieback, salt-tolerant plant species, or waterlogging, with a high relative wetness index.
7. Read very low salinity potential as a process statement: rapidly drained soils over highly permeable Hawkesbury or Narrabeen sandstone, with continual flushing and relatively fresh groundwater, where salinity is not expected.
8. Record the category, the landscape unit, the geology and the map's own limitations in the desk study, then decide on site investigation. Use the map to scope the investigation, never to replace it.
9. Where the site sits in known or high potential, or in a low-lying convergent position within any category, carry the finding through to the field programme — targeted sampling for EC and salt profile, and observation for the listed indicators.

## Decision rules

- The map is explicitly not a substitute for on-site investigation, and it must be used together with its companion guidelines document — using the map alone risks an inaccurate hazard assessment.
- Map boundaries are valid at 1:100 000 scale and have been smoothed to accommodate uncertainty and anomalies. They must not be used at property scale.
- The map is an extension of the November 2000 study. The area covered by the 2000 map was incorporated but not reassessed, so a site inside that earlier footprint may show signs of salinity that do not appear on this map.
- Salinity is a dynamic process — areas showing salinity after the mapping date will not appear, and moderate potential areas are explicitly flagged as places where salinity may emerge if risk factors change adversely.
- Locating salinity by aerial photograph interpretation is difficult in established urban areas, so absence of mapped salinity in built-up areas carries less weight than in open country.
- High salinity potential is a predisposition statement (soil, geology, topography and groundwater conditions similar to known salinity areas), not a record of observed salinity.

## Checks

- Confirm the companion guidelines document was actually consulted, and cite it in the desk study.
- Confirm the site is not being read at a scale finer than the map supports; a boundary passing near the site is not a property-scale line.
- Confirm whether the site falls inside the November 2000 study footprint that was not reassessed.
- Confirm the soil landscape unit read off the map matches the unit from the soil landscape mapping for the site.
- Confirm the map date (March 2003, mapping condition August 2002) against the site's development history — later earthworks, irrigation or drainage change the picture.
- Where the desk study result and the site walkover disagree, take the site observation as the stronger evidence and say so.

## Common errors

- Quoting the map category as the site's salinity classification, when it is a regional potential category and not a site classification.
- Reading the map at property scale off a smoothed 1:100 000 boundary.
- Assuming an unmapped urban site is clear, when aerial photo interpretation is unreliable in established urban areas.
- Ignoring the landscape position modifier and giving a whole lot the category of the unit it sits in.
- Using the map without the companion guidelines document.
- Treating a 2002 mapping condition as current.

## Caveats

- Mapping condition dates from August 2002 and the sheet from March 2003. Salinity is dynamic and the map has not been reassessed; treat it as a screening layer with a two-decade lag.
- Soil landscape codes and geological unit codes are read from the map legend and should be confirmed against the current soil landscape and geological mapping.
- Coverage is western Sydney only; the categories and their landscape associations do not transfer outside the mapped area.
- The publisher's own disclaimer excludes liability for actions taken on the basis of the map.
