# Set vibration limits and monitoring for work near buildings and services

> **Source:** `Planning-Environment-Sustainability-Construction-Noise-and-Vibration-Strategy-ST-157.pdf` (Appendix A.3 construction vibration objectives — A.3.1 human perception, A.3.2 effects on building contents, A.3.3 effects on structures, A.3.4 general screening criterion, A.3.5 vibration sensitive and special structures including heritage, sensitive equipment and buried pipework (pp. 59-65), with Table 15 human comfort levels, Table 16 transient guide values and Table 18 DIN 4150-3 values; Appendix D minimum working distances Table 20 and D.1 works within minimum working distance (pp. 71-72); Section 8.4 noise and vibration verification (pp. 50-51). **Outcome.** Vibration management levels for both human comfort and cosmetic damage, tied to receiver type, with minimum working distances applied and a monitoring or shut-down regime in place where the works cannot stay outside them.

## Inputs

- Plant items proposed, with size, weight or model (vibratory roller tonnage, hydraulic hammer mass and carrier size, pile driver type, piling rig type, hand tools)
- Distance from each plant item to the nearest structure and to the nearest occupied receiver
- Receiver classification: normal building, heritage, vibration sensitive structure, sensitive scientific or medical equipment, buried pipework and services
- Whether the vibration source is transient, intermittent or continuous
- Time period the works fall in (human comfort objectives apply differently by period)
- Local geotechnical conditions between source and receiver

## Procedure

1. Classify each vibration source and each receiver first — the applicable criterion depends on both.
2. Read the minimum working distance for each plant item from the strategy's table, for both cosmetic damage and human response. The two distances differ substantially; human response distances are typically several times the damage distances.
3. Compare the actual working distance against both. Cosmetic damage distances must be complied with at all times unless the relevant authority approves otherwise.
4. Where the human comfort distance is breached but the damage distance is not, assess against human comfort criteria for the applicable period. Human comfort is assessed on a vibration dose value basis with preferred and maximum levels, and is the controlling criterion for most receivers because people perceive vibration well below damage thresholds.
5. Where the cosmetic damage distance is breached, first try to substitute a construction method with lower source vibration where feasible and reasonable.
6. If substitution is not feasible, do not proceed unless attended vibration measurements are taken at the start of the works to establish whether the cosmetic damage objective is at risk; where it is, install a permanent monitoring system that warns the plant operator as levels approach the objective, by flashing light, audible alarm or SMS.
7. For buried pipework and services, use the separate guidance rather than the building criteria — below-ground structures sustain higher vibration and are resistant to damage unless in very poor condition.
8. For heritage structures, sensitive scientific and medical equipment, high technology facilities, recording studios and cinemas, run a specific assessment; the general criteria and general minimum distances do not apply.
9. Where the damage management level is exceeded, put vibration monitoring in place; where the human comfort level is exceeded, follow the mitigation and management hierarchy and the community engagement measures.
10. Record the outcome in the impact statement, including the criterion adopted for each receiver type and the monitoring regime.

## Decision rules

- Cosmetic damage minimum working distances must be complied with at all times unless the relevant authority approves otherwise; the human response distances are the trigger for assessment, not an absolute limit.
- Human comfort is normally the controlling criterion, because perception thresholds sit well below damage thresholds — often by around an order of magnitude.
- The tabulated minimum distances are indicative and vary with the plant item and with local geotechnical conditions; they apply to cosmetic damage of typical buildings under typical conditions. Monitoring is the way to confirm them on a specific site.
- More stringent conditions apply to heritage and other sensitive structures than the tabulated distances.
- Human comfort distances in the table relate to continuous vibration. Most construction vibration is intermittent, and higher levels over shorter periods are allowed on that basis.
- Cosmetic damage guide values are drawn from transient vibration guidance and are for minimal risk; the underlying standard notes minor damage becomes possible at magnitudes above those values.

## Checks

- Confirm the plant size or weight class used to read the distance matches what will actually be on site — the distances step up steeply with roller tonnage and hammer mass.
- Confirm every receiver within the larger (human response) distance has been classified, not just those within the damage distance.
- Confirm buried services and pipework in the zone have been picked up separately.
- Confirm attended measurements are scheduled at the commencement of any works inside a cosmetic damage distance.
- Confirm the alarm or warning threshold on any continuous monitoring system is set below the objective, so it warns on approach rather than on exceedance.
- Confirm whether an exceedance triggers monitoring, mitigation, or both, and who has authority to stop the work.

## Common errors

- Applying the cosmetic damage distance and ignoring the much larger human response distance, then fielding complaints.
- Reading the distance for a smaller roller or hammer than the one that turns up.
- Treating the tabulated distances as site-verified when they are indicative and geotechnically dependent.
- Applying general building criteria to heritage fabric or to sensitive equipment.
- Monitoring only after a complaint, instead of attended measurement at commencement inside the damage distance.
- Assessing continuous-vibration human comfort criteria against intermittent works without recognising the allowance for shorter duration.

## Caveats

- Numeric distances and guide values are cited from the strategy's tables and must be read from the controlled document, not recalled — they are plant-class specific and change between versions.
- TfNSW Infrastructure and Place divisional standard; project approval conditions, EPL conditions and asset owner requirements may be more onerous.
- The minimum working distances address cosmetic damage to typical buildings. They are not structural adequacy criteria and do not cover settlement or ground movement caused by the same plant.
