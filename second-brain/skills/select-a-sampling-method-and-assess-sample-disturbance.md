# Select a sampling method and assess sample disturbance

> **Source:** `Principles of Geotechnical Engineering 8th Edition.pdf` (Das 8th ed., Ch. 17, §§17.4–17.5 and 17.8 (common sampling methods, sample disturbance and area ratio, rock coring, recovery ratio and RQD)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** The right sampler for each required test, with quantified sample quality (area ratio, recovery ratio) and, for rock, recovery ratio and RQD.

## Inputs

- The laboratory tests planned and whether each needs an undisturbed or a disturbed but representative sample
- Soil type and consistency at each sampling depth
- Sampler dimensions: outside and inside diameters, cutting shoe geometry, and whether a piston is used
- For rock, core barrel type and size, and the length of each coring run

## Procedure

1. Match sampler to purpose: split-spoon (split-barrel) samplers give disturbed but representative samples suitable for classification, grading and Atterberg limits, and simultaneously give the standard penetration blow count; thin-walled tube (Shelby) samplers give samples suitable for consolidation, triaxial and unconfined compression testing; piston samplers give better quality in soft clays by preventing the sample from being pushed aside or over-recovered.
2. Compute the sampler's area ratio from the outside and inside diameters — the ratio of the annular steel area to the sample area, expressed as a percentage — as the primary index of the disturbance the sampler imposes.
3. Compare the area ratio with the accepted limit: a sample is generally treated as undisturbed if the area ratio is at or below about 10 percent. A standard split spoon is far above that limit; a thin-walled tube is close to it.
4. Record the recovery ratio for each sample or run: the length recovered divided by the length pushed or cored. Low recovery indicates loss, and recovery well over 100 percent in a tube indicates the sample has swelled or been drawn in.
5. Seal, label and transport undisturbed tubes with the ends waxed and the orientation marked, and avoid vibration and temperature extremes.
6. For rock, use a double-tube core barrel to reduce torsional damage, avoid barrel sizes below the practical minimum where core tends to break up, and record recovery ratio and rock quality designation (the summed length of intact core pieces at or above the standard threshold length, divided by the length cored) for each run.
7. Classify rock mass quality from RQD using the published classification, and read the RQD alongside the recovery ratio — high recovery with low RQD means intact but fractured rock.

## Decision rules

- Do not run consolidation, triaxial or unconfined compression tests on split-spoon samples and present the results as undisturbed properties.
- A recovery ratio near unity in rock indicates intact rock; heavily fractured rock typically returns half or less. Poor recovery may itself be the most important finding of a run.
- Extrude and trim tube samples promptly and record any disturbance observed; disturbance discovered at extrusion should be reported with the test result.
- In sensitive or very soft clays, only piston sampling or specialist samplers will produce results usable for design.
- For SPT sampling, record every blow-count interval and the hammer type, so the blow count can be corrected later.

## Checks

- Check the area ratio computed for the actual samplers used on the job, not the nominal specification.
- Check recovery ratios against the driller's log and query low recovery before accepting the interval as tested.
- Check RQD is computed with the standard piece-length threshold and only along the core axis.

## Common errors

- Accepting a thin-walled tube sample where the recovery ratio shows the sample was compressed or lost.
- Computing RQD including pieces broken by handling rather than by natural discontinuities.
- Storing undisturbed tubes upright without sealing, allowing drainage and moisture loss before testing.

## Caveats

- Textbook learning material, not a design standard — verify method currency and all values against the source and Australian practice before use
- Casing and core barrel size tables and the RQD classification are reproduced in the source.
