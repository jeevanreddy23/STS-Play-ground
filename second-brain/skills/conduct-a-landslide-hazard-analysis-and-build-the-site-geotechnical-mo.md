# Conduct a landslide hazard analysis and build the site geotechnical model (AGS 2007)

> **Source:** `42_1_4_amend.pdf` (AGS Practice Note Guidelines for Landslide Risk Management 2007 (Australian Geomechanics Vol 42 No 1, March 2007), Sections 4-5, Appendices B and E), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A documented geotechnical model with characterised landslide hazards, estimated annual probabilities and travel distances, forming the front half of a defensible Landslide Risk Management (LRM) report.

## Inputs

- Desk-study data: geological maps, aerial photos, prior reports, council landslide inventory, local knowledge
- Site survey plan (or tape-and-clinometer plan) with contours, features and services
- Preliminary development design or assumed development (an element at risk must exist)
- Subsurface data from exposures, test pits or boreholes to below potential failure surfaces; groundwater/rainfall response information

## Procedure

1. Define scope with client and regulator requirements; issue a reverse brief (Section 4).
2. Assemble and source-reference all desk-study data (Section 5.1).
3. Inspect the site and surrounds and field-map geomorphic features to scale, covering upslope, downslope and adjacent areas, using standard mapping symbols (Section 5.2.2, Appendix E).
4. Establish the subsurface profile; where pre-existing landslides are suspected, prefer test pits (to see shear planes) or boreholes with inclinometers (Section 5.2.3).
5. Assess groundwater levels and their response to trigger rainfall; qualitative estimates may suffice for low-risk jobs, monitored piezometers for higher-risk or drainage-design cases (Section 5.2.4).
6. Draw natural-scale cross-sections integrating all data to express the geotechnical model, its groundwater and its uncertainties (Section 5.2.5).
7. Identify and characterise each landslide type (classification, volume, location, mechanism, travel distance potential) using Appendix B terminology, considering slope-forming processes and rates (Sections 5.2.6-5.3).
8. Estimate annual probability P(H) for each landslide with a technique matched to study level: inventory/history, geomorphic reasoning (Varnes principle), rainfall-frequency correlation, event trees, or expert judgement 'degree of belief'; partition as P(trigger) x P(slide given trigger) across the trigger range where useful (Section 5.4).
9. Estimate travel distance and probability of spatial impact P(S:H) using site evidence and empirical methods (e.g. Khalili et al 1996 for intact rotational slides; Hunter and Fell 2002, Corominas 1996 for break-up slides/flows), calibrated locally (Section 5.4.3).

## Decision rules

- Choose the qualitative likelihood term FROM the estimated probability — never adopt a probability from a qualitative term (practitioners diverge by orders of magnitude, Section 5.4.2(a)).
- Investigation is adequate only when the model explains slope processes, landslide extent, triggers and process rates; otherwise investigate further (Section 5.2.8).
- Full probabilistic/simulation frequency modelling only for special cases with extensive calibration data; expert judgement is often the only viable option.
- Sanity-check assessed annual probabilities against implied cumulative probability over the design life and known area performance (Section 5.4.2(c)).

## Checks

- Cross-sections at natural scale (plus a natural-scale summary if exaggeration used).
- Hazards addressed above, below and adjacent to the site, not just within the lot.
- Frequency reasoning documented transparently, with sensitivity/uncertainty stated; expert-judgement accuracy typically half to two orders of magnitude.

## Common errors

- Skipping the site walkover/field mapping — mandatory for every assessment.
- A geotechnical model that ignores off-site landslides which could travel onto or regress into the site.
- Inferring more precision in frequency than the data supports, without a stated conservative bias.
- Ignoring altered triggers (development, drainage changes, climate change) when extrapolating from historical performance.

## Caveats

- Verify method details and referenced correlations against the full Practice Note and its Commentary before use; this summary omits the Commentary's worked examples.
- Check for later AGS updates or council-specific policies that modify requirements.
