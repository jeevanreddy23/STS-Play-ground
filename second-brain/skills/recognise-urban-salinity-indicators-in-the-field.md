# Recognise urban salinity indicators in the field

> **Source:** `booklet5new.pdf` ('Salinity Indicators' section, pp. 3-5 of Introduction to Urban Salinity), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Rapid, low-cost identification of sites where salinity is present or emerging, triggering targeted testing rather than assumption.

## Inputs

- Site walkover access (buildings, roads, drainage features, vegetation)
- Optional field EC meter for screening
- Knowledge of local salt-tolerant species

## Procedure

1. Scan vegetation for indicator signals: dieback, yellowing/defoliation, stunted growth, community shift toward salt-tolerant species (e.g. cumbungi, sea barley grass, annual beard grass, couch, salt sandspurrey, Juncus reeds).
2. Scan buildings for indicators: white efflorescence staining, a 'tide mark' showing capillary rise height on walls, mortar and brick fretting grain-by-grain, blistering paint/render, rapid metal corrosion, damage concentrated below the damp-proof course.
3. Scan ground and infrastructure: bare scalded patches, surface salt crystals, puffy dry or greasy wet soil, black staining, unexplained seepage, roads rutting or potholing faster than traffic would explain, damage near stormwater/detention structures.
4. Treat indicators as hypotheses: confirm with soil/water testing (e.g. field EC1:5 screening then lab tests) before attributing damage to salinity, since moisture, poor materials or other causes can mimic the symptoms.
5. Note the water source implicated (groundwater rise, leaking pipes/pools, overwatering, blocked gutters) because management differs by source.

## Decision rules

- Presence of salt-tolerant plants alone does not prove active salinity — they may simply be planted or opportunistic; use them to justify testing, not conclusions.
- A tide mark plus efflorescence points to rising damp carrying salts; damage from the top of walls down points to falling damp (gutters/roof) instead.
- Damage confined below the DPC line indicates the DPC is working; damage above it suggests bridging or failure.

## Checks

- Each suspected indicator has a follow-up test or inspection recorded.
- Alternative causes (plumbing leaks, condensation, poor drainage) explicitly considered.
- Observations photographed and located for the site map.

## Common errors

- Diagnosing salinity from a single indicator without testing.
- Missing the distinction between the salt source and the water source mobilising it.
- Expecting obvious white salt crystals — at most salty sites they are rare; subtler indicators dominate.

## Caveats

- Classification and aggressivity calls need verification against the source documents and project spec; indicator observation is screening only.
- Merged sources: booklet3siteinvestigationsforurbansalinity (2).pdf p. 5 (outbreak indicators list) and INDUSTRY_GUIDE_T56_Residential_Slabs_and_Footings_in_Saline_Environments.pdf Section 3.4 (inspection of existing buildings) teach the same skill.
- Species lists are NSW-oriented; verify local indicator flora.
