# Use NR44 commentary to brief teams on R44 intent and economical material choices

> **Source:** `nr44 Earthworks Specification.pdf` (Foreword; commentary to Clauses 6.1.2 and 7.4.1 (NR44 Ed 2 Rev 0, April 2013)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Site and design teams apply R44 the way the Principal intends - right materials in the right zones, testing effort where it matters - reducing disputes.

## Inputs

- Project R44 edition and completed annexures
- Available cutting materials and their processing costs
- NR44 guide text (shaded = spec text of Ed 4 Rev 1; italics = commentary)

## Procedure

1. Use NR44's structure deliberately: shaded text reproduces R44 Ed 4 Rev 1 wording, italics carry the Principal's intent - quote the spec, apply the intent.
2. Selected Material Zone sourcing: NR44 stresses economy - the SMZ exists to put the best job-won materials directly under the pavement; a lesser material with adequate CBR can beat a hard rock needing expensive processing, and imported SMZ material is a fallback, not a default.
3. Proof rolling: the guide clarifies the clause is not intended to require proof rolling of every lift - it defines the method for when proof rolling is required or directed; plan test rolling at meaningful stages.
4. Pre-tender risk allocation: designate treatments for known problem materials (wet clays, low-CBR cutting floors) in the documents so contractors price them and stop-and-test cycles are avoided.
5. Use the guide's sampling/characteristic-value walk-throughs (CBR characteristic value Q, per-lot sample counts by tonnage) to train staff on how conformity is actually computed under RMS Q.

## Decision rules

- Never cite NR44 as a contractual requirement - it is explicitly not a contract document.
- Where NR44 commentary conflicts with the project's R44 edition or contract, the contract wins.
- Commentary can justify a sensible engineering position in a dispute narrative, but conformity is decided on R44 criteria.

## Checks

- Guide edition (based on R44 Ed 4 Rev 1) mapped against the project's R44 edition for clause renumbering
- Team briefings distinguish spec text from commentary
- Material selection decisions documented with the economy rationale

## Common errors

- Enforcing italicised commentary as if it were specification text
- Assuming every lift needs a witnessed proof roll
- Defaulting to imported select material without exhausting site-won sources

## Caveats

- Verify the R44 edition current for the project - NR44's clause pointers track Ed 4 Rev 1 and drift from later editions.
- Contract documents override both R44 and any guide interpretation.

## Standards cited

RMS R44 Ed 4 Rev 1, RMS Q
