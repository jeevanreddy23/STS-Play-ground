# Determine the liquid limit of road construction materials to TfNSW T108

> **Source:** `t108.pdf` (TfNSW Test Method T108 Issue 3.0 (1 July 2021), Clauses 3-8), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A reported liquid limit (to the stated rounding, or 'Not obtainable') for soils, gravels, crushed rock, recycled and RAP materials, derived from a multi-point Casagrande-apparatus test.

## Inputs

- Representative sample prepared per TfNSW T105 giving at least ~250 g passing the 425 um sieve (Clause 4)
- Casagrande liquid limit apparatus and grooving tool conforming to AS 1289.3.1.1, with rubber base hardness within the specified IRHD range (Clause 3)
- Potable water (distilled/demineralised for sensitive clays or reference testing)
- Moisture content equipment per TfNSW T120; calibrated timer

## Procedure

1. Prepare the passing-425-um fraction per T105; for moisture-sensitive materials (e.g. halloysite, peat) prepare in the natural state without drying back, rubbing wet soil through the sieve (Clause 4).
2. Mix with incremental water additions using palette knives with firm pressure, minimum mixing time per Clause 5 (longer for medium-high plasticity clays), until a thick homogeneous paste at a consistency closing the groove in the target blow-count window.
3. Seal and cure the paste at room temperature for the minimum curing period (Clause 5), then remix and re-verify consistency; record dates/times.
4. Check the apparatus: cup drop height set with the gauge, hinge play, cup wear and grooving-tool tip width within the tolerances of Clause 6(a)-(b).
5. Place paste in the cup, level to no more than the maximum soil depth, cut a single continuous groove, and crank at two revolutions per second until the groove closes over the specified length; record blows.
6. Repeat until two consecutive determinations agree within one blow; take a moisture-content subsample from the closure zone and determine w per T120.
7. Repeat at least three more moisture points, always proceeding drier to wetter, targeting an even spread of blow counts over the range given in Clause 6(f).
8. Plot moisture content vs log(blows), fit a line of best fit, and read the moisture content at 25 blows as the liquid limit (Clause 7); report per Clause 8.

## Decision rules

- If the soil pat slips in the cup, remix with added water and retry; if slipping persists, discard results and report that the liquid limit could not be obtained (Clause 6(d)(iv)).
- If blow count increases after adding water, reject all previous points and restart from the mixing step (Clause 6(f)).
- Take the plastic limit subsample (min ~40 g) from the cured paste before the test; a linear shrinkage (T113) sample may be taken near 25 +/- 3 blows.

## Checks

- Apparatus check results recorded (drop height, wear, hinge play, tool tip width) before testing.
- Consecutive-blow agreement within one blow before accepting each point.
- Points evenly distributed across the specified blow range and taken drier-to-wetter.

## Common errors

- Inadequate mixing/curing giving a falsely low liquid limit (water not penetrating absorptive particles and clay aggregations).
- Redrawing the grooving tool back and forth, redistributing moisture and widening the groove.
- Letting the sample dry between points so blow counts drift upward.
- Micaceous soils (e.g. granitic sands) showing rising liquid limit with prolonged mixing — note mica in the report.

## Caveats

- Verify all tolerances, times and blow-count windows against the current issue of T108 before laboratory use; methods are periodically reissued.
- This is the TfNSW variant — it differs in detail from AS 1289.3.1.1 and from one-point methods.
