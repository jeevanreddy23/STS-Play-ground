# Interpret the rock-core section of a borehole log

> **Source:** [YouTube — How to Read and Understand Borehole Logs Part 2: Rock Core, Weathering, Strength, Discontinuity, RQD](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NMNzMLSO91w&t=0s) (00:00-05:15). A YouTube lecture — learning

**Outcome.** Correct reading of a real borehole log's rock columns — weathering grade, strength grade, fracture spacing, RQD, core recovery, and the per-depth discontinuity records — and recognition of where soil logging conventions stop and rock conventions begin

## Inputs

- A borehole log with rock core section (description, weathering, strength, fracture spacing, RQD, core recovery, discontinuity columns)
- The log's legend for graphic lithology patterns and symbols

## Procedure

1. Identify the soil-rock boundary: upper soil intervals (fill, clay, extremely weathered material behaving as soil) carry SPT results and sample marks but no weathering/strength/fracture-spacing entries, because those parameters only apply to rock
2. Read the weathering column symbols (EW, HW, MW, SW etc.) for each rock interval
3. Read the strength column grades (extremely low, very low, low, medium, high, very high, extremely high)
4. Read fracture spacing in metres as the distance between discontinuities visible in the core (e.g. 0.2-0.5 m spacing implying spacing mostly greater than 0.1 m)
5. Read RQD as a percentage — high values (e.g. 91%) indicate high-quality rock — and cross-check that wide fracture spacing is consistent with high RQD
6. Read core recovery; 100% is ideal, lower values mean core was lost to drilling issues, not necessarily poor rock
7. Read the discontinuity records: each entry gives depth (e.g. 3.30 m, 3.55 m, 3.71 m), type, inclination, roughness, aperture, and surface coating symbols
8. Use the legend at the bottom of the log to decode graphic lithology patterns rather than memorising them

## Decision rules

- Absence of weathering/strength/spacing data over an interval signals soil-type material, not missing data
- High RQD plus wide fracture spacing plus full recovery together indicate good quality rock mass; treat inconsistencies between them as a prompt to re-examine the log

## Checks

- Fracture spacing, RQD and core recovery tell a consistent story for each interval
- Every discontinuity record has a depth and a full symbol string

## Common errors

- Treating low core recovery as low rock quality when it may be a drilling/technical artefact
- Trying to memorise graphic log patterns instead of using the legend supplied with the log

## Caveats

- YouTube lecture — learning material, not an approved design procedure; verify against project specification and current standards
- Symbol conventions vary between logging houses; always defer to the specific log's legend
