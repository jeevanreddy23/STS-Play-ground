# Design a preloading or surcharge scheme with vertical drains

> **Source:** `Principles of Geotechnical Engineering 8th Edition.pdf` (Das 8th ed., Ch. 11, §11.18 (methods for accelerating consolidation settlement) and §11.19 (precompression), with the case history in §11.17), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A precompression design: surcharge height, required duration and drain layout that removes most post-construction primary settlement, with a monitoring plan to confirm removal before unloading.

## Inputs

- Compressible layer thickness, drainage boundaries and coefficient of consolidation (vertical and, if drains are used, horizontal)
- Compression indices and preconsolidation pressure
- Permanent structural load per unit area to be applied later
- Available surcharge fill and the time available in the programme
- Settlement plate, piezometer and (if slopes are involved) inclinometer provisions

## Procedure

1. Compute the primary settlement expected under the permanent structural load alone.
2. Compute the primary settlement under the combined structural plus surcharge load.
3. The required degree of consolidation under the combined load is the ratio of the first to the second — that is the point at which the settlement already achieved equals the total settlement the permanent load would eventually cause.
4. Convert that degree of consolidation to a time factor, and hence to a surcharge duration, using the coefficient of consolidation and the field drainage path.
5. Trade off surcharge height against duration: a higher surcharge reaches the target sooner. Iterate until surcharge height and duration both fit the site and programme constraints.
6. If the required duration is still too long, add vertical drains (prefabricated band drains or sand drains) and redesign for radial plus vertical drainage, choosing drain spacing and pattern from the required time; note that radial consolidation uses the horizontal coefficient of consolidation, and allow for smear around the drain and for well resistance.
7. Check stability of the surcharge embankment itself, including staged construction where the foundation is soft.
8. Instrument with settlement plates and piezometers, monitor settlement and excess pore pressure, and only remove the surcharge when the field data confirm the target has been reached.
9. Allow for secondary compression continuing after the surcharge is removed, particularly in organic soils.

## Decision rules

- Surcharge removes primary settlement, not secondary compression — for peats and organic clays check secondary compression separately, and consider a longer or larger surcharge to pre-empt it.
- Design the drain spacing on the horizontal coefficient of consolidation, which is generally larger than the vertical, but discount it for smear caused by drain installation.
- Do not unload on the calculated date alone: use the observational method, with removal triggered by measured settlement and dissipated pore pressure.
- Where the foundation is soft, the surcharge may need staged placement with stability checks at each stage.

## Checks

- Check the settlement plate records against the predicted time-settlement curve and update the coefficient of consolidation by back-analysis (for example with an Asaoka or hyperbolic construction).
- Check piezometer response confirms the excess pore pressure has largely dissipated across the full layer thickness, not just near the drains.
- Check the surcharge fill is placed over the whole footprint plus a margin, so edge zones are also treated.

## Common errors

- Sizing the surcharge on the structural load only and forgetting the surcharge load itself contributes to the total settlement being consolidated.
- Using the vertical coefficient of consolidation for radial drainage design.
- Removing the surcharge to programme rather than to measured performance.

## Caveats

- Textbook learning material, not a design standard — verify method currency and all values against the source and Australian practice before use
- Radial consolidation charts and the time factor tables are in the source; smear and well-resistance treatments require the specialist literature.
