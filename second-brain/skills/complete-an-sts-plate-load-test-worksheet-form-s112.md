# Complete an STS plate load test worksheet (Form S112)

> **Source:** [Google Drive — Field Sheets/PLT1.jpg, PLT3.jpg, PLT5.jpg and Field Sheets (1)/PLT-1 (1).pdf](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1JtJkR_LZropCNOzh7PHahUJxa5iYkFl_/view) (Four completed S112 worksheets from two different jobs (132/138 McEvoy Street, Alexandria and 105-113 Hollinsworth Road, Marsden Park), all with the same header fields, load/unload/reload cycle and three dial gauge columns). Reconstructed from STS's own
> field records on Google Drive — completed forms, labelled photos and
> scans, catalogued in [[Field records catalogue]]. It shows what the
> records evidence, not necessarily the current approved procedure —

**Outcome.** A completed 'Load Settlement - Time Relationship Worksheet (Plate Load Testing)' recording dial gauge settlements through a load / unload / reload cycle for one test location, ready for office reduction.

## Inputs

- Form S112 (Load Settlement - Time Relationship Worksheet, Date of Issue 01/10/19, Revision 1)
- Loading system with load readout in kN (and/or bar)
- Three (up to four) dial gauges on the plate
- Thermometer
- Test plate (records show 600 mm diameter, 25 mm thick)

## Procedure

1. Fill the header: Project, Project No. / STS No., Client, Technician (dual initials where two staff attend, e.g. KM/SN), Date Tested.
2. Circle the test method on the printed line: BS1377:Part 9 | ASTM D1196.
3. Record Soil Description (e.g. 'Gravelly Sand Gray' or the pavement material such as DGB20), Page number (circled; one page per test location, numbered through the job), Plate Diameter (mm), Plate Thickness (mm), Temp at Start, Temp at End.
4. Fill Static Load Details and Field Density (t/m3) if applicable, and Test Location (PLT1, PLT3, PLT5 ...).
5. Take the zero-load Initial reading on all dial gauges and note 'Initial reading' in Comments.
6. Load in increments, writing one row per increment with Load (kN)/(Bar) and Dial Gauge 1, 2 and 3 readings (records show the sequence 0, 2, 4, 13, 31, 50, 68, 87, 110 kN, extended to 150 and 180 kN on one job).
7. Unload in decrements recording the same columns (records show 87, 40, 17, 2 kN), leaving a blank row between load stages.
8. Reload through the increments again (4, 13, 31, 50, 68, 87 ... kN) recording all gauges.
9. Columns for Time, Elapsed Time (min), Temp., Ave Set. (mm) and Def. Rate (mm/min) are on the form for holds where readings must stabilise.
10. Complete the footer equipment traceability: Load Cell No., Dial Gauge No(s), Stop Watch No., Thermometer No.
11. If a reading is written in error, strike through and write the correct value above (visible on the records); never overwrite illegibly.

## Decision rules

- One sheet per test location; pages numbered consecutively across the job so PLT1 = page 1, PLT3 = page 3, etc.
- Leave a blank row between the loading, unloading and reloading stages so the cycle boundaries are unambiguous.
- Gauges may read in different senses (one record shows a gauge counting from 300.00 and another job recording negative values); record what the gauge shows and let the office reduce it.

## Checks

- Initial reading row present and flagged in Comments
- Every load increment row has all active dial gauges filled
- Unload readings recover consistently across gauges (records show recovery on all three gauges)
- Header temperatures at start and end both recorded
- Equipment numbers in the footer filled in

## Common errors

- Missing the strike-through convention and overwriting a misread value
- Not separating load/unload/reload stages, making the cycle hard to reduce
- Leaving footer instrument numbers blank (blank on some of the example sheets)
- Header STS number corrected after the fact — write the confirmed job number before starting

## Caveats

- Reconstructed from STS field records — confirm against the current STS procedure before treating as the standard method
- Time / Elapsed Time / Ave Set / Def Rate columns are blank on all four examples, so the hold-duration practice is not evidenced
- The printed method line reads 'BS1377:Part 0' on the scans — it is taken to mean Part 9 (plate loading), but confirm on the current form

## Standards cited

BS 1377: Part 9 (printed on form), ASTM D1196 (printed on form)
