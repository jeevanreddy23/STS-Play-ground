# Choose the load-carrying mechanism for a rock foundation before selecting design parameters

> **Source:** `Pells Paper on Foundations on sandstone and shale in The Sydney Region.pdf` (Pells, Mostyn & Walker, Australian Geomechanics Dec 1998, Section 3 (pp 18-19) and Figure 1 (p 20)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A deliberate base-only, side-shear-only or combined design basis for a footing or socket, consistent with geometry, constructability and the applied mechanics of side/base load sharing.

## Inputs

- Depth in rock D and minimum plan dimension b
- Construction method and achievable base/sidewall cleanliness
- Expected sidewall roughness class
- Relative stiffness of footing concrete, sidewall rock and toe rock

## Procedure

1. If D/b is less than 1, design for base bearing only.
2. If the base may be dirty or base concrete quality is doubtful, design for side shear only, and recognise the safety implications of losing the end-bearing backup.
3. For combined designs, let load share between sidewall and base follow relative stiffnesses via a proper interaction method; never apportion notional allowable side and base values by hand - they are not additive (a 5 m long, 0.6 m diameter socket in good sandstone carries over ~95% of load in side shear).
4. Confirm roughness R2 or better so sidewall behaviour is non-brittle (peak strength holds plastically after small displacement).
5. Remember peak side shear mobilises at much smaller displacement than peak end bearing: mobilising significant base resistance requires full sidewall slip, which places the whole safety margin in end bearing.
6. For load-displacement modelling, idealise the socket response as bilinear, ignoring the short progressive-slip region.

## Decision rules

- If ultimate end-bearing capacity is uncertain, do not design for full side slip.
- If sockets are drilled by simple augers without attention to cleanliness, modern parameters are inappropriate; only very conservative allowable loads are defensible.
- In slaking rocks, use mechanical sidewall-cleaning tools instead of water-based cleaning.

## Checks

- Sidewall roughness assessed against the R1-R4 scheme (reproduced in the companion papers).
- Cleanliness method (drilling under water, water-stir, cleaning teeth) matched to rock sensitivity to water.

## Common errors

- Adding allowable side shear and allowable end bearing as if independent.
- Claiming combined capacity for sockets whose base cannot be inspected.
- Assuming smooth (R1) sockets behave plastically; brittle interface failure is possible below R2.

## Caveats

- Sydney sandstone/shale experience base; verify transferability elsewhere.
- Design reference only: decisions must be verified against the source paper and reviewed by a qualified engineer before use.
