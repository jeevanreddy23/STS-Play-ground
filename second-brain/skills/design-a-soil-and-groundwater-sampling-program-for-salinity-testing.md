# Design a soil and groundwater sampling program for salinity testing

> **Source:** `booklet3siteinvestigationsforurbansalinity (2).pdf` (Phase 2, pp. 7-11 (Soils, Laboratory Analysis, Groundwater Tests)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A defensible sampling regime (locations, depths, sample handling, lab selection) that yields reliable salinity, sodicity and corrosivity data per landform unit.

## Inputs

- Phase 1 desktop review and site walk outputs (landform units, suspected outbreaks)
- Proposed depth of disturbance (e.g. pipeline trench depth)
- Access to backhoe/drill rig; laboratory with NATA or ISO 9000 accreditation

## Procedure

1. Describe each profile to the greatest of: 3 m depth, depth to bedrock if shallower, or the depth of potential physical/chemical impact from the development (3 m reflects typical backhoe reach).
2. Record for each horizon: depth, Munsell colour and mottling, field pH, field texture, moisture status, structure/peds, fabric, coarse fragments, root quantity, and hard pans; log survey metadata (MGA reference, exposure type, surveyor, date).
3. Sample each major soil horizon; if no distinct horizons, sample at 20 cm, 0.5 m, 1.0 m, 1.5 m, 2.0 m, 2.5 m and 3.0 m. Where surface salt expression exists, test the top 2 cm separately.
4. Collect a minimum of about 1.5 kg per sample in labelled cotton bags; air-dry promptly and dispatch to the laboratory soon after collection; retain samples until project completion where possible.
5. Bulk topsoil samples (mix six or more sub-samples within a 10 m radius) for more reliable topsoil results — never bulk subsoils.
6. Select roughly 5-20% of described profiles for full laboratory analysis, with at least one lab-analysed profile per major horizon in each landform unit.
7. For larger projects or where groundwater data is absent, install piezometers (possibly nested at multiple depths for layered groundwater systems); log depth, ECe, pH, texture/colour and moisture for each layer, then analyse groundwater for EC, pH, Na, Ca, K, Mg, sulphate, carbonates and chlorides.

## Decision rules

- Number of lab profiles: use the ranges in Table 1 column 6 of the booklet (p. 6) tied to mapping scale, and weigh the per-profile lab cost (2002 figure: about $150/sample, i.e. ~$1050 for a 7-depth profile) against project value.
- Use field test kits to screen which properties justify full laboratory analysis.
- Nested piezometers of different depths where regional and local groundwater systems may be stacked; recharge sites show short-term weather response in levels, discharge sites do not.
- Register piezometers/bores with the state groundwater authority and use licensed drillers (statutory requirement in NSW).

## Checks

- Every sample clearly labelled with site number and depth interval; lab report documents methods, equipment and units.
- Laboratory holds NATA or ISO 9000 accreditation for the tests used.
- Groundwater chemistry compared across bores/depths to test whether waters share a common source.
- New profile data lodged in the public soil database (SALIS in NSW) for future reuse.

## Common errors

- Bulking subsoil samples (only topsoil bulking is acceptable).
- Letting moist samples sit undried, degrading result reliability.
- Sampling only the surface when the development disturbs deeper horizons (trenches, cut and fill).
- Omitting the separate top-2 cm sample where salt crust is visible, diluting the surface salinity signal.

## Caveats

- Classification and aggressivity calls must be verified against the source document and the project specification.
- Costs quoted are 2002 values — use only as relativities.
- A similar minimal scheme in INDUSTRY_GUIDE_T56_Residential_Slabs_and_Footings_in_Saline_Environments.pdf (Section 3.3) uses a single random pit tested at 500 mm intervals to 2.5-3.0 m for single lots; that is a floor, not a substitute for landform-based design on larger sites.
