# Classify Sydney sandstone and shale with the Pells three-criteria system and state the controlling criterion

> **Source:** `PELLS MOSTYN WONG (2019) CLASSIFICATION OF SANDSTONES AND SHALES IN THE SYDNEY.pdf` (Australian Geomechanics Vol 54 No 2, June 2019; Section 3 and Tables 2a-2c (p 33), misuse guidance in Section 2.2 (pp 31-32)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A defensible Class I-V assignment for Hawkesbury Sandstone or Ashfield Shale, with the rock volume and controlling criterion recorded, and misuse of the system avoided.

## Inputs

- Saturated substance unconfined compressive strength (UCS) data
- Defect (joint and bedding) spacing measured as true spacing in core or exposure
- Percentage of seams (clay, fragmented, highly weathered zones) within the defined zone of influence
- Definition of the rock volume being classified

## Procedure

1. Assemble quantitative data for all three criteria: saturated UCS, defect spacing, and % seams in the vertical interval of influence.
2. Assign the class from Tables 2a (sandstone) and 2b (shale) of the 2019 paper; the class thresholds differ between sandstone and shale, so use the correct table.
3. Apply the rule that all three criteria must be satisfied; the worst criterion governs the class.
4. Describe defect spacing with the ISRM / ISO 14689 terms tabulated in Table 2c.
5. When reporting, state the volume of rock mass the classification covers and which criterion (strength, spacing or seams) controlled the class.

## Decision rules

- Never classify from pile-rig cuttings or from drilling resistance; the system needs quantitative strength, spacing and seam data.
- Do not apply the system to small components of the rock mass, to major fault zones, or to zones affected by dykes and sills.
- Do not use the system for slope stability, excavatability or rock-cutting assessments; use the Bertuzzi & de Ambrosis ground-type matrix instead of the foundation classes when linking to tunnel support design.

## Checks

- UCS values are for saturated substance, not dry.
- Defect spacing is true spacing, not apparent spacing in inclined core.
- Seam percentage is computed over the correct zone of influence (1.5 times least footing dimension for pads; socket length plus footing width for sockets).

## Common errors

- Attaching engineering properties to a class without supporting lab or field data.
- Reporting a class without stating the rock volume or the controlling criterion.
- Using the sandstone table thresholds for shale or vice versa.

## Caveats

- Zone-of-influence definitions come from the companion 1998 paper; cross-check both documents.
- Design reference only: class thresholds and their application must be verified against the source tables and reviewed by a qualified engineer before use.

## Standards cited

ISO 14689 / ISRM defect spacing terminology, AS 1726 (geotechnical site investigations), Pells et al (1978) and Pells, Mostyn & Walker (1998) original classification papers
