# Determine the Atterberg limits and plasticity index of a fine-grained soil

> **Source:** `Principles of Geotechnical Engineering 8th Edition.pdf` (Das 8th ed., Ch. 4, §§4.2 (liquid limit, percussion and fall cone) and 4.3 (plastic limit, plasticity index)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Liquid limit, plastic limit and plasticity index for the fraction passing the 0.425 mm sieve, with the test method used clearly identified.

## Inputs

- Air-dried soil passing the 0.425 mm sieve, thoroughly remixed with distilled water and cured
- Percussion-cup liquid limit device with the appropriate grooving tool, or a fall-cone apparatus
- Ground glass plate for plastic limit rolling, moisture tins, balance and oven

## Procedure

1. Percussion method: work the paste into the cup, cut the standard groove, and record the number of blows to close the groove over the standard length; sample the closed zone for moisture content.
2. Repeat at three or more moisture contents so the blow counts bracket the defining count (aim for counts spread either side of 25), plot moisture content against log blow count, fit the straight flow line and read the liquid limit at the defining blow count.
3. Report the slope of that flow line as the flow index if index behaviour is of interest.
4. Fall-cone method: drop the standard cone from point contact and record penetration in the standard time at four or more moisture contents; plot moisture content against log penetration and read the liquid limit at the defining penetration.
5. Plastic limit: roll an ellipsoidal mass by hand on the glass plate until threads of the standard diameter just crumble, and take the moisture content at crumbling; alternatively use the heavier fall cone at the same defining penetration.
6. Compute plasticity index as liquid limit minus plastic limit; report as non-plastic where no thread can be formed.

## Decision rules

- State which liquid limit method was used — percussion and fall-cone results are not interchangeable, especially at high liquid limits.
- Reject a percussion run in which the soil slides in the cup rather than flowing, and reject a blow count far outside the accepted range.
- Never oven-dry a soil before limits testing unless the objective is specifically the oven-dried liquid limit used for organic soil identification.

## Checks

- Check the flow line is genuinely linear over the tested range; curvature indicates inconsistent mixing or drying between points.
- Check the cup drop height and base hardness are within calibration, and the grooving tool type is recorded.
- Check the plasticity index against the liquid limit on the plasticity chart: a point plotting above the upper (U) bound indicates a testing error.

## Common errors

- Adding water to a drying sample instead of remixing from wetter to drier, which gives irreproducible results in clays.
- Testing the whole soil rather than the fraction finer than 0.425 mm.
- Rolling plastic-limit threads on a surface that absorbs water, biasing the plastic limit low.

## Caveats

- Textbook learning material, not a design standard — verify method currency and all values against the source and Australian practice before use
- Cup drop height, groove closure length, defining blow count, cone geometry, cone masses and defining penetration are specified numbers — take them from the governing standard.
- Australian practice uses AS 1289.3.1.1/3.1.2 (liquid limit) and AS 1289.3.2.1 (plastic limit).
