# Photograph a site or test location with a time-GPS overlay

> **Source:** [Google Drive — 2520E-G/Site Photos/TimePhoto_20260624_100529.jpg and TimePhoto_20260624_100527.jpg](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Bkw9QTMHd-nWJuagplD0iUCpn0H83uZu/view) (Two consecutive overlay-stamped photos of a bulk excavation with piling rig and pier reinforcement cages; overlay carries date-time, bearing, full address, altitude, speed, compass rose and map inset). **Outcome.** A dated, geolocated, orientation-stamped site photograph that can stand as evidence of site conditions at a specific moment (e.g. bulk excavation level, pier cage delivery, piling rig on site).

## Inputs

- Phone with a timestamp-camera app (records show a 'TimePhoto'-style overlay)
- GPS/location services enabled
- Clear line of sight to the works being recorded

## Procedure

1. Open the timestamp camera app so the overlay is active before shooting (overlay in the records shows: date and time to the second, compass bearing in degrees plus cardinal direction, street address, suburb, LGA, state, altitude, speed, a compass rose, and a Google map inset with a location pin).
2. Stand at a vantage point that captures the works, plant and site boundary fencing in one frame.
3. Frame so the overlay text does not cover the subject of interest.
4. Take successive shots from the same standpoint at different bearings to cover the area (records show consecutive frames 2 seconds apart at 236° SW and 270° W of the same excavation).
5. Keep the app's default filename (TimePhoto_YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS.jpg) so files sort chronologically.
6. File the photos in the job's Site Photos folder under the STS project number (e.g. 2520E-G/Site Photos).

## Decision rules

- If the shot is evidentiary (levels, founding conditions, plant on site, progress claims), use the overlay app rather than the plain phone camera — plain PXL_ camera shots also exist in the same folder but carry no visible metadata.
- Change bearing, not standpoint, between consecutive shots so the compass stamp documents what direction each frame faces.

## Checks

- Overlay shows the correct site address and a plausible altitude before leaving the vantage point.
- Timestamp matches the works being recorded (the stamp is burned into the pixels and cannot be corrected later).
- Whole area of interest is inside the frame — excavation face, plant, and fencing visible in the examples.

## Common errors

- Using the plain camera app so the photo has no visible time/location stamp
- Overlay text obscuring the feature the photo is meant to prove
- GPS not settled, stamping a stale address or altitude

## Caveats

- Reconstructed from STS field records — confirm against the current STS procedure before treating as the standard method
- The specific app is not named in the records; the overlay format matches the 'TimePhoto' filename prefix
- Two other photos in the same folder (PXL_ prefix) could not be retrieved, so the split between overlay and plain photos is inferred from filenames only
