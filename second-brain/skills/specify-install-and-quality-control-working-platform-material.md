# Specify, install and quality-control working platform material

> **Source:** `Working Platforms for tracked plant.pdf` (Sections 3.3, 3.4 and Chapter 4 (Installation)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A platform built from suitable granular (or approved recycled) material, placed and compacted to specification with records proving it, including correctly installed geosynthetics.

## Inputs

- Design specification including required material strength and acceptable variation
- Candidate platform material data: grading, maximum particle size, strength, durability, crushability, geochemical properties
- Geosynthetic type and function (separation/filtration fabric vs strengthening geogrid) and manufacturer lapping requirements
- Operational constraints (e.g. pile positional tolerance driving a smaller maximum particle size)

## Procedure

1. Specify material for compactibility, durability, trafficability and drainage; set maximum particle size to suit operations (a smaller max size is needed where tight pile positional tolerance applies — see Section 3.3); exclude organic matter and clay; avoid materials that crush significantly under trafficking.
2. Review geochemical properties: sulfate attack risk on substructure concrete (especially if the platform becomes permanent works), and hazards in unsorted demolition rubble (asbestos, rebar, plaster).
3. Distinguish geosynthetic functions: geofabrics to separate/filter a granular platform from cohesive subgrade; geogrids to strengthen. Place strengthening reinforcement at or near the platform base; follow overlap requirements.
4. Protect geosynthetics from damage in storage, handling and installation; place an adequate thickness of material over them before compaction, and a protective granular layer beneath where the subgrade could damage them.
5. Place and compact in accordance with the specification with competent supervision; observe placement for revealed subgrade weak areas needing removal, treatment or design change.
6. Run quality-control tests and sample the material at suitable frequencies; keep records certifying installation as specified (Section 1.6).
7. Use plate load tests to indicate platform adequacy, choosing a plate size as close to the plant track size as practicable to replicate the loading.
8. Compact and level the finished surface; mark permissible working area boundaries and provide service markers and any interceptor ditches/drainage.

## Decision rules

- If platform material is excavated and re-used elsewhere on site, re-check it still meets the design assumptions before re-use.
- On cohesive formations in wet conditions, provide a granular filter layer or geofabric to stop upward fines migration degrading the platform.
- If quality-control results fall outside the specified acceptable variation, stop and refer to the platform designer.

## Checks

- Certification that the platform was installed as specified, backed by supervision and test records.
- Small plate tests recognised as unrepresentative — plate size versus track size documented.
- Geosynthetic cover thickness before compaction plant traffics over it.

## Common errors

- Using unsorted demolition rubble containing plaster, asbestos or rebar.
- Compacting directly on exposed geogrid/geofabric and damaging it.
- Confusing separation fabric with structural reinforcement and claiming a thickness reduction from a non-structural layer.
- No sampling regime, so degraded or variable material goes undetected.

## Caveats

- OCR noise present; verify particle-size limits and any test frequencies against the original document before specifying.
- Material availability and specification practice differ by country — adapt to local specifications (e.g. TfNSW/TMR) while keeping the performance intent.

## Standards cited

BRE BR 470 (2004), BS 6031:1981 (earthworks), Highways Agency Specification for Highway Works, CIRIA SP123 (geosynthetics)
