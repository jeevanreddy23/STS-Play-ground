# Select surface treatments for pavements by salt content before sealing

> **Source:** `Techniques-to-use-on-roads-affected-by-salinity.pdf` (Section 5.2-5.3.2 and Table 2, pp. 8-10), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A seal/prime strategy chosen from measured salt content of pavement fines, preventing salt crystallisation from blistering and destroying thin bituminous surfacings.

## Inputs

- Base material samples for salt testing (fines fraction passing 2.36 mm)
- Salt content test capability (gravimetric on dried saturation extract; RTA Test Method T200 for chloride content of roadbase)
- Sealing options: prime, primerseal, final seal, asphalt, concrete

## Procedure

1. Suspect salt when a bituminous surface shows eruptions of small blisters; test the uppermost base material rather than assuming.
2. Determine salt content of the fines (% passing the 2.36 mm sieve, measured gravimetrically by drying the saturation extract) — note the criterion applies to fines, not the whole sample.
3. Apply the treatment ladder (after Januszke 1984, Table 2 of the paper, p. 9): roughly 0-1.5% salt, a prime is acceptable; about 1.6-2.5%, apply a primerseal followed as quickly as possible by the final seal; about 2.6-3.0%, a swept sacrificial primed surface then primerseal and final seal may work, otherwise treat as the highest band; above 3.0%, special investigation is required and no single method is assured.
4. For the mid band (<2.5%), the cited practice is a primerseal with size 10 aggregate and AMC4 primer binder at about 1.8 L/m2.
5. Specify low-salt aggregates and construction water for sealing work (cited RTA limits: ~600 ppm chloride, ~400 ppm sulphate maximums).
6. Prefer substantial seal treatments over light primes/primerseals in saline areas, since light treatments are the most salt-vulnerable; concrete and well-designed asphalt surfaces are generally the most salt-durable.

## Decision rules

- Light treatments (primes, primerseals) left exposed in saline conditions will distress — minimise the time between primerseal and final seal.
- If salt exceeds ~3% in fines, do not proceed on a standard method; investigate (source of salt, moisture regime) and trial treatments.
- Salt can also crystallise beneath seals over fine crushed rock bases, 'blowing up' the seal into potholes — treat promptly when blisters appear.

## Checks

- Salt content measured on the correct fraction (fines) and reported as % with method stated.
- Aggregate and water salt certificates reviewed before sealing.
- Post-sealing surveillance for blistering in the first seasons.

## Common errors

- Priming a salty base and losing the prime to crystallisation before sealing.
- Testing whole-sample salt content and comparing against fines-based thresholds.
- Attributing seal blisters to bitumen quality rather than underlying salt.

## Caveats

- Classification and aggressivity calls must be verified against the source document and the current road agency specification — thresholds derive from 1984 South Australian work and RTA-era methods that may have been updated.
- Salt content bands are for thin bituminous surfacings on unsealed/granular pavements; they do not size asphalt or concrete solutions.
- Companion (OCR-corrupted) source roads-and-salinity.pdf cites related limits work in Australian Road Research 20(4) 1990 — verify against a clean copy.

## Standards cited
