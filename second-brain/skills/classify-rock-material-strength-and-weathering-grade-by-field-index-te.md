# Classify rock material strength and weathering grade by field index tests on core

**Outcome.** A field strength grade (extremely low to extremely high) and weathering grade (fresh to extremely weathered) assigned to rock core using hand, thumb, and geological-pick index tests

## Inputs

- Rock core or hand specimen
- Geological pick/hammer
- Optionally water (to test deterioration of extremely weathered material)

## Procedure

1. Extremely low: material behaves more like soil
2. Very low: crumbles or breaks easily with fingers/thumb
3. Low: can be broken by hand fairly easily
4. Medium: breaks by hand only with difficulty
5. High: breaks with one firm blow of the geological pick
6. Very high: needs more than one solid hammer blow (often two or three)
7. Extremely high: the pick blow makes the rock ring audibly rather than break
8. For weathering: fresh rock shows no staining; slightly weathered (SW) shows slight discoloration but near-fresh strength; moderately weathered (MW) and highly weathered (HW) show progressively stronger discoloration and lower strength; extremely weathered (EW) has degraded to soil-like material and may deteriorate further when water is added

## Decision rules

- Weathering grade is a good proxy indicator of strength: heavily weathered implies weak, fresh or slightly weathered implies high strength
- If material is so weathered it behaves as soil, log and assess it as soil, not rock

## Checks

- Strength grade and weathering grade assigned to the same interval should be mutually consistent

## Common errors

- Assigning a high strength grade to discolored, weathered core without actually attempting the index test
- Confusing discoloration (weathering indicator) with mineral staining confined to joint surfaces

## Caveats

- YouTube lecture — learning material, not an approved design procedure; verify against project specification and current standards
- Grade boundaries are qualitative; different logging standards attach different UCS ranges to these grades — check the applicable standard before quantitative use
