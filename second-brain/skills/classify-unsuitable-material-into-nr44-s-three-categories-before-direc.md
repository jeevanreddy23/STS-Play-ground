# Classify unsuitable material into NR44's three categories before directing removal

> **Source:** `nr44 Earthworks Specification.pdf` (Guide commentary to Clause 2.4 (NR44 Ed 2 Rev 0, April 2013; based on R44 Ed 4 Rev 1)), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** Faster, better-costed decisions on foundation material: spoil, treat in place, bridge over, or accept - instead of defaulting to blanket removal.

## Inputs

- Foundation inspection observations after topsoil strip or at cutting floor level
- Moisture condition and any in-situ test results (CBR, strength)
- Embankment height over the area and distance below the Selected Material Zone
- Earthworks balance and program context

## Procedure

1. Sort the suspect material into NR44's three working categories: inherently unsuitable (peat, muck, some silts - cannot go in the works at all), unsuitable by virtue of position (e.g. low CBR at cutting floor - fine elsewhere in general fill), and unsuitably wet (problem is moisture, not the soil).
2. For inherently unsuitable ground, prefer designs/directions that bridge over it with embankment height rather than excavate; NR44 notes removal under adverse conditions typically multiplies cost several-fold versus raising grade.
3. For position-based unsuitability over extensive areas, use the standard foundation treatments (e.g. excavate-and-replace or working platform types) rather than ad-hoc removal, and reuse the material in general earthworks.
4. For unsuitably wet material, weigh drying, stabilising, spoiling or using as-is deep in fills based on earthworks balance, drying sites, climate and long-term performance.
5. Where embankments are high (roughly above 1.5 m per the guide), judge the foundation mainly as a working platform; where the foundation is close beneath the Selected Material Zone (within about 1.2 m) or at cutting floor level, allow program time for in-situ testing.
6. Decide quickly - exposed foundations wet up in rain and the delay costs usually exceed the cost of removing a bit more material.

## Decision rules

- A quick decision with a small extra removal cost usually beats a delayed optimal decision.
- Material wet but sound can often be placed deep in high fills with geotechnical concurrence; monitor heave and pore pressure dissipation rather than rejecting it.
- Where a treatment can be designated pre-tender, specify it - removing offending material as general earthworks avoids stop-and-test cycles.

## Checks

- Hold point discipline retained: Principal inspects before each lot of unsuitable is replaced
- Before/after surveys captured for quantity measurement
- Category decision recorded with its basis (inspection, testing, judgement)

## Common errors

- Testing obviously inherently-unsuitable material instead of directing spoil immediately
- Removing position-unsuitable material to spoil when it is acceptable general fill
- Leaving foundations exposed awaiting decisions through wet weather

## Caveats

- NR44 is a guide, not a contract document - it explains intent and never overrides R44 or the contract.
- Based on R44 Ed 4 Rev 1; verify commentary still matches the R44 edition current for the project.
