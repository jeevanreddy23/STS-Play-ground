# Screen construction works to decide simple or detailed noise and vibration assessment

> **Source:** `Planning-Environment-Sustainability-Construction-Noise-and-Vibration-Strategy-ST-157.pdf` (Section 7 assessing construction noise and vibration impact, especially Table 2 simple-or-detailed and Sections 7.1.1 to 7.1.7 (pp. 27-31) and Section 7.2 detailed assessment (pp. 31-35); Section 1.4 construction hours (p. 8); Appendix A objectives (pp. 56-66); Appendix D minimum working distances (pp. 71-72); Appendix E estimator tool (p. 73). Version 4.1, ST-157, April 2019 (content dated April 2018).), held in the local reference library — see

**Outcome.** A defensible decision on whether a construction activity needs a simple (tool-based) noise and vibration assessment or a detailed assessment by a qualified acoustic consultant, with the screening logic and its outcome written into the impact statement.

## Inputs

- Construction period the works fall in: standard construction hours, or the defined out-of-hours work periods
- Total duration of the construction activity
- A representative set of construction stages or scenarios, each with its plant and equipment list
- Locations of noise sensitive receivers, and any other sensitive receiver types (heritage, sensitive scientific or medical equipment, buried pipework and services)
- Locations of vibration sensitive receivers relative to the work area
- Whether vibration intensive plant will be used, and its size or weight class
- Background noise levels, estimated or measured
- Whether construction traffic will change the without-construction noise level

## Procedure

1. Confirm which construction period the works sit in — standard hours or which out-of-hours period — because the applicable objectives change with the period.
2. Apply the duration screen. Works of short duration (the strategy uses a six week cut-off) can be assessed with the construction noise estimator tool; longer works go straight to detailed assessment.
3. Define the construction scenarios: a representative range of stages, each with its own plant and equipment list, reflecting realistic worst-case plant operating at any one time.
4. Set the noise study area by identifying noise sensitive receivers, including the other sensitive receiver types listed in the objectives appendix.
5. Set the vibration study area by a two-step screen. First, are there receivers within the minimum working distance for cosmetic damage for the plant proposed? If yes, go to detailed assessment. If no, second, are the works within the minimum working distance for human response? If yes, assess vibration impact; if no, the simple vibration assessment is complete and a clear low-risk statement goes in the report.
6. Run the separate proximity screen for vibration sensitive receivers within 100 m of the work area — if any, confirm objectives by receiver type and note that the estimator tool cannot be used.
7. Determine the noise objectives: estimate or measure background, set noise management levels, and identify sleep disturbance objectives for the night period where relevant.
8. Screen construction traffic noise: if it will exceed the without-construction level by 2 dBA, or if sleep disturbance objectives are triggered by night movements, the estimator tool cannot be used.
9. If all screens pass, run the estimator tool: calculate levels at the worst affected receivers, document background level, management levels and predicted levels, apply standard mitigation and adjust the prediction, review the additional mitigation the tool returns, start notifications, and fix the verification monitoring locations.
10. Write the impact statement or assessment with the minimum content set: justification for using a simple assessment with the environmental management representative's endorsement, work duration and periods, plant list for the noisiest and most vibration significant operations, nearest sensitive receivers, standard and additional mitigation measures, and the complaints management process.

## Decision rules

- Any receiver inside the minimum working distance for cosmetic damage forces a detailed assessment.
- Detailed assessment is required, and must be done by an appropriately qualified person such as an acoustic consultant, where any of the following apply: noise with special audible characteristics; works impacting receivers for more than six weeks; a moderate to large number of impacted receivers including moderate to high density residential or commercial; exceedance of construction traffic noise objectives; exceedance of sleep disturbance objectives.
- If unsure whether a detailed assessment is needed, start with the simple assessment and use the estimator tool as the screening test.
- Where no vibration intensive activity is proposed and no vibration sensitive receiver is within the screening distances, record a clear statement that the risk is assessed as low to negligible rather than leaving it unaddressed.
- No quantitative assessment is required for activities that generate no noise-producing work, for example inspections using no noise generating equipment.
- Some activities within a large project may need only a simple assessment even where the project overall needs a detailed one; the management plan sets the structure for that.

## Checks

- Confirm the plant list used in the screen is the realistic worst case operating simultaneously, not a nominal list.
- Confirm the receiver search caught the non-residential sensitive types, not just dwellings.
- Confirm background levels were estimated or measured by a stated method rather than assumed.
- Confirm the justification for a simple assessment is endorsed by the environmental management representative and is in the report.
- Confirm verification monitoring locations are nominated before works start.

## Common errors

- Screening on nominal plant rather than the noisiest realistic combination.
- Missing the cosmetic damage minimum working distance screen and going straight to the noise tool.
- Using the estimator tool where a vibration sensitive receiver sits within 100 m, or where construction traffic or sleep disturbance objectives are triggered — cases the tool explicitly cannot cover.
- Salami-slicing a long duration activity into sub-six-week pieces to avoid detailed assessment.
- Leaving vibration unaddressed in the report instead of recording the low-risk finding explicitly.

## Caveats

- This is a Transport for NSW Infrastructure and Place divisional standard for rail and transport infrastructure projects. It is not a general NSW requirement — check what the project's own approval, EPL and conditions of consent require.
- The estimator tool referenced is an internal TfNSW tool; the workflow is transferable but the tool may not be available.
- Version 4.1 was issued April 2019 with a stated review date of April 2020; confirm the current version before relying on it.
