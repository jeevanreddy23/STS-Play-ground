# Maintain, calibrate and troubleshoot the AX 01a plate bearing tester

> **Source:** `BedAnlAx01a_engl V40a.pdf` (Chapters 10-15 and 19, pp. 19-23), held in the local reference library — see
> [[Reference library]]. This note is a paraphrased working aid, not the
> document. Values, clauses and criteria must be read from the source before
> they are relied on, and engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** The engineer can keep the device within its calibration and safety regime and diagnose common hydraulic faults in the field.

## Inputs

- AX 01a system and its calibration certificate
- Manufacturer-supplied hydraulic oil
- Access to Anix GmbH or authorised specialists for repairs

## Procedure

1. Before every use, inspect the measuring device, cables, load cell, displacement transducer, connectors, pump, hose, cylinder and measuring bridge; never assemble or use a damaged system (pp. 5, 20).
2. Recalibrate once per year and after any repair, per the DIN 18134 requirement cited in the manual (p. 19).
3. Change hydraulic oil at least yearly using only the manufacturer's oil; plan hose replacement at six-year intervals under normal use (including at most two years' storage), per German accident-prevention rules (p. 19).
4. Store only in dry rooms, keep the battery charged (2 h charge; operating range 0 to +38 deg C), and never expose the system to heavy rain - it is only splash-proof (pp. 4, 19-20).
5. Diagnose hydraulic faults from the symptom table: e.g. a cylinder that will not advance points to an open release valve, loose coupler, low oil, pump fault, overload or leaking seals; advance in spurts indicates air in the system; failure to hold load indicates pump fault, leaking connection, wrong setup or leaking seals (p. 23).
6. Respect hydraulic safety limits: 700 bar maximum, never exceed component ratings, never handle pressurised hoses, keep clear of hydraulically supported loads (pp. 21-22).

## Decision rules

- Any visible damage means do not test - the manual treats this as a potentially fatal hazard.
- Repairs only by Anix or Anix-authorised staff; non-genuine parts void safe operation.

## Checks

- Calibration date on the device Info screen is within the last year.
- Force zero is checked (and reset unloaded if drifted, e.g. from temperature) before testing (p. 13).

## Common errors

- Zeroing the force sensor while it is still loaded.
- Using non-manufacturer oil or lubricants.
- Ignoring temperature limits or leaving the battery flat in storage.

## Caveats

- The calibration clause cites the 2001 edition of DIN 18134 even though the manual's procedures follow the 2012 edition.
- Hose-replacement interval derives from German statutory insurance rules; local WHS rules may differ.

## Standards cited

DIN 18134:2001-09
