# Assign a Geological Strength Index (GSI) to a rock mass in the field

> **Source:** [YouTube — Geological Strength Index (GSI): How to Use It in Practice](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=co1UptrxLf4&t=27s) (00:27-05:34). A YouTube lecture — learning
> material, not an approved design procedure. Equations and numbers were
> heard through auto-captions; read them off the video before use, and
> verify anything design-relevant against the standard and the project
> specification. Engineering conclusions need qualified review.

**Outcome.** A GSI value expressed as a range (e.g. 45 plus or minus 5) for an exposed rock mass, from a joint assessment of blockiness/structure and joint surface conditions on the GSI chart

## Inputs

- Visual access to the rock mass (outcrop, cut slope, tunnel wall) or good photographs
- GSI chart (Hoek and Marinos, 2000, named by the lecturer; variants exist)

## Procedure

1. Assess the structure axis of the chart: from intact/massive (very few joints, GSI approaching 100 in theory) through blocky, very blocky, to laminated/sheared/disintegrated
2. Trace visible discontinuities (mentally or on a photo) to judge blockiness honestly — the worked example's road-cut was judged 'very blocky'
3. Assess the surface-condition axis: fresh/very rough surfaces grade through good, fair, poor to very poor as weathering increases; the example was moderately weathered
4. Read GSI at the intersection of the structure row and condition column (about 45 in the example)
5. Report the value as a range, typically plus or minus 5 (e.g. 45-50), acknowledging assessor subjectivity

## Decision rules

- More weathering generally means both more joints (structure degrades) and worse surface condition — the assessment tends to move diagonally on the chart
- GSI below about 20 is very difficult to assess reliably; the chart also works poorly for laminated/sheared/disintegrated structures
- Because assessment is subjective and varies between engineers, never report a single-point GSI

## Checks

- Structure and surface-condition assessments were made independently before combining on the chart
- The reported range acknowledges the chart's resolution rather than implying precision

## Common errors

- Quoting a single precise GSI value instead of a range
- Applying the chart to sheared/disintegrated masses or GSI < 20 conditions where it works poorly
- Assessing only the most photogenic part of the exposure rather than the representative structure

## Caveats

- YouTube lecture — learning material, not an approved design procedure; verify against project specification and current standards
- GSI feeds the generalized Hoek-Brown criterion; use it with that framework rather than in isolation

## Standards cited

GSI chart of Hoek and Marinos (2000) (publication named by lecturer)
