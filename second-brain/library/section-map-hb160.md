# Section map — HB 160:2006

Standards Australia Handbook — Soils testing (reconfirmed 2016). The plain-
language companion to the AS 1289 test-method series: what each test is for,
how it behaves, and where it misleads. Reached for when a result needs
interpreting rather than repeating — why a CBR came out low, what a shrink-swell
index is actually measuring, when a nuclear gauge drifts — and each clause names
the AS 1289 method it explains. Local copy:
`G materials/HB160-2006_R2016 UNSECURED.pdf` — see [[Reference library]].

> **Clause numbers and headings only.** A finding aid so a clause can be cited
> and located without opening the whole document. The handbook is copyright and
> its text is not reproduced here or anywhere in this vault. Read the clause in
> the handbook before relying on it.

## Extraction note

**The local copy's text layer is enciphered.** Its embedded fonts use a fixed
character offset — every printable character is stored 28 code points below its
true value, so `pdftotext` returns strings such as `7XERHEVHW` for "Standards".
This map was produced by extracting the raw text layer and adding 28 to each
character code, line by line, leaving the un-enciphered runs (the reconfirmation
notice and copyright pages, which are in a normal font) untouched. Literal
spaces inserted by the extractor had to be excluded from the shift, or they
decode to `<`.

Consequence for the vault: HB 160 **can** be full-text searched, but only
through the same decode — a plain grep of the PDF will miss everything. Two
artefacts survive in the decoded text: em dashes come through as `{`, and round
brackets are dropped, so the clause names above have had those restored by eye
against the section headings in the body.

Headings are from the contents pages (iv–v) at two levels. Deeper sub-clauses,
tables and figures exist and are not listed. Appendix A is the one most often
wanted in isolation — it gives turnaround times per test, which is what a
programme argument usually turns on.
